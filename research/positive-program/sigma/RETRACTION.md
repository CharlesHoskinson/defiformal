# Retraction — four referees, four refutations

**Every substantive irreducibility claim made in this session is withdrawn. The
delegated allocation mandate appears to BE generated by the basis. What survives is
smaller, and is about the corpus rather than the algebra.**

---

## The four claims and their fate

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| **C1.** Every basis family is permission-free (`CHARACTERISATION.md` §2) | **FALSE.** `Post` is definitionally authority-parameterised — P4: "parameterised by an authority `n ∈ N` … defined iff `n` is the declared writer". My table omitted `Post`. |

## Why C4 fell, since it was the last one standing

**Acyclicity is descriptive, not definitional.** P4's signature makes `R_X` a
*parameter*. P4.3 reads "***When*** `R_X` is acyclic the sequence of writes is a
chain" — a hypothesis, vacuous if P4.2 had fixed it. And P4.4, *Idempotence*,
admits `(x,x) ∈ R_X`; a DAG has no self-loops.

**The corpus itself contains role cycles.** `L6/usdc.qnt:105-125` is
`blacklist`/`unblacklist` writing `restricted : str ⇀ bool` — a per-principal
permission map, set `true` at `:109` and `false` at `:120`. `pause`/`unpause` at
`:127-145` is a second. Both are finite protocol-written coordinates, i.e. `Φ`. So
`R_Φ` is not acyclic **in the extraction `BASIS.md` was derived from**.

**And a monotone encoding works anyway.** Take `grants, revokes : N ⇀ T`. Each
write is `post_T`, `R_T` is `<`, acyclic, P4.2-legal. The guard
`revokes(n) < grants(n)` is a B-term over `Cmp`. Every carrier stays acyclic while
the derived role cycles. The corpus already runs this shape — `markUsed` /
`canDeliverOnce`, `L4/common.qnt:105-109`.

**My own evidence didn't say what I said it did.** `COUNCIL.md` §3's table cited
`wit_lenderGranted` (grants **u2**) beside `wit_lenderRevoked` (revokes **u1**) —
different principals, an antichain, not a cycle. I built `wit_lenderRoleCycle` to
test exactly this and it reports `[violation]`, so the cycle *is* reachable — which
cuts **against** C4, because a reachable cycle is only an obstruction if it is
inexpressible, and it isn't.

## What actually survives

1. **`Post` carries an authority parameter.** The permission coordinate is *in* the
   basis. This is the one durable thing to come out of the characterisation work,
   and it is the opposite of what I claimed.
2. **The 57 specs delete it.** 0 of 17 gate-opening actions model a caller
   (`GATE-1.1B-PAIR4.md`); `DELETION-11.md` confirms four documented cases. So the
   **743/820 generation figure was measured against specs missing exactly what P4
   carries** — the measurement, not the algebra, is where the gap lives.
3. **`P4.2` is wrong as documentation.** Its enumeration of `R_X` claims `R_Φ` is
   acyclic; `usdc`'s `restricted` and `paused` falsify that inside the extraction.
   A real defect, and a small one.
4. **Convention 6h and the retrofit stand**, untouched by any of this. They are
   about spec fidelity to contracts, not about irreducibility.
5. **The Lean development stands as a true theorem** with a premise that does not
   hold for this basis. Correctly scoped in its own header; reusable if a
   permission-free sub-basis is ever isolated.

## Consequence for the generation theorem

**I have no counterexample.** Nothing found this session refutes generation for `P`.
The mandate — the corpus's most-confirmed gap, four independent witnesses — appears
to be `Post(Φ) ⋈ Cmp ⋈ Led.move`, i.e. generated.

The original 743/820 result therefore remains the only evidence of incompleteness,
and it was measured against a corpus that had deleted the authority parameter. That
figure needs re-running against re-specs that carry callers before it means
anything — which is 2.2, and which is where the roadmap already pointed.
