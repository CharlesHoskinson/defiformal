## What the pinned source actually does

All Vyper references below are to `source-entry/upstream/contracts/pools/3pool/StableSwap3Pool.vy`; `source.stdout` retains the complete numbered read.

At lines 195–218, `get_D` sums exactly three normalized balances, returns zero when their sum is zero, initializes D to S, and sets Ann to amp times 3. It performs at most 255 outer iterations. Each has three sequential integer divisions updating D_P in array order; replacing them with one rational product changes rounding. After computing the next D, it breaks when the absolute difference from the preceding D is at most one. Both increasing and decreasing/equal branches implement that guard.

The final `return D` is outside the loop. If arithmetic completes for all 255 iterations without the guard firing, the function successfully returns a residual D. There is no explicit nonconvergence assertion/revert. This is a source control-flow classification, not a reached-exhaustion witness or convergence theorem. An iteration count of 255 alone cannot distinguish a guard firing on the last iteration from exhaustion: retain a separate stop reason. Zero-sum return is a distinct successful control path, with zero iterations. A guard-fired result certifies adjacent-iterate tolerance only; it does not by itself prove an exact invariant root, a bound on real-valued residual error, or tolerance of a further iterate.

No get_D-level explicit refusal guard exists. Mixed zero/positive balances encounter the denominator `_x * 3` at line 208. On a nonzero-sum path amp=0 encounters unsigned `Ann - 1` at line 210. Arithmetic overflow, underflow and division-by-zero behavior must be bound to actual compiler/runtime semantics before being advertised as observed refusals. Source text and its comment acknowledge zero-division danger but are not compiled evidence. Do not import Lean Nat subtraction/division-by-zero totalization or Python unbounded integers as Vyper semantics.

## Arithmetic and caller obligations for the author

1. Freeze the three-element input representation and observation fields, including returned D, iteration count, zero-sum/guard-fired/exhausted stop reason, and the handling of arithmetic failure. If only normalized get_D is modeled, name normalization, storage, fees, token calls and ramp reachability as explicit boundary assumptions; do not imply full pool fidelity.
2. For the ordinary nonzero domain, require or prove that each xp is positive, all prefix sums fit uint256, amp times 3 fits and is at least one, and every executed arithmetic intermediate fits. At each inner step this includes `D_P * D` and `xp[i] * 3`. At each outer update it includes `Ann * S`, `D_P * 3`, their sum and multiplication by D, `(Ann - 1) * D`, `4 * D_P`, and their sum. The outer denominator must be strictly positive. Positivity of inputs alone is not an overflow proof. Preserve evaluation and floor-division order, including the guarded subtraction used in the tolerance test.
3. Normalization at lines 175–190 uses fixed rates `[10^18, 10^30, 10^30]` divided by `10^18`; thus mathematical xp is `[b0, 10^12*b1, 10^12*b2]`. Both normalization routines agree at these constants. The source multiplies before dividing, so replacing the expression by its reduced mathematical form requires intermediate overflow obligations. The product bound can be stronger than merely requiring the final xp to fit. Denomination and coin order are part of the contract.
4. Amplification is the actual uint256 amp used at line 204; do not silently substitute another Curve A convention or A_PRECISION scale. The constructor documentation describes `_A` as multiplied by n*(n-1), and directly stores it at lines 138–139 without a positivity or MAX_A check. `ramp_A` at 702–714 separately checks a positive future A below 10^6 and bounded relative change. Therefore the later ramp checks do not establish a universal constructor-domain restriction. Either model arbitrary admitted constructor values with failure behavior, or declare and justify a narrower initial-state domain. For `_A` interpolation at 156–163, bind ordered times, nonnegative elapsed time, positive `t1-t0`, and intermediate products; the other branch returns future_A directly.
5. Freeze the actual caller endpoint used in campaigns. Direct get_D users include virtual price (234), get_y (368), and one-coin withdrawal calculation (641); get_D_mem (223–224) serves quote, deposit and imbalance paths. A successful D does not guarantee caller success. Variable denominators outside get_D include token supply (238, 504, 642), D0 (265, 326, 344, 546, 557, 651–653), and, in get_y/get_y_D, each selected balance times 3, Ann times 3, Ann, and `2*y+b-D` (382–389, 610–617). A cycle-detection return can change the final D parity and iteration count relative to the source cap. Its declared sweeps at caps 8 and 96 are historical contextual analysis, not execution of this pin or source-faithful exhausted outputs. This review did not execute either historical model.
