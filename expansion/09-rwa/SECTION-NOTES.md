## Lane narrative

**What the section introduction should say.** The category asks who the obligor is, where the register of record is, who determines value, and who can force a transfer. It uses 24 elements and **none exclusively** — nothing in the vocabulary is specific to a claim on an off-chain obligation — and its shared core is an allowlist, a pause, a pro-rata claim and an upgradeable implementation. The vocabulary can express the wrapper and none of the wrapped. This is the largest gap by capital in the project.

**The finding: the register-of-record gap is confirmed at primary sources, by three different legal devices.** One prospectus states shares may not be recorded on the fund's books and will not be redeemed; one describes the tokens as digital twins with the registry moving to reflect them; one says the token is prima facie evidence of ownership as a share certificate would be. Three products, three constructions, one gap.

**Corrections.** The tranche element is refuted for one protocol (zero repository hits) and for another (the junior layer is sponsor equity). One product's proxy owner is an **externally-owned account**, which arms the prohibition on a mutable implementation under immediate single-key control — and that is a fact about the holder, which no element names. Two products the lane called identical are not; the cross-domain attribution between them is reversed.

**The honest limit.** Round one could reach **no offering document, security agreement or LP agreement for any of the five**. A second round is targeting exactly that, because the claim that the instrument underneath is undocumented is only worth stating if someone has looked properly.
