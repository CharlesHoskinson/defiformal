## 1. WHAT IT DOES

An investor subscribes USDC and receives USYC, which is not a note or a deposit but a **share in a Cayman Islands mutual fund**: "USYC is the onchain representation of **Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund Ltd.** (SDYF)" (`introduction.md`), and "Each USYC token serves as a digital representation of a share of the Hashnote International Short Duration Fund Ltd. (the 'Fund'), a Cayman Islands registered mutual fund" (Circle press release, 2025-07-24). **The party that owes the money is the Fund — SDYF, a Cayman Islands exempted mutual fund licensed by CIMA — not Circle**; Circle's Bermuda entity is the Fund's agent: "The Fund has appointed Circle International Bermuda Limited ('CIBL'), a Bermuda Monetary Authority licensed digital asset business, as its **token administrator**, responsible for the management of USYC on behalf of the Fund" (same disclaimer). Circle's own 10-K is blunt that it stands behind nothing: SDYF is an unconsolidated variable interest entity and "**The Company provides no guarantees and has no other financial obligations to each of the funds.**"

NAV is struck **once per business day, off-chain, by the prime broker, and then published on-chain**: "USYC price is reported to the USYC Oracle, a Chainlink-style smart contract, once per business day after the Prime Brokerage has reported the prior day's activity", "typically... at 9 am ET (subject to delays due to operational or technical reasons)", and "USYC price is calculated based on the Net Asset Value (NAV) of the Prime Brokerage account divided by the total supply of USYC on issue" (`token-price.md`). USYC is **price-accruing, not share-count-accruing** — verified on-chain: the oracle's latest answer at round 483 is 1.133066960425761961 (18 dp) while token balances are static. Notably, **subscriptions after 2:00 pm ET are priced at a forecast, not an observed NAV**: "the effective subscription price is a forecast of the next price report" (`token-price.md`).

Redemption runs through the **Smart Contract Teller**, which burns USYC and pays USDC "24/7/365" at "the latest reported price". Only allowlisted wallets may redeem, the minimum investment is US$100,000, and the product is closed to U.S. Persons under Regulation S. The instant path is explicitly **capacity-bounded and discretionary**: "You can view the liquidity available on the USYC page in real time. If you require more liquidity on a specific day, **please contact us** at customer-support@circle.com" (`product-structuring.md`). **No numeric cap is published in the documentation, and the fallback is a manual, relationship-mediated request — not a documented T+1 queue.** I found no published gating, side-pocket, or wind-down provision; those live in the Fund's offering documents, which are not public (see §5(e)).

## 2. DESIGN

Every item is tagged **[ON-CHAIN]** (a deployed mechanism I verified) or **[LEGAL]** (an instrument or off-chain arrangement).

### Token contract and transfer restrictions — [ON-CHAIN]
Ethereum mainnet `0x136471a34f6ef19fE571EFFC1CA711fdb8E49f2b`, verified live: `name()` = **"US Yield Coin"**, `symbol()` = "USYC", `decimals()` = 6. It is a **permissioned ERC-20**: "The investor's ownership of this fund is reflected on a blockchain via a **permissioned ERC-20 smart contract**" (`product-structuring.md`). The gate is wired on-chain — I called `authority()` on the token and it returns `0x902D906b8d988092213bE799B18Bd2cbd64F808C`, which is exactly the Entitlements contract address listed in the docs.

### The Smart Contract Teller — [ON-CHAIN], their own term
USYC's own vocabulary. Verbatim: "To help investors enter and exit their positions efficiently, we provide a **Smart Contract Teller** to facilitate investing in the fund and liquidating the asset onchain." Subscription: "Through the Teller, investors can subscribe to USYC with USDC 24/7/365. Each stablecoin has an after-hours threshold, and any after-hours subscription will be subscribed at the next business day's price. This price is calculated in advance based on the current interest rate generated by the fund." Redemption: "The Smart Contract Teller provides USYC holders immediate liquidity for USYC. At any time of the day, investors can redeem USYC at the latest reported price through the Teller." Cut-off: "For subscription funds arriving after 2 PM EST, USYC shares are minted on the next business day."

Its ABI is minimal (Circle's own quickstart): `deposit(uint256 _assets, address _receiver)` and `redeem(uint256 _shares, address _receiver, address _account)`. Verified on the mainnet Teller `0xeE35F963BFC71b51eC95147f26c030D674ea30e6`: `asset()` = `0xA0b8...eB48` (USDC) and `oracle()` = `0x74f2199AEb743f68f05943e5715A33EaF2b61f53`, i.e. the Teller prices strictly off the same oracle the docs describe.

**Material finding: the Teller is not a funded escrow.** I queried its USDC balance and it is **0.0 USDC**; the Cross Chain Teller holds 0.000499 USDC; the BSC Cross Chain Teller holds 0.0 USDC. The "immediate liquidity" is therefore supplied at transaction time from an operationally-controlled treasury, not from an on-chain reserve a holder can inspect or claim. This is the mechanical basis for calling instant redemption discretionary rather than guaranteed.

### Allowlist / KYC regime — [ON-CHAIN] enforcement over a [LEGAL] determination
"**Entitlements are the gatekeepers to most of the functionality of USYC.** The wallets associated with KYC'ed entities receive a Role that entitles them to hold and transfer the token. The USYC Smart Contracts queries the Entitlements contract to verify the caller of a transaction has the appropriate permissions to perform a transfer or to receive the token." The determination itself is off-chain and human: "Fund administrator performs KYC/AML checks based on documents provided" then "Wallet screening provider whitelists wallet address provided" (`investor-onboarding.md`).

### Sanctions screening — [ON-CHAIN], continuous, not a static bit
"In addition to checking that a wallet is permissioned, the Entitlements contract also performs **OFAC sanctions checks by querying an onchain oracle** for matches to sanctioned addresses. The oracle is maintained by **Chainalysis**." This runs per transfer, at transfer time.

### Freeze / pause / burn powers — [ON-CHAIN], holder identified
"The Entitlements contract can also **freeze all transactions involving USYC**. As every type of action involving USYC verifies the Role of the sender, the Allowlist is positioned to revert all transactions. In the event there is suspicious activity, Hashnote has the ability to prevent any transaction from being completed."

I confirmed the powers from deployed bytecode by extracting PUSH4 dispatch selectors from each implementation:
- **USYC token impl** `0xbf0f2f3aad6b99893d80c550fbacec915545eb92` exposes `mint(address,uint256)`, `burn(uint256)`, **`burn(address,uint256)`** (0x9dc29fac — an administrative burn of another party's balance), `owner()`, `authority()`, `transferOwnership(address)`, `upgradeTo(address)`, `upgradeToAndCall(address,bytes)`.
- **Entitlements impl** `0xb59b15683710822c40df789a8e67ecfe30460c76` exposes **`pause()`, `unpause()`, `paused()`**, plus `owner()`, `transferOwnership`, `upgradeTo`. Live `paused()` currently returns `false`.
- **Teller impl** `0xb69ecb156dc0028198028c501340d5367845ca72` exposes `mint(address,uint256)`, `burn(address,uint256)`, `authority()`, `upgradeTo`.

**A targeted search for `forceTransfer`, `seize`, `clawback`, `destroyBlackFunds`, `freeze(address)`, `blacklist(address)` found none of them.** So the accurate statement is: the control plane can **freeze globally and burn a holder's balance**, but I found **no forced-transfer / clawback-to-a-named-recipient function**. (Caveat: selector extraction proves presence strongly and absence only moderately.)

### NAV / price feed — [ON-CHAIN] publication of an [LEGAL]/off-chain determination
Two distinct "USYC / USD" oracles exist and the docs are inconsistent about which is canonical:
- `0x74f2199AEb743f68f05943e5715A33EaF2b61f53` — listed on `smart-contracts.md`; `description()` = "USYC / USD", `decimals()` = **18**, `latestRound()` = **483**, `latestRoundData()` answer = **1.133066960425761961**, `updatedAt` = 1785846203 (2026-08-04). **This is the operative one — the Teller's `oracle()` points at it**, and its answer matches the public API to the last digit.
- `0x4c48bcb2160F8e0aDbf9D4F3B034f1e36d1f8b3e` — the address linked from `token-price.md`; `description()` = "USYC / USD", `decimals()` = **8**, round **773**, answer **1.13281096**. A separate feed on a different round schedule and a slightly different value.

"The USYC Oracle adheres to the Chainlink aggregator interface." Live payload from `https://usyc.hashnote.com/api/price-reports` (round 483): principal 3,007,906,817.13; interest 291,303.55; balance 3,008,198,120.68; totalSupply 2,654,890,748.199602; fee 25,709.296994.

### Chains and how USYC gets there — [ON-CHAIN]
Mainnet deployments per `smart-contracts.md`: **Ethereum** (token, Teller, Cross Chain Teller, Oracle, Entitlements), **BSC/BNB Chain** (token, Cross Chain Teller, Entitlements), **Solana** (SPL22 token, Teller-and-Permissions). ## 3. REPO

**There is no public source repository for USYC. Flatly: none exists that I could find.**

What I tried: GitHub repository search for `usyc in:name,description` returned 54 repositories, all unrelated (a high-school Java project, a Spanish-language digital library, and a cluster of random-suffix junk repos). A scoped search `org:circlefin usyc` returned **0**. The `hashnote` GitHub org lookup was rate-limited before resolving, but no Hashnote-published contract repo surfaced in any search. Circle's own docs never link source; they say only "Interacting with USYC onchain is seamless through an **audited smart contract**" and "The token contract is audited and verified on Etherscan" — pointing at Etherscan, not at a repo. **I also did not locate any published audit PDF**; the docs assert an audit exists but link no report.

Because there is no repo, "do deployed contracts match the repo" is not answerable and there is no license, language declaration, or directory layout to record. Here is what IS verifiable on-chain instead, all confirmed by direct RPC on 2026-08-04:

**Ethereum mainnet addresses (from `smart-contracts.md`, each independently confirmed to hold code):**
- **USYC ERC-20 token** — `0x136471a34f6ef19fE571EFFC1CA711fdb8E49f2b`. Proxy, 833 bytes. `name()` = "US Yield Coin", `symbol()` = "USYC", `decimals()` = 6, `totalSupply()` = 77,375,020.092057. `owner()` = `0x13ff8cAbb86Edf94a2Df4F98773BdA4005182DD6`. `authority()` = `0x902D906b8d988092213bE799B18Bd2cbd64F808C` (the Entitlements contract — the permission gate is provably wired).

Implementation addresses are listed in the §2 control-plane table. The EIP-1967 *admin* slot was empty on all of them while the *implementation* slot was populated, consistent with UUPS (upgrade authority held by the implementation's `owner()`) rather than a TransparentUpgradeableProxy with a separate ProxyAdmin.

**Etherscan verification, quoted:** WebFetch on Etherscan returns HTTP 403 from this host, so I retrieved the token page with a stealth browser fetch (`StealthyFetcher`, status 200). The page title reads verbatim: **"US Yield Coin (USYC) | ERC-20 | Address: 0x136471a3...db8E49f2b | Etherscan"**. I did not obtain the verified-source tab contents, so **UNKNOWN — I cannot quote Etherscan's compiler/verification metadata or the Solidity source itself; my function-level findings come from deployed bytecode, not from verified source.**

**What events reveal:** I ran `eth_getLogs` for USDC `Transfer` into the mainnet Teller across the most recent 4,000 blocks and found **0 events** — consistent with the mainnet Teller being near-dormant while activity concentrates on BNB Chain. ## 5. RESIDUE CHECK

### (a) Teller → prime broker → T-bills + reverse repo; prime broker identity; reserve composition; repo counterparty exposure — **CONFIRMED, with one part UNKNOWN**

The mechanism is confirmed verbatim: "The investor sends stablecoin USDC via our Smart Contract Teller. **Hashnote then transfers the investor's funds to its Prime Brokerage to invest into T-Bills and enter repo/reverse-repo agreements.**" (A2)

**Prime broker identity: CONFIRMED — Marex**, stated as "Prime Broker | Marex" in the official service-providers table (A4). This is a primary source, not an aggregator.

**Reserve composition: CONFIRMED but only qualitatively.** Circle's own docs say the fund "invests **primarily in reverse repurchase agreements (reverse repo) backed by U.S. government securities**" (B1) and the USYC docs say assets are "primarily deployed in reverse repo, ensuring maximum liquidity and minimal duration risk" (A1). There is a caveated risk note: "*Fixed income assets such as Treasury Bills may lose value."

**Repo counterparty exposure: UNKNOWN.** I searched the documentation set, Circle's developer docs, and the FY2025 10-K. **No repo counterparty is named anywhere, no counterparty concentration limit is published, and no holdings breakdown exists in any format I could retrieve.** Note the contradiction worth flagging for the paper: Circle's developer docs claim "The token price **and fund holdings** are published through an onchain Oracle" (B1), but the oracle's `latestRoundData()` returns only a price, and the richest public payload (`/api/price-reports`, E2) contains only principal, interest, balance, price, totalSupply and fee — **no holdings composition at all.** The claim that holdings are published on-chain is not supported by what the oracle actually returns.

**On `At`:** the corpus gloss "reserve/NAV attestation" needs splitting. There **is** a daily NAV publication (the oracle round). There is **no published reserve attestation report** — I found none, and the only assurance provider named is Cohen and Company as annual **auditor** (A4), whose report I could not locate. So `At` is supported as *NAV attestation* and **unsupported as *reserve* attestation**. The distinction matters: USYC gives you a daily price signed by the issuer, not an independent reserve report.

### (b) REGISTER OF RECORD — **CONFIRMED, and the direction of the sentence is the finding**

This is the most important question and the documents answer it directly. Three quotes, all from A2:

> "Ownership in SDYF is represented as **shares at the Transfer Agent, and whose digital twins are represented onchain** as a Smart Contract token (ERC-20 specification)."

> "**The fund administrator moves shares in the SDYF share registry to reflect movements in the USYC token.**"

> "**The client's redemption of the USYC token will be reflected in the fund registry by our fund administrator.**"

**The on-chain token is explicitly downstream of an off-chain share register. It is not the register.** The word "digital twin" is theirs, and a twin is by construction a copy of something that exists elsewhere. The Transfer Agent holds the shares; the fund administrator (NAV Consulting, A4) maintains the registry.

But note the **direction of causation**, which is the subtle and genuinely interesting part: the registry "**moves… to reflect** movements in the USYC token", and redemption "**will be reflected in** the fund registry". Operationally the token leads and the register follows — the chain is the point of first execution. Legally the register is still the record of who owns shares in a Cayman mutual fund. So USYC sits in an unusual position: **on-chain-primary in ordering, off-chain-primary in legal effect.** A formalisation that treats the token as the ledger of record will misstate what a holder owns; a formalisation that treats it as a mere mirror will misstate where transactions actually sequence. Both readings are wrong alone.

### (c) The obligor — **CONFIRMED as SDYF (Cayman); entity NOT renamed; creditor ranking effectively moot at FY2025**

**Obligor: Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund Ltd. ("SDYF"), a Cayman Islands mutual fund licensed by CIMA.** The press-release disclaimer is explicit that Circle's role is agency, not obligation: "The Fund has appointed Circle International Bermuda Limited ('CIBL')… as its **token administrator**, responsible for the management of USYC **on behalf of the Fund**." (D1)

**Has the issuer entity changed since the acquisition? No — the Fund still carries the Hashnote name.** Circle acquired the *manager*, not the fund: "we acquired 100% of the ownership interest in **Hashnote Holdings LLC**, a Delaware limited liability company…, which, through its affiliates, is the **fund manager** of Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund Ltd." (C1). The fund itself was not acquired and was not renamed. What *did* change is the issuance venue: "Since February 2025, we have issued USYC to eligible non-U.S. customers **out of Bermuda** under our existing DABA License" (C1), and USYC was **relaunched in Q3 2025** (C1).

**Flag an unresolved inconsistency across primary sources on who "issues" USYC:**
- USYC docs + Circle dev docs + 10-K regulatory section: "**Circle International Bermuda Limited issues USYC**" (A1, B1, C1).
- 10-K Note 3 + press-release disclaimer: SDYF is "a tokenized money market fund and **the issuer of USYC**" (C1); CIBL is merely "**token administrator**" (D1).

These are not reconcilable on their face. The most defensible reading, and the one the paper should use, is that **the economic obligor is the Fund** (that is where the assets and the shares are, and Circle disclaims all guarantees) while **CIBL is the regulated issuing/administering agent** in Bermuda. I flag it rather than resolve it, because the offering documents that would settle it are not public.

**Do creditors rank ahead of tokenholders?** Structurally yes — shareholders in any fund rank behind creditors. But the 10-K reports SDYF's balance sheet directly: **"Total assets $ 1,537,818 [thousand] / Total liabilities $ —"** at 2025-12-31 (C1). **At the last audited date the Fund had zero liabilities**, so there was nothing ranking ahead of tokenholders. This is a much stronger disclosure than most tokenized funds provide and it is a genuinely favourable finding. Caveat honestly: it is a **point-in-time** figure at 2025-12-31, when the fund was $1.54B; it is now ~$3.0B (E3), and I have no updated liability figure. Repo and reverse-repo activity can create liabilities intra-period.

### (d) Instant-redemption capacity — **`Rd` as an unconditional right is REFUTED for USYC**

**The cap is not published as a number.** The documentation says only: "You can view the liquidity available on the USYC page in real time. **If you require more liquidity on a specific day, please contact us** at customer-support@circle.com." (A2) I attempted to retrieve the figure programmatically and failed — `https://usyc.hashnote.com/api/liquidity` and `/api/teller` both return **404**; only `/api/price`, `/api/price-reports` and `/api/stats` exist, and none carries a liquidity or capacity field. **UNKNOWN — the current numeric instant-redemption cap. It is displayed only in the browser widget on the USYC page, which I did not render.**

**Is it discretionary and revocable? Yes, on three independent pieces of evidence:**
1. **The Teller holds no USDC.** Verified on-chain: mainnet Teller USDC balance **0.0**; BSC Cross Chain Teller **0.0**. There is no escrowed pool backing the promise. Liquidity must be pushed in at transaction time by an operator.
2. **Overflow is handled by human contact**, not by an on-chain queue or a documented T+1 settlement path. "Please contact us."
3. **Enhanced liquidity is a paid, negotiated tier**: "'Private Liquidity Teller' (PLT) is available for **special use cases**. PLTs allow the client to have up to 100% instant liquidity into USDC at all times… **Additional fees apply for this service.**" (A7) If 100% instant liquidity must be purchased separately, then the default Teller manifestly does not provide it.

So the answer to the vocabulary question is: **`Rd` asserts an unconditional redemption right; USYC's on-chain instant redemption is conditional, capacity-bounded, unfunded on-chain, and priced as a service tier.** Whether the *underlying share* carries an unconditional redemption right against the Fund is a separate matter governed by the offering documents — see (e) — and is **UNKNOWN**. Also note the fallback is **not** documented as T+1: I found no statement anywhere that exhausted Teller liquidity converts into a T+1 settlement. That characterisation should not be asserted in the paper.

### (e) The offering memorandum / subscription agreement — **NOT OBTAINABLE**

**UNKNOWN — I could not obtain the offering memorandum, private placement memorandum, or subscription agreement.** What I tried: the full USYC documentation tree (all 9 pages in `llms.txt`), Circle's developer docs, Circle's pressroom, and EDGAR full-text search (the Fund is not an EDGAR filer; USYC is offered under a Securities Act exemption so no registration statement exists — C1: "offered in reliance on an exemption from the registration requirements of the Securities Act").

The press release confirms these documents exist and confirms they are the instrument that actually creates the claim, while also confirming they are private: "An offer to sell or a solicitation of an offer to buy Fund interests and USYC is **only made to eligible investors through the Fund's offering documents** which contain important information about certain risks, fees and expenses, and other material information related to an investment in the Fund." (D1)

Consequently: **redemption gating provisions, suspension rights, side letters, and wind-down mechanics are all UNKNOWN.** The onboarding flow is gated behind a portal account requiring KYC (A5), so the documents are not reachable without becoming an investor. **The paper should not assert anything about USYC's gating or side-letter regime.** Note that the PLT (A7) is functionally a side-letter-like arrangement disclosed in public documentation — that is the only glimpse of bilateral variation available.

### (f) Qualified custody and segregation from Circle's balance sheet — **SEGREGATION CONFIRMED; "QUALIFIED CUSTODY" REFUTED as a term**

**Segregation from Circle's balance sheet: CONFIRMED, and unusually well-evidenced.** The 10-K states SDYF is a variable interest entity "**not consolidated** by the Company due to the fact that we are not the primary beneficiary as we do not have an obligation to absorb losses or a right to receive benefits that could potentially be significant to each fund", that Circle's "maximum exposure to loss… is limited to its insignificant investment and its obligations to perform services as the manager", and decisively: "**The Company provides no guarantees and has no other financial obligations to each of the funds.**" (C1) The fund's assets are not Circle's assets and Circle's creditors have no claim on them — and symmetrically, **holders have no recourse to Circle.**

**Segregation at the asset level: asserted, not independently verified.** "Assets are protected through **segregated custodial accounts at our prime broker**, guaranteeing direct access for shareholders and a high level of asset security." (A1) This is the issuer's own assertion; I found no attestation, audit report, or third-party confirmation of it.

**"Qualified custody": REFUTED as a description.** The phrase "qualified custodian" appears nowhere in any USYC or Circle source I fetched. There is **no separately named custodian at all** — custody is described as sitting with the prime broker (Marex), which is a broker, not a qualified custodian in the Advisers Act sense. Fireblocks appears only as "MPC Wallet" (A4), i.e. key management for the token operations, not fund-asset custody. **The paper should not describe USYC as employing qualified custody.**

### (g) Sanctions/AML as ongoing process vs `Aw` as a static allowlist bit — **`Aw` as a static bit is INSUFFICIENT**

**Confirmed: screening is continuous and enforced per transfer, on-chain.** "In addition to checking that a wallet is permissioned, the Entitlements contract also performs **OFAC sanctions checks by querying an onchain oracle** for matches to sanctioned addresses. The oracle is maintained by Chainalysis." (A2) Because "The USYC Smart Contracts queries the Entitlements contract to verify the caller of a transaction has the appropriate permissions" on every transfer, the sanctions check is **evaluated at each transaction against a live external list**, not stamped once at onboarding.

So USYC's permission gate is a **conjunction of two things with different temporal character**: (i) a static-ish allowlist bit set at onboarding by the fund administrator after KYC/AML (A5), and (ii) a dynamic negative check against a third-party oracle that can turn a previously-valid wallet invalid without any action by Circle, Hashnote, or the holder. `Aw` captures (i). It does not capture (ii), which is really an **external oracle acting as a revocation channel** — and which is also, structurally, a live dependency on Chainalysis's data availability and correctness.

### Anything else USYC does that the vocabulary has no name for

1. **Collateral mobility at derivatives venues — the dominant actual use, and entirely unnamed.** Circle's 10-K says USYC "is intended **primarily for use as collateral in digital asset markets**" (C1) — this is the *primary* purpose, not a side use. Concretely: Binance institutional clients "use it as off-exchange collateral for derivatives trades", and "USYC will be kept with supporting banks through **Binance Banking Triparty**, or with **Ceffu**, Binance's institutional custody partner" (D1). The token sits immobilised with a third-party custodian, pledged as margin, while accruing NAV, and the exchange extends trading capacity against it. **The vocabulary has no symbol for pledged-but-yielding collateral held by a tri-party custodian.** This also explains the BNB Chain supply concentration measured in §2 — 97% of USYC exists on the chain of the exchange that accepts it as margin. The token's distribution is a function of a single venue's collateral policy, which is itself a concentration risk with no name in the vocabulary.

2. **Forward-priced subscription against a forecast NAV.** "After 2:00 pm ET until the next price report, the effective subscription price is a **forecast of the next price report**" and this forecast "is calculated in advance based on the current interest rate generated by the fund" (A3, A2). Subscribers after the cut-off transact at a **predicted** price. This is neither `Ex` (an observed external datum) nor `Ix` (mechanical index accrual) — it is an issuer-computed projection, and the issuer bears or captures the difference. Unnamed.

3. **Asymmetric pricing between the two legs.** "Redemptions always occur at the current USYC price, and Subscriptions occur at the Effective Subscription Price… meaning redemptions occur at a different price than subscriptions" (A3). A two-price mechanism with a time-varying spread. Unnamed.

4. **The Private Liquidity Teller as a purchasable liquidity SLA.** A per-client, fee-bearing contractual guarantee of instant convertibility (A7). This is a *tiered* redemption right — some holders have a strictly better claim on liquidity than others, for money. The vocabulary treats redemption as a uniform property of an instrument; USYC's is stratified by contract.

5. **Dual-ledger twinning itself** (see (b)) — the register/token split has no symbol, and neither does the fund administrator's role as the reconciling party.

6. **The issuer-agency split** — a Cayman fund as obligor with a Bermuda-regulated *token administrator* as the operating and regulated face. The entity a holder deals with is not the entity that owes them.

## 6. NOTES FOR THE DELTA SECTION

*(Reporting only. No corpus file was read for editing and none was modified.)*

### The prior record is internally inconsistent before any evidence is considered
The corpus record decomposes USYC as `At, Aw, Ex, Fz, Gp, Rd, Sh, Up` (8 symbols) and BUIDL as `At, Aw, Fz, Gp, Ps, Rd, Sh, Up, Xf, Xm` (10 symbols), yet the lane recorded the two as an **identical** decomposition. **They are not identical as written** — USYC's array uniquely contains `Ex`, BUIDL's uniquely contains `Ps`, `Xf`, `Xm`. The two sets differ by four symbols. The "identical" annotation is a bookkeeping error independent of anything I found; it cannot be repaired by adjusting USYC's array, because the arrays as recorded already disagree.

### What the DESIGN actually supports, symbol by symbol

**`Ex` (external data oracle) — CONFIRMED, and USYC has two of them.**
(i) The price oracle: `description()` = "USYC / USD", `decimals()` = 18, round 483, answer 1.133066960425761961, verified live at `0x74f2199AEb743f68f05943e5715A33EaF2b61f53`, and provably the one the Teller uses (`Teller.oracle()` returns exactly that address). "The USYC Oracle adheres to the Chainlink aggregator interface" (A3). (ii) The Chainalysis sanctions oracle queried on-chain by Entitlements on every transfer (A2). `Ex` is not merely present, it is load-bearing twice over — pricing *and* permissioning both depend on external feeds. **Keep `Ex`.**

**`Ps` (1:1 reserve-backed peg-swap module) — REFUTED.**
USYC has no peg. Its price is **1.133066960425761961 and monotonically rising** (E1/E4) because it is a NAV-accruing fund share. The Teller is a NAV-priced mint/redeem against a daily oracle round, with a 4 bps subscription fee and a 3 bps redemption fee (A8) and a deliberately *asymmetric* two-price mechanism (A3) — the structural opposite of a 1:1 par swap. **This is the clearest single difference from BUIDL**, whose `Ps` corresponds to a genuine 1:1 par conversion facility. **USYC should not carry `Ps`.** Anything in the corpus that equates the two products' redemption modules is wrong on the arithmetic alone.

**`Xf` (cross-domain asset transfer) — SUPPORTED; USYC should probably carry it, and its absence is the more surprising gap.**
USYC is live on **Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana** with a dedicated **Cross Chain Teller** on both Ethereum (`0x5dbeCcECEbCdC2ce3258f6E638373d2923560c7d`) and BSC (`0xf38979E05650be7926EA07BB59C48Fb9b1DB3D08`), documented as: "For Binance (BSC), you can use the Cross Chain Teller to subscribe and redeem native USYC from Ethereum" (A6). And the multichain fact is not marginal — I measured it: **BNB Chain holds 2,573,937,729.19 USYC against Ethereum's 77,375,020.09, i.e. ~97% of supply.** A decomposition that omits cross-domain machinery from USYC while including it for BUIDL is describing USYC's minority deployment as if it were the whole product.

One qualification, stated honestly: issuance is **native per chain** ("USYC will be natively issued on BNB Chain", D1), so this is not a lock-and-mint bridge, and `Xf` should be read as *multi-domain native issuance with a cross-domain subscription/redemption path*, not as bridging.

**`Xm` (verified messaging) — UNKNOWN.** I could not determine the messaging layer. Every ABI probe against the Cross Chain Teller (`owner()`, `token()`, `messenger()`, `tokenMessenger()`, `endpoint()`, `lzEndpoint()`) reverted or returned empty against 7,920 bytes of unverified bytecode, and no document names a protocol. **Do not assert `Xm` for USYC in either direction.**

**`Up` (mutable proxy) — CONFIRMED on-chain, strongly.** EIP-1967 implementation slots are populated on the token, Teller, Entitlements and Oracle, and `upgradeTo(address)` + `upgradeToAndCall(address,bytes)` selectors are present in all three implementations I disassembled. Implementation and owner addresses are tabulated in §2. **Keep `Up`.**

**`Gp` (guardian/pause) — CONFIRMED on-chain.** `pause()` / `unpause()` / `paused()` selectors present in the Entitlements implementation; live `paused()` = false. Documented as "System Halt": "The Entitlements contract can also **freeze all transactions involving USYC**" (A2). **Keep `Gp`.**

**`Fz` (freeze / forced transfer) — CONFIRMED for freeze and for forced *burn*; REFUTED for forced *transfer*.** The token implementation exposes `mint(address,uint256)` and **`burn(address,uint256)`** — an administrative burn of another party's balance. A targeted selector search for `forceTransfer`, `seize`, `clawback`, `destroyBlackFunds`, `freeze(address)` and `blacklist(address)` returned **none**. So the issuer can freeze everything and can destroy a holder's tokens, but there is no evidence of a power to reassign them to a named recipient. If `Fz` is glossed as "freeze/forced transfer", the gloss overstates USYC by one power and understates it by another. (Caveat: bytecode selector extraction is strong evidence of presence, weaker evidence of absence.)

**`Aw` — retained but insufficient.** See §5(g): USYC's gate is allowlist ∧ live-oracle-sanctions-check, and the second conjunct is dynamic and externally controlled.

**`At` — retained but equivocal.** See §5(a): daily NAV publication yes; reserve attestation report, none found.

**`Sh` (pro-rata share accounting) — CONFIRMED.** One token corresponds to one SDYF share: "Hashnote mints into the investor's wallet an amount of USYC tokens **equivalent to the number of shares** the investor has in the SDYF" (A2), and price = NAV ÷ total supply (A3). Cross-chain token supply (≈2,651.3M) reconciles to the oracle's share count (2,654.9M) within 0.13%, so the share/token correspondence holds empirically across the whole product.

**`Rd` — present in name, conditional in substance.** See §5(d).

### Symbols the corpus omitted that the DESIGN arguably supports
- **`Ix` (index accrual):** USYC is price-accruing — value accrues in the token price, not in balances (verified: oracle answer rises round over round; supply is independent). If `Ix` denotes accrual-by-index rather than by rebase, USYC is a clean instance and the corpus omits it.
- **`Sv` (servicing & determination discretion):** strongly present and omitted. The issuer determines the price, may delay it ("subject to delays due to operational or technical reasons", A3), *forecasts* the subscription price after 2 pm ET, and decides discretionarily how much instant liquidity exists on any given day. That is determination discretion in the ordinary sense.
- **`Wq` (withdrawal queue):** correctly absent — there is no queue. But the corpus should not read that absence as evidence of unconditional liquidity; the queue is replaced by an email address (§5(d)).

### Rank basis at 2026-08-04
**The magnitude holds at a primary source. The rank does not.**
- **Confirmed primary:** `https://usyc.hashnote.com/api/stats` returns `total_aum` = **3,005,480,354.608642** with `last_updated` 2026-08-05T02:50:01.085Z; the independent `/api/price-reports` round-483 payload gives `balance` = **3,008,198,120.68**. Both corroborate **$3.01B**. This is the issuer's own endpoint, not an aggregator.
- **`#1` ranking: AGGREGATOR ONLY.** `https://app.rwa.xyz/treasuries` shows USYC first at ~$3.0B, ahead of BUIDL at $2.67B **[AGGREGATOR]**. No primary source I fetched asserts a rank — Circle's 10-K makes no "largest" claim about USYC. **The rank must be cited to rwa.xyz and labelled; the $3.01B figure may be cited to Circle's own API.**

### Bottom line for the delta
USYC and BUIDL are **not** the same instrument and should not carry the same array. On the evidence: USYC gains `Ex` (twice over) and almost certainly `Xf`; it loses `Ps` outright; `Xm` is undetermined; `Fz` needs re-glossing to *freeze + forced burn*; `Rd` needs a conditionality qualifier; and `Ix` and `Sv` look like genuine omissions. The deepest structural fact — that the token is a **digital twin of a transfer-agent share register**, operationally leading but legally following — has no symbol at all, and it is the fact that determines what a USYC holder actually owns.
