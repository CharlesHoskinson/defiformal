# Stage 1 research — RWA / tokenised treasuries & private credit

**Category:** `09-rwa` · **Lane:** Stage-1 research · **All access dates: 2026-08-04** unless a payload timestamp says otherwise.

**Note on source quality for this category.** This is the worst-sourced category in the project and the reason is structural, not accidental. Three of the five applications are funds wearing a token, so the instrument that actually creates the holder's claim — the offering memorandum, the subscription agreement, the security agreement — is private by construction, disclosed only to investors who have already cleared KYC. What is public splits into four tiers of very different quality. **Tier 1, genuinely primary and machine-checkable:** the chain itself (I read every contract claim below by direct JSON-RPC `eth_call` / `eth_getCode` / `eth_getStorageAt` against public Ethereum nodes rather than trusting an explorer), the protocols' own GraphQL APIs (`api.maple.finance/v2/graphql`, `api.centrifuge.io`, `usyc.hashnote.com/api/*`), the source repositories at a named tag, and SEC EDGAR filings, which are sworn. **Tier 2, primary but self-serving:** docs sites and product pages. Maple and Centrifuge both publish clean Markdown at `.md` URLs with an `llms.txt` index, which made verbatim quotation cheap and reliable; Securitize's product page is a JS shell and required a stealth browser fetch. **Tier 3, party statements in press form:** press releases issued by the parties themselves, used only for facts the parties are asserting about their own product. **Tier 4, aggregators:** used for ranking only, always labelled `[AGGREGATOR]`. Etherscan and `sec.gov/cgi-bin` return HTTP 403 to this host; EDGAR's JSON and Archives endpoints work with a declared User-Agent, and that is where the strongest evidence in this file comes from. Where a fact was not obtainable I have written `UNKNOWN` and said what I tried; there are roughly two dozen such marks below and they are load-bearing, because in this category **the unobtainable facts are systematically the ones that determine what a holder owns** — not one offering memorandum, private placement memorandum, security agreement, control agreement, master loan agreement or LP agreement across all five applications was publicly reachable, and every one of them is KYC-gated by design. The correct reading of that is not that the research is incomplete but that the category's decisive instruments are structurally private, which is itself the finding.

## Ondo Finance (USDY + OUSG + Ondo Global Markets)

> **Structural warning, read before decomposing.** Ondo restructured USDY on **2025-12-15**. Its own live documentation is now **internally inconsistent about who the USDY obligor is**: one page says Ondo USDY LLC (Delaware), another says Ondo Global Markets (BVI) Limited, and both are live and undated. Any decomposition that names a single USDY obligor asserts more than Ondo's published record supports.

### 1. WHAT IT DOES

**USDY.** A holder sends USDC (or a USD wire for ≥$100,000) and receives USDY, an ERC-20 whose redemption price rises daily; the sibling rUSDY holds price at $1.00 and rebases the balance instead. The claim is a **note, not a fund share**: the issuing SPV's "activities are limited to (1) borrowing funds from prospective lenders, (2) issuing USDY tokens **to evidence the LLC's debt obligations to those lenders**" — Ondo calls holders *lenders*. **Who owes the money is contested in Ondo's own live docs** (see the warning above, and §5(a)). Price is not a NAV: an `RWADynamicOracle` is "configured with a series of *ranges* — each range has a start time, end time, and a daily interest rate that compounds across the range", so the price accrues deterministically on-chain between administrative rate updates; live `getPrice()` = **1.14179424** on 2026-08-04. Redemption has two rails: an atomic on-chain `redeem(...)` on `USDY_InstantManager`, gated on OndoIDRegistry membership ("Transactions from an address that is not registered in the OndoIDRegistry will revert with `UserNotRegistered`"), and an off-chain USD wire **to non-US bank accounts only**. On default, holders have a first-priority security interest enforced by **Ankura Trust** — but recourse is **capped at the collateral**: "All payments to be made by the Issuer… shall only be satisfied by recourse to the sums received or recovered… **Purchasers shall have no further recourse to the Issuer.**"

**OUSG.** Subscribed in USDC/PYUSD/RLUSD/USD for a **unitized Delaware limited partnership interest** — "Investors become limited partners in the Fund by acquiring OUSG tokens." The obligor is **Ondo I LP** (Delaware, SEC CIK 0001957431; GP Ondo I GP LLC; investment manager Ondo Capital Management LLC), relying on Rule 506(c) and ICA §3(c)(7). Ondo computes NAV itself with **NAV Consulting** as administrator holding read-only account access, and pushes it on-chain "once every Business Day… sometime between 4-6pm ET but, because this is a **manual process**, it may sometimes take longer", using "a conservative estimate of the Net Income it expects to receive for the day" trued up the next day. Live `OndoOracle.getAssetPrice(OUSG)` = **116.118574**. Instant mint/redeem is atomic but capped at **$50M global / $25M per investor per rolling 24h**; non-instant redemption is next-Business-Day against a 4pm ET cut-off. **No security agent, no collateral pledge and no overcollateralisation is documented for OUSG** — LPs hold a plain pro-rata claim, a materially weaker position than USDY holders, and Ondo does not claim otherwise.

**Ondo Global Markets / Ondo Stocks.** USDon (Ondo's own dollar token) is exchanged for a tokenised equity tracker (`TSLAon`, `NVDAon`, …), minimum $1.00, instant. The claim is a **structured note under Swiss law**: "a debt instrument issued by Ondo Global Markets (BVI) Limited… a bankruptcy-remote special purpose vehicle (SPV) organized in the British Virgin Islands. Tokenholder rights and obligations are governed by **Swiss law**." Holders get no shareholder rights of any kind. Price is set per trade by an off-chain **EIP-712-signed quote** valid ~30 seconds, priced by "a little bit of (proprietary) math" with spreads driven by "quote size, target profit, and several other proprietary considerations". Redemption is atomic into USDon (and optionally USDC, subject to swapper inventory); **there is no USD wire redemption at all**, and redemption is conditional on passing the Issuer's diligence — "there is a risk that a person acquiring Tokens on secondary markets will not meet such due diligence requirements and therefore **may not be able to redeem**". Disputes go to **arbitration in Zurich under the Swiss Rules**, and "Token holders are entitled to damages only… and are **not** entitled to the remedy of specific performance."

### 2. DESIGN

- **USDY transfer restrictions — [ON-CHAIN], and the gate is deliberately switched off.** `USDY.sol` enforces allowlist AND blocklist AND sanctions list in `_beforeTokenTransfer`, on `msg.sender`, `from` and `to`. But live `USDY.allowlist()` returns `0x5cd9e3a4c9933133b512da1b6ba4672160e0c665`, whose verified source is `contract AllowlistStub { function isAllowed(address) external pure returns (bool) { return true; } }`, confirmed behaviourally against a never-transacted address. **This is the mechanism behind Ondo's "permissionless yieldcoin" marketing: the gate is installed, wired into the transfer hook, and disabled.** `setAllowlist(address)` is callable by `LIST_CONFIGURER_ROLE` (**1 holder**), so it is re-armable in one transaction with no timelock.
- **OUSG transfer restrictions — [ON-CHAIN], a genuine allowlist.** `CashKYCSenderReceiver` requires all three parties KYC'd: `require(_getKYCStatus(_msgSender()), "…must be KYC'd to initiate transfer")`. Membership lives in `OndoIDRegistry`, whose state is `mapping(address rwaToken => mapping(address user => bytes32 userID))` — **binding an on-chain address to an off-chain user identity, per token.**
- **Ondo Stocks transfer restrictions — [ON-CHAIN], DENYLIST ONLY.** `GMToken._beforeTokenTransfer` → `OndoComplianceGMView` → `OndoCompliance.checkIsCompliant`, which reverts only on `UserBlocked()` or `UserSanctioned()`. **Anyone not blocked may receive and hold a tokenised equity; only a KYC'd party may mint or redeem it.** Holding is permissionless, the primary market is gated. Sanctions source is the Chainalysis oracle.
- **Freeze and forced transfer — [ON-CHAIN], and forced transfer is ABSENT.** `Blocklist` `0xd8c8174691d936E2C80114EC449037b13421B0a8` freezes in both directions. Every Ondo token carries `burn(address from, uint256 amount) onlyRole(BURNER_ROLE)`, commented "This function can be considered an admin-burn" — but the live role census on 2026-08-04 gives `BURNER_ROLE` member count **0 on USDY, 0 on OUSG, 0 on NVDAon**. **No `forceTransfer` / `seize` / `recover` exists in any Ondo contract read.** Ondo can immobilise value and (latently) destroy it; it cannot reassign it.
- **Pause — [ON-CHAIN], with a hot guardian.** USDY `PAUSER_ROLE` has **2** holders: the 4-of-7 admin Safe and `0x2e55b738f5969eea10fb67e326bee5e2fa15a2cc`, which is **a Gnosis Safe with threshold 1 of 9 owners** — any one of nine signers can halt USDY. The same address holds `PAUSER_ROLE` on OUSG and `EXECUTOR_ROLE`+`CANCELLER_ROLE` on the Ondo Stocks timelock. GM tokens additionally have a per-token `TokenPauseManager` used for market-hours and corporate-action halts.
- **Price feeds — [ON-CHAIN] publication of off-chain determinations, three different shapes.** USDY: range-compounding `RWADynamicOracle`, read through `USDYOracleWrapper` `0x87b126e5518b6a1Bb8465779b4607C45C643DF90`. OUSG: `OndoOracle` `0x9Cad45a8BF0Ed41Ff33074449B357C7a1fAb4094`, pushed manually once per business day — and note that the **legacy** oracle was bounded ("The price can only be set +/- 74 bps relative to the price movement of the BlackRock Short-term US Treasuries (SHV) ETF") while **the current one carries no documented sanity band**. Ondo Stocks: **no execution price feed at all**; price arrives as a signed `Quote` with `attestationId` replay protection and an expiration, and the on-chain `SyntheticSharesOracle` publishes only the shares-per-token multiplier.
- **Subscription/redemption cut-offs.** USDY instant: atomic; USDY fiat: 8pm UTC cut-off, "processed within five Business Days", $500 minimum (archived terms), non-EVM chains carry a $5,000 minimum and require emailing support. OUSG instant: atomic, $5,000 minimum, $50M/$25M caps; non-instant: 4pm ET, next business day, $100K subscribe / $50K redeem. OGM: atomic against a 30-second quote, $1 minimum. **"Business Day" is defined by Wilmington, Delaware bank holidays and Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia closures** — a US banking calendar governing a product sold exclusively to non-US persons.
- **The 40–50 day lockup — [LEGAL] basis, [ON-CHAIN] enforcement, currently dormant.** Archived: "you will not be able to claim your transferable Tokens until the **Restricted Period** for your Certificate ends; this is usually between **40 and 50 days** from the time you deposited funds." Pre-issuance the lender held a **Temporary Global Certificate**, mirrored on-chain by USDYc, "An onchain **bookkeeping representation** of the Temporary Global Certificate". Enforcement is not a constant — `USDYManager.setClaimableTimestamp(uint256, bytes32[]) onlyRole(TIMESTAMP_SETTER_ROLE)` with a `MintNotYetClaimable()` revert; **there is no `40` and no `50` anywhere in the code.** Status today: `USDYc.totalSupply()` = **0**, the language is gone from current docs, and `USDY_InstantManager` mints atomically.
- **Collateral agent — [LEGAL], CONFIRMED as Ankura Trust Company.** For USDY (archived): "investors in USDY have what's known as a 'first security interest' in USDY's underlying bank deposits and Treasuries, with **Ankura Trust** acting as the collateral agent… we have entered into **control agreements** with Ankura Trust and each of the banks and custodians holding assets backing USDY." For Ondo Stocks (current): "**Ankura Trust Company** serves as the Verification Agent and Security Agent… Holds a **first-priority security interest**". Secondary buyers are bound automatically: "Each person who acquires Tokens on the secondary market, **by acquiring such Tokens, appoints Ankura Trust Company, LLC as the Security Agent**." **Enforcement requires a tokenholder vote**: Ankura may act "subject to USDY Tokenholder approval… upon acceleration of the loans by the vote of USDY holders." Nothing on-chain implements this vote.
- **Register of record / transfer agent — [LEGAL].** **No transfer agent is named for any Ondo product** in anything fetched. NAV Consulting is OUSG's *fund administrator*, not a transfer agent. See §5(d).
- **Control plane — [ON-CHAIN], and the timelock covers the minority of the system.** USDY, rUSDY, USDYc, OUSG, OndoIDRegistry and USDon are EIP-1967 proxies; GM tokens are BeaconProxies. Upgrade authority: USDY / rUSDY / USDYc → Safe `0x1a694a09494e214a3be3652e4b343b7b81a73ad7` **4-of-7, no timelock**; OUSG → Safe `0xaed4caf2e535d964165b4392342f71bac77e8367` **4-of-7, no timelock**; OndoIDRegistry → Safe **3-of-5, no timelock**; USDon and the GM beacon → `TimelockController` `0x3715b2154d2ff4c5b027c7a1f734b53f27bc34f1`, `getMinDelay()` = **7200 s = 2 hours**, `PROPOSER_ROLE` = 1 (a 4-of-7 Safe), `EXECUTOR_ROLE` = 2 (the 1-of-9 Safe and a bare EOA), `TIMELOCK_ADMIN_ROLE` = **0 (renounced)**. **One beacon upgrade changes the code of all 444 Ethereum GM tokens at once.** USDY role census: `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` 1, `MINTER_ROLE` 4 (including the LayerZero OFT adapter), `PAUSER_ROLE` 2, `BURNER_ROLE` 0, `LIST_CONFIGURER_ROLE` 1.

## Circle USYC (Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund Ltd.)

### 1. WHAT IT DOES

An investor subscribes USDC and receives USYC, which is neither a note nor a deposit but **a share in a Cayman Islands mutual fund**: "USYC is the onchain representation of **Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund Ltd.** (SDYF)", and each token "serves as a digital representation of a share of the Hashnote International Short Duration Fund Ltd. (the 'Fund'), a Cayman Islands registered mutual fund". **The party that owes the money is SDYF, a CIMA-licensed Cayman exempted mutual fund — not Circle.** Circle's Bermuda entity is the Fund's agent: "The Fund has appointed Circle International Bermuda Limited ('CIBL')… as its **token administrator**, responsible for the management of USYC on behalf of the Fund", and Circle's own 10-K disclaims everything: "**The Company provides no guarantees and has no other financial obligations to each of the funds.**" NAV is struck once per business day off-chain by the prime broker and then published on-chain — "USYC price is calculated based on the Net Asset Value (NAV) of the Prime Brokerage account divided by the total supply of USYC on issue", reported "typically… at 9 am ET" — so the token is **price-accruing, not balance-accruing** (verified: oracle round 483 answer 1.133066960425761961 while balances are static). Only allowlisted, non-U.S. wallets may subscribe or redeem; the minimum is US$100,000 and the offering relies on Regulation S. Redemption runs through the **Smart Contract Teller**, which burns USYC and pays USDC "24/7/365" at "the latest reported price" — but the instant path is capacity-bounded and discretionary: "If you require more liquidity on a specific day, **please contact us**". On default or wind-down the answer is UNKNOWN, because the gating and suspension provisions live in the Fund's private offering documents; what is knowable is that at 2025-12-31 SDYF reported **total assets $1,537,818 thousand and total liabilities $—**, so at that date nothing ranked ahead of tokenholders.

### 5. WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

**(a) Prime broker, reserve composition, repo counterparty — CONFIRMED, with one part UNKNOWN.** Mechanism confirmed verbatim (A2). Prime broker **Marex**, primary-sourced at A4. Reserve composition confirmed only qualitatively ("primarily… reverse repo backed by U.S. government securities"). **Repo counterparty exposure: UNKNOWN** — no counterparty is named anywhere, no concentration limit is published, no holdings breakdown exists in any retrievable format. Note the contradiction: Circle's docs claim "The token price **and fund holdings** are published through an onchain Oracle", but `latestRoundData()` returns only a price and the richest payload carries only principal, interest, balance, price, supply, fee — **no composition at all**. On `At`: there *is* a daily NAV publication; there is **no reserve attestation report**. `At` is supported as NAV attestation and unsupported as reserve attestation, and the vocabulary conflates them.

**(b) REGISTER OF RECORD — CONFIRMED, and the direction of the sentence is the finding.** Three quotes settle it: "Ownership in SDYF is represented as **shares at the Transfer Agent, and whose digital twins are represented onchain**"; "**The fund administrator moves shares in the SDYF share registry to reflect movements in the USYC token**"; "**The client's redemption of the USYC token will be reflected in the fund registry by our fund administrator.**" The token is explicitly downstream of an off-chain share register — a "twin" is by construction a copy of something that exists elsewhere. But the *direction of causation* is the subtle part: the registry moves **to reflect** the token, and redemption **will be reflected in** the registry. **Operationally the token leads and the register follows; legally the register is still the record.** USYC is on-chain-primary in ordering and off-chain-primary in legal effect. A formalisation that treats the token as the ledger of record misstates what a holder owns; one that treats it as a mere mirror misstates where transactions sequence. Both readings are wrong alone, and the vocabulary can express neither.

**(c) Obligor — CONFIRMED as SDYF (Cayman); entity not renamed.** Circle acquired the *manager* ("100% of the ownership interest in **Hashnote Holdings LLC**… which, through its affiliates, is the **fund manager** of Hashnote International Short Duration Yield Fund Ltd."), not the fund. **An unresolved inconsistency across primary sources must be flagged:** the USYC docs, Circle's developer docs and the 10-K regulatory section all say "**Circle International Bermuda Limited issues USYC**", while 10-K Note 3 says SDYF is "**the issuer of USYC**" and the press-release disclaimer calls CIBL merely "**token administrator**". These are not reconcilable on their face; the defensible reading is that the **economic obligor is the Fund** and CIBL is the regulated issuing agent. Flagged, not resolved — the offering documents that would settle it are private. **Creditor ranking:** structurally creditors rank ahead, but SDYF reported **total liabilities $—** at 2025-12-31 (point-in-time, at $1.54B; the fund is now ≈$3.0B and no updated liability figure exists).

**(d) Instant-redemption capacity — `Rd` as an unconditional right is REFUTED.** The cap is not published as a number: "You can view the liquidity available on the USYC page in real time. **If you require more liquidity on a specific day, please contact us**." `**UNKNOWN — the numeric cap**`; `/api/liquidity` and `/api/teller` both 404, and no existing endpoint carries a capacity field. It is discretionary on three independent grounds: the Teller holds **0.0 USDC** (no escrowed pool backs the promise); overflow is handled by an email address, not a queue; and 100% instant liquidity is a **separately priced tier** — "'Private Liquidity Teller' (PLT)… allow the client to have up to 100% instant liquidity into USDC at all times… **Additional fees apply.**" If 100% instant liquidity must be purchased, the default Teller manifestly does not provide it. **Also: the fallback is NOT documented as T+1** — no source says exhausted Teller liquidity converts into T+1 settlement, and the paper must not assert it.

**(e) Offering memorandum — NOT OBTAINABLE.** `UNKNOWN`. Tried the full docs tree, Circle's developer docs, the pressroom, and EDGAR full-text (the Fund is not an EDGAR filer; the offering relies on an exemption so no registration statement exists). The press release confirms the documents exist and are the instrument that creates the claim: "An offer… is **only made to eligible investors through the Fund's offering documents**." **Gating, suspension, side letters and wind-down are therefore all UNKNOWN and must not be asserted.** The PLT is the only publicly visible glimpse of bilateral variation.

**(f) Qualified custody and segregation — segregation CONFIRMED; "qualified custody" REFUTED as a term.** Segregation from Circle's balance sheet is unusually well-evidenced: SDYF is an unconsolidated VIE, Circle's "maximum exposure to loss… is limited to its insignificant investment and its obligations to perform services as the manager", and "The Company provides no guarantees…". Symmetrically, **holders have no recourse to Circle.** Asset-level segregation is asserted, not independently verified. **The phrase "qualified custodian" appears nowhere in any USYC or Circle source fetched, and there is no separately named custodian at all** — custody sits with the prime broker, which is a broker. Fireblocks is "MPC Wallet", i.e. key management, not fund-asset custody. The paper must not describe USYC as employing qualified custody.

**(g) Sanctions/AML as process vs `Aw` as a static bit — `Aw` is INSUFFICIENT.** The gate is a **conjunction of two things with different temporal character**: a static-ish allowlist bit set once at onboarding, and a dynamic negative check against a third-party oracle that can invalidate a previously-valid wallet with no action by Circle, Hashnote or the holder. `Aw` captures the first. The second is an **external oracle acting as a revocation channel**, and also a live dependency on Chainalysis's data availability and correctness.

**Additional unnameables.**
1. **Collateral mobility at derivatives venues — the dominant actual use, entirely unnamed.** The 10-K says USYC "is intended **primarily for use as collateral in digital asset markets**" — the *primary* purpose, not a side use. Binance institutional clients hold it "as off-exchange collateral for derivatives trades", kept "through **Binance Banking Triparty**, or with **Ceffu**". The token sits immobilised with a third-party custodian, pledged as margin, accruing NAV, while an exchange extends trading capacity against it. **There is no symbol for pledged-but-yielding collateral held by a tri-party custodian.** This also explains the BNB-chain concentration: 97% of the supply exists on the chain of the single venue that accepts it as margin — a distribution that is a function of one exchange's collateral policy, itself a concentration risk with no name.
2. **Forward-priced subscription against a forecast NAV.** "After 2:00 pm ET… the effective subscription price is a **forecast of the next price report**." Neither `Ex` (an observed external datum) nor `Ix` (mechanical accrual): an issuer-computed projection, with the issuer bearing or capturing the difference.
3. **Asymmetric pricing between the two legs.** "redemptions occur at a different price than subscriptions" — a two-price mechanism with a time-varying spread.
4. **The PLT as a purchasable liquidity SLA.** A per-client, fee-bearing contractual guarantee of instant convertibility. Redemption is treated by the vocabulary as a uniform property of an instrument; USYC's is **stratified by contract**.
5. **Dual-ledger twinning itself**, and the fund administrator's role as the reconciling party.
6. **The issuer-agency split** — a Cayman fund as obligor with a Bermuda-regulated token administrator as the operating face. *The entity a holder deals with is not the entity that owes them.*

### 6. DELTA

**The corpus record is internally inconsistent before any evidence is considered.** It decomposes USYC as `At, Aw, Ex, Fz, Gp, Rd, Sh, Up` (8) and BUIDL as `At, Aw, Fz, Gp, Ps, Rd, Sh, Up, Xf, Xm` (10), yet `identicalClaimedByLane` records the two as identical. **They are not identical as written** — USYC's array uniquely contains `Ex`; BUIDL's uniquely contains `Ps`, `Xf`, `Xm`. The sets differ by four symbols. This is a bookkeeping error independent of any finding, and it cannot be repaired by editing USYC's array because the arrays as recorded already disagree.

Symbol by symbol, on the evidence:

- **`Ex` — CONFIRMED, twice over.** The price oracle (`description()` "USYC / USD", 18 dp, round 483, answer 1.133066960425761961) is provably the one the Teller uses (`Teller.oracle()` returns exactly that address), and the Chainalysis sanctions oracle is queried by Entitlements on every transfer. Pricing *and* permissioning both depend on external feeds. **Keep `Ex`.**
- **`Ps` — REFUTED.** USYC has no peg. Its price is **1.133066960425761961 and monotonically rising** because it is a NAV-accruing fund share, and the Teller is a NAV-priced mint/redeem with a 4 bps / 3 bps fee pair and a deliberately asymmetric two-price mechanism — the structural opposite of a 1:1 par swap. **This is the single clearest difference from BUIDL.** USYC must not carry `Ps`.
- **`Xf` — SUPPORTED, and its absence is the more surprising gap.** Live on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana with dedicated Cross Chain Tellers on Ethereum (`0x5dbeCcEC…60c7d`) and BSC (`0xf38979E0…3D08`), and the multichain fact is not marginal: **BNB Chain holds 2,573,937,729.19 USYC against Ethereum's 77,375,020.09**. A decomposition that omits cross-domain machinery from USYC while including it for BUIDL is describing USYC's minority deployment as if it were the whole product. Qualification: issuance is **native per chain**, so `Xf` here means multi-domain native issuance with a cross-domain subscribe/redeem path, not lock-and-mint bridging.
- **`Xm` — UNKNOWN.** Do not assert in either direction; every ABI probe reverted and no document names a protocol.
- **`Up` — CONFIRMED on-chain**, EIP-1967 slots populated on four contracts with UUPS upgrade entrypoints in each implementation. **Keep.**
- **`Gp` — CONFIRMED on-chain**, `pause()`/`unpause()`/`paused()` in the Entitlements implementation; live `false`. **Keep.**
- **`Fz` — CONFIRMED for freeze and forced *burn*; REFUTED for forced *transfer*.** If glossed "freeze/forced transfer", the gloss overstates USYC by one power and understates it by another. (Selector extraction is strong evidence of presence, weaker of absence.)
- **`Sh` — CONFIRMED.** "Hashnote mints… an amount of USYC tokens **equivalent to the number of shares** the investor has in the SDYF", and price = NAV ÷ supply; cross-chain token supply (≈2,651.3M) reconciles to the oracle's share count (2,654.9M) within 0.13%.
- **`Rd` — present in name, conditional in substance.** See §5(d).
- **Omissions the design supports:** **`Ix`** (USYC is price-accruing — value accrues in the price, not in balances; a clean instance the corpus omits) and **`Sv`** (the issuer determines the price, may delay it "subject to delays due to operational or technical reasons", *forecasts* the subscription price after 2 pm ET, and decides discretionarily how much instant liquidity exists on a given day — determination discretion in the ordinary sense). **`Wq` is correctly absent** — there is no queue — but that absence must not be read as evidence of unconditional liquidity; the queue is replaced by an email address.

**Rank basis.** The magnitude holds at a primary source; the rank does not. `usyc.hashnote.com/api/stats` returns `total_aum` **3,005,480,354.608642** (`last_updated` 2026-08-05T02:50:01Z) and the independent round-483 payload gives `balance` **3,008,198,120.68** — both corroborate **$3.01B from the issuer's own endpoint**. The **`#1` ranking is aggregator-only** (`app.rwa.xyz/treasuries`, **[AGGREGATOR]**); no primary source asserts a rank, and Circle's 10-K makes no "largest" claim. Growth datum: SDYF total assets were $1,537,818k at 2025-12-31, so the fund roughly doubled in seven months.

**Bottom line.** USYC and BUIDL are not the same instrument and must not carry the same array. USYC gains `Ex` (twice) and almost certainly `Xf`; loses `Ps` outright; `Xm` undetermined; `Fz` needs re-glossing; `Rd` needs a conditionality qualifier; `Ix` and `Sv` look like genuine omissions. The deepest structural fact — the token as a **digital twin of a transfer-agent share register, operationally leading but legally following** — has no symbol at all, and it is the fact that determines what a USYC holder actually owns.

## BlackRock BUIDL (BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund Ltd., via Securitize)

### 1. WHAT IT DOES

A qualified purchaser subscribes USD (or USDC) through Securitize Markets and receives BUIDL, which is **a share in a British Virgin Islands limited company** — `BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund Ltd.`, incorporated 2023, registered office care of 50 Hudson Yards, New York, exempt from the Investment Company Act under **Section 3(c)(7)** and offered under **Rule 506(c)**, per its own sworn Form D. **The obligor is that BVI company**, whose board on the current filing is Ian Pilgrim (Bermuda), W. William Woods (Cayman, Paradigm Governance Partners), Noelle L'Heureux (San Francisco), and Jennifer Collins (Carne Group, Cayman). The fund seeks a **stable $1.00 NAV** and says so with an explicit warning: "You could lose money by investing in the Fund. Although the Fund seeks to preserve the value of your investment at $1.00 per share, it cannot guarantee it will do so… **The Fund's sponsor has no legal obligation to provide financial support to the Fund.**" Yield is paid as new shares, daily: "Dividends are accrued each calendar day to the **holder of record at 3:00pm ET**, and paid out the following business day in the form of **new BUIDL tokens**." Redemption is a same-business-day facility mediated by the transfer agent — "Requests for redemptions must be received in good order by the **Transfer Agent** between 8:00 AM and 3:00 PM ET on the applicable Redemption Date to achieve same-day settlement" — and the eligibility screen is high: the Ethereum class is marked **Qualified Purchaser, $5M minimum, $250k subsequent**. On default or a break of the buck there is no on-chain mechanism at all; the remedy is a claim against a BVI company under its private subscription documents. **UNKNOWN — the fund's suspension/gating power; the subscription documents are not public and are not on EDGAR.**

### 2. DESIGN

- **Token contract and transfer restrictions — [ON-CHAIN].** BUIDL is a **Securitize DS Protocol ("Digital Securities") token**, verified by direct RPC rather than by assertion: `getDSService(uint256)`, `totalIssued()`, `walletCount()` and `isPaused()` all respond on the mainnet contract. The DS Protocol's public interfaces define the powers precisely — `issueTokens(address,uint256)`, `issueTokensCustom(...)` with a lock and a `_releaseTime`, `burn(address,uint256,string _reason)`, and **`seize(address _from, address _to, uint256 _value, string _reason)`** — an explicit forced transfer with an on-chain reason string, all `onlyIssuerOrAbove`. Every transfer runs `preTransferCheck(address,address,uint)` against the compliance service.
- **Identity-keyed balances — [ON-CHAIN], and this is the structurally interesting part.** DS Protocol tokens carry `balanceOfInvestor(string _id)` and an on-chain **Registry Service** keyed on an *investor ID string*, not an address: `registerInvestor(string _id, string _collision_hash)`, `addWallet(address,string _id)`, `getInvestor(address)`, `setCountry(string,string)`, and `setAttribute(string _id, uint8 _attributeId, uint256 _value, uint256 _expiry, string _proofHash)` with attribute constants `KYC_APPROVED = 1`, `ACCREDITED = 2`, `QUALIFIED = 4`, `PROFESSIONAL = 8`. **The ledger is keyed on legal identity; wallets are attached to an investor, attributes carry an expiry, and a `proofHash` points at an off-chain document.** BUIDL's live services: Trust `0x3a68756d0335e75c909ba1e8fefc1d4832c36c60`, Registry `0x0dac900f26de70336f2320f7ccedee70ff6a1a5b`, Compliance `0x07a1ebfb9a9a421249ddc71bddb8860cc077e3a9`.
- **Allowlist / KYC regime — [ON-CHAIN] enforcement of a [LEGAL] determination.** "BUIDL adheres to high KYC/AML standards and **only permits token transfers to whitelisted investors**." Determination is Securitize's off-chain onboarding; enforcement is the compliance service invoked on transfer.
- **NAV feed — there is none.** No oracle, no price contract, no `latestRoundData`. The token is a 6-decimal unit assumed at $1.00 and the accrual is delivered as *supply*, not as *price*. This is a real architectural difference from every other member of this category.
- **Subscription and redemption — [LEGAL] with an [ON-CHAIN] settlement leg.** Redemption cut-off 8:00 AM–3:00 PM ET on a Redemption Date for same-day settlement. The Securitize page also carries a monthly-close subscription calendar ("Funds must arrive by 11:00am New York Time… on the stated subscription deadline date in order to be deployed into the fund on the first day of the following month") — **flagged as uncertain**: the Luxembourg-time reference and the generic deadline table suggest a shared Securitize platform template rather than BUIDL-specific terms. **UNKNOWN — BUIDL's authoritative subscription calendar.**
- **Fund structure and service providers — [LEGAL].** BVI limited company, `is40Act` false, Section 3(c)(7), Rule 506(c); placement agent **Securitize Markets, LLC** (CRD 283256); cumulative `totalAmountSold` **$5,135,523,412** across **28** recorded investors as of the 2026-07-23 signature; minimum investment accepted on the Form D **$100,000**. Portfolio: "core investments of overnight repo and 3-month Treasuries". **UNKNOWN — I could not confirm BNY Mellon's exact role split (fund administrator vs custodian vs digital-asset custody) at a primary source**; the Securitize product page names a "Institutional-Grade Custody Partners" section whose entity names did not render in the scrape, and BNY Mellon's role is attested only in press. Do not assert it.
- **Transfer agent — [LEGAL], and the corpus names the wrong entity.** The SEC-registered transfer agent is **Securitize Transfer Agent, LLC** — CIK 0001782266, TA file number **084-06638**, FINS **003641**, TA-1/A filed 2025-12-30 from 78 SW 7th Street, Miami. It was **named "Securitize LLC" until 2025-04-14**. Its sole member is Securitize, Inc.; CEO Carlos Domingo. Its TA-1 declares an engaged service company in both directions: **Pacific Stock Transfer** (084-01145, Las Vegas). *A tokenised fund's transfer agent outsources recordkeeping to a legacy stock transfer agent.* **Securitize Markets, LLC is the broker-dealer and placement agent, not the transfer agent** — a distinct entity and a distinct registration.
- **Multi-chain — [LEGAL] structure with a [THIRD-PARTY] transfer path.** Each chain is a **separate share class of the same fund**, with its own management fee (Ethereum/Arbitrum/Optimism 50 bps; Aptos/Avalanche/Polygon/Solana 20 bps) — not one issuance bridged. Movement between chains is outsourced and disclaimed: "**Bridging is provided by a 3rd party provider and availability cannot be guaranteed** at all times or between all blockchains."
- **Control plane — [ON-CHAIN], and this is the most alarming finding in the category.** The Class A token `0x7712c34205737192402172409a8f7ccef8aa2aec` is a **703-byte hand-rolled delegatecall proxy**, not EIP-1967. Its runtime exposes exactly four functions — `owner()` (0x8da5cb5b), `target()` (0xd4b83992), `setOwner(address)` (0x13af4035), `setTarget(address)` (0x776d1a01) — with storage slot 0 = owner and slot 1 = implementation, and a fallback that delegatecalls to slot 1. Live: `owner()` = `0xe01605f6b6dc593b7d2917f4a0940db2a625b09e`, `target()` = `0x603bb6909be14f83282e03632280d91be7fb83b2` (17,236 bytes). **That owner has `eth_getCode` = `0x` and nonce 10 on two independent RPC endpoints — it is an externally owned account.** A single private key can call `setTarget` and replace the entire implementation of a multi-hundred-million-dollar BlackRock fund token, with no timelock and no multisig at the proxy layer. The I-Class token `0x6a9da2d710bb9b700acde7cb81f10f1ff8c89041` is a standard EIP-1967 proxy (implementation `0x9e2693f54831f6f52b0bb952c2935d26919a3626`) with the **same** EOA owner.

### 5. WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

**(a) REGISTER OF RECORD — CONFIRMED, and settled by the strongest sentence found anywhere in this category.** First, the entity: the SEC-registered transfer agent is **Securitize Transfer Agent, LLC** (084-06638), *not* Securitize Markets, LLC — the corpus names the wrong company (see §6). Second, the substance. Securitize's own product page states:

> "Redemptions of Shares effected on the blockchain at or immediately prior to the redemption deadline on a Redemption Date **may not be recorded on the Fund's books and records prior to such deadline**, including if the Transfer Agent has not yet determined that the transfer of Shares to its wallet is final. In such event, **the Shares will not be redeemed on such Redemption Date**; rather they will be redeemed on the next Redemption Date."

This is decisive. **The Fund's books and records are a distinct artefact from the blockchain, and they are authoritative over it.** A completed on-chain transfer is not an effective redemption; the transfer agent must independently *determine* finality and *record* it. The chain is an instruction channel that the transfer agent reads; the register is a separate book that the transfer agent writes. The 58-symbol vocabulary has no way to say this, and it is the single most important structural fact about BUIDL. Note also that the DS Protocol's `balanceOfInvestor(string _id)` means even the *on-chain* ledger is keyed on legal identity rather than address — the chain is not a bearer ledger even in form.

**(b) BlackRock's investment discretion — CONFIRMED unconstrained.** The only public statement of policy is a marketing phrase, "core investments of overnight repo and 3-month Treasuries". Nothing on-chain constrains duration, issuer mix, or repo exposure; there is no oracle, no holdings feed, no covenant. The manager can change the entire risk profile of the instrument without touching a contract, and **no element in the vocabulary is sensitive to that**. CONFIRMED as unnameable.

**(c) The obligor and its gating power — obligor CONFIRMED, gating UNKNOWN.** Obligor: BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund Ltd., a BVI limited company incorporated 2023, board named in §1, relying on 3(c)(7) and 506(c). Recourse is disclaimed at the sponsor level: "**The Fund's sponsor has no legal obligation to provide financial support to the Fund**, and you should not expect that the sponsor will provide financial support to the Fund at any time." **UNKNOWN — the board's suspension power. I could not obtain the subscription documents; the fund is not an EDGAR reporting company and files only Form D.** Do not assert a gate.

**(d) Dividend mechanics — CONFIRMED but the corpus has the frequency wrong.** "Dividends are accrued each calendar day to the **holder of record at 3:00pm ET**, and paid out **the following business day** in the form of **new BUIDL tokens**." This is **daily**, not monthly. The mechanism is: an off-chain declaration, against an off-chain determination of who was "holder of record" at a wall-clock instant, settled **in kind by minting**. Neither `Sh` nor `Ix` nor `Rb` names it. It is not `Ix` (no index; the unit stays $1.00), not `Rb` (balances change by an issuance event, not a global scaling factor), and not plain `Sh` (the pro-rata claim is constant per token; what varies is how many tokens you are given). **"A discretionary dividend declared off-chain against a holder-of-record snapshot and settled in kind by minting" has no symbol.** CONFIRMED unnameable, and the corpus's "monthly" is stale.

**(e) Reg D 506(c) / Reg S — 506(c) CONFIRMED, Reg S NOT CONFIRMED, and the real gate is 3(c)(7).** The Form D's `federalExemptionsExclusions` are exactly `06c`, `3C`, `3C.7` — Rule 506(c) plus **Investment Company Act Section 3(c)(7)**, and the product page corroborates with "Investor Eligibility: **Qualified Purchaser**". **Reg S is not evidenced** (Reg S offerings are not reported on Form D, so its absence is not evidence of absence — mark it UNKNOWN, not refuted). The point for the vocabulary: `Aw` names the gate but never *what it is testing for*. Here the gate is testing two different legal predicates at once — accredited-investor status under the Securities Act and qualified-purchaser status under the Investment Company Act — and it is 3(c)(7), the tighter one, that caps the fund's investor base and drives the $5M minimum. `Aw` cannot distinguish a gate that exists to satisfy a resale restriction from one that exists to preserve a fund's exemption from registration; **they fail differently and the remedies differ.** CONFIRMED unnameable.

**(f) BNY Mellon — UNKNOWN.** I could not confirm the custodian/administrator role split at a primary source. The Securitize page's custody-partner section did not render entity names in the scrape, and BlackRock publishes no BUIDL fact sheet I could retrieve. Press asserts BNY Mellon is custodian; that is Tier-4 and I decline to rely on it. **The corpus's claim that "the custodian's failure is the dominant tail risk and is unnameable" is probably right, but the custodian's identity is not established here.**

**(g) Minimum and calendar — CONFIRMED, with a contradiction between two primary sources.** The Form D swears `minimumInvestmentAccepted` = **$100,000**; the product page advertises **$5M** minimum with $250k subsequent for the Ethereum class. Both are primary. The reconciliation is almost certainly that $100,000 is the lowest the *issuer will accept* under the exemption while $5M is the *platform's* per-share-class ticket, but I did not find a source that says so. Report both.

**(h) The Circle 1:1 BUIDL→USDC facility — status UNKNOWN, and the corpus's characterisation is right in principle.** The current product page carries no reference to a Circle-operated par redemption facility; the only liquidity language is footnoted third-party disclaimers — "3rd party liquidity can be available during business hours of the fund as well as outside of business hours of the fund. **Availability of 3rd party liquidity is not guaranteed.**" **UNKNOWN — whether the Circle facility still operates in 2026 and at what capacity.** What *is* established is the structural point: third-party liquidity is disclaimed on BUIDL's own product page, so attributing a peg-swap module to BUIDL attributes to the instrument a property it does not itself have.

**Additional unnameables.**
1. **Single-key upgradeability of an institutional fund token.** `owner()` on the Class A proxy is an **EOA**, and it can call `setTarget` to replace the implementation with no delay. The vocabulary's `Up` says only "mutable implementation proxy"; it cannot express *who* may mutate, *how fast*, or *with what independence*. This is exactly the shape prohibition X9 ("`Up` with immediate single-key control") is meant to catch, yet the corpus records `armedProhibitions: []` for BUIDL. **The vocabulary has the prohibition and the decomposition failed to arm it** — that is a finding about the decomposition process, not about the vocabulary.
2. **Identity-keyed balances with expiring attributes and a document hash.** `balanceOfInvestor(string)`, `setAttribute(id, attrId, value, **expiry**, **proofHash**)`. `Aw` is a bit; this is a subsystem with its own key space, its own time dimension, and its own pointer into a legal file. An attribute that *expires* means eligibility decays without any actor doing anything.
3. **A share class per chain.** Same fund, different management fee, different token, one register. The vocabulary's `Xf`/`Xm` describe moving a claim between domains; BUIDL instead **issues a legally distinct claim per domain against a single off-chain book**. There is no symbol for "one obligor, N tokens, one register".
4. **Forced transfer as routine administration.** `seize(from, to, value, reason)` with a reason string is not an emergency power in a transfer-agent context — it is the on-chain expression of the ordinary legal remedies a transfer agent already has (lost certificates, court orders, escheatment). `Fz` reads as an emergency lever; here it is the normal operation of a securities register.
5. **The transfer agent's own outsourcing.** Securitize Transfer Agent, LLC's TA-1 names **Pacific Stock Transfer** as its service company. The chain of custody of the authoritative record runs token → Securitize → a legacy Nevada transfer agent. Nothing in the vocabulary reaches one link of that chain, let alone three.

## Maple Finance (syrupUSDC / syrupUSDT / syrupUSDG + institutional pools)

### 1. WHAT IT DOES

A lender deposits USDC (or USDT/USDG) into a Maple pool and receives **an ERC-4626 vault share** — "Each pool contract inherits the ERC 4626 standard… This standard informs how the LP Tokens accrue value from borrower repayments of strategies." The share is price-accruing: on the live syrupUSDC pool, `convertToAssets(1e6)` returns **1,176,799**, i.e. 1 syrupUSDC = 1.176799 USDC. **Who owes the money is a two-step chain and only the first step is on-chain:** the lender holds a claim on the Pool contract; the Pool holds `MapleLoan` contracts; each `MapleLoan` "is the contract that represents the agreement between a Lender and a Borrower" and is funded to a **named institutional borrower entity** under a bilateral agreement whose terms — governing law, recourse, covenants — are **not published** (`UNKNOWN`, see §5(a)). Pool value is `totalAssets` minus `unrealizedLosses`, where interest accrues mechanically from loan schedules and losses are **declared by a human**: the Pool Delegate or Governor calls `impairLoan` to recognise a "paper loss" and either may reverse it. Withdrawal is a **FIFO queue**, not a redemption right: "Withdrawals are processed on a first-in, first-out basis as liquidity becomes available… **Most withdrawals are processed in under 24 hours, but could take up to 30 days.**" On borrower default the chain is: grace period → optional `triggerDefault()` by the Pool Delegate → collateral repossessed and liquidated → first-loss cover applied → **any remaining loss is a straight reduction in `totalAssets`, borne pro rata by every remaining lender.** From the borrower's side: institutions agree terms with Maple off-chain, are onboarded through KYC, and receive a fixed-term or open-term loan they must over-collateralise; collateralisation is monitored 24/7 by Maple's operations team; a margin call gives them 24 hours to top up; failing that, "**Maple liquidates their collateral as part of the legal agreement with the borrower**."

### 2. DESIGN

- **Contract set — [ON-CHAIN].** `Pool` (ERC-4626, deliberately minimal, immutable) → `PoolManager` (all administrative logic; the Pool's only external counterparty) → `LoanManager`s attached as *strategies* via `addStrategy` (fixed-term and open-term variants) → `MapleLoan` instances. Plus `WithdrawalManager`, `MapleGlobals` (singleton protocol config), `MapleTreasury`, `PoolPermissionManager`, and a factory-per-contract pattern whose factories are allowlisted in Globals "so protocol contracts can verify on-chain that contracts have been vetted by the Maple DAO." External strategies (Aave, Sky) provide a secondary cash yield.
- **The permission gate — [ON-CHAIN], and far richer than a bit.** `PoolPermissionManager` implements four levels, verified against source constants: `PRIVATE = 0` (allowlist only), `FUNCTION_LEVEL = 1`, `POOL_LEVEL = 2`, `PUBLIC = 3`. Levels 1 and 2 match a **256-bit lender bitmap against a pool bitmap** — "Each bit represents a criteria off-chain that a lender must meet" — evaluated as `(poolBitmap & lenderBitmaps[lender]) == poolBitmap`. Two properties matter: **`PUBLIC` is a one-way ratchet** ("Once a lending pool is set to public, it cannot be changed back to permissioned"; invariant D), and **an unset bitmap returns `true`** ("the `hasPermission` function will always return true if the bitmaps are not set. This is by design"). Enforcement follows the holder: "sending permissioned pool LP tokens to another address will revert unless the receiving address is also on the Global Allowlist."
- **Is syrupUSDC permissionless? No — measured.** `PoolPermissionManager.permissionLevels(0x7ad5ffa5…6158f)` returns **1**, i.e. `FUNCTION_LEVEL`. syrupUSDC is *function-level bitmap-gated*, not `PUBLIC`. Deposits from contracts route through `SyrupRouter` (`0x134cCaaA4F1e4552eC8aEcb9E4A2360dDcF8df76`) "with authorization handled [by] PoolPermissionManager."
- **Pool Delegate discretion — [ON-CHAIN] powers over [LEGAL] judgements.** The delegate funds and refinances loans, sets the liquidity cap and fees, calls `impairLoan` / `removeLoanImpairment`, and elects whether to `triggerDefault()` ("the Pool Delegate has the *option* to repossess the Loan"). Governor override is asymmetric and explicit: "If the **Governor** impairs the Loan, the Pool Delegate **DOES NOT** have the right to revert the impairment. If the **Pool Delegate** impairs the Loan, the Governor **DOES** have the right to revert."
- **Collateral — the honest answer is *both*, and the corpus's is wrong for the protocol layer.** At the protocol layer collateral is **on-chain and in the loan contract**: the default procedure is "repossess it and represent the principal and interest value of the Loan as `unrealizedLosses`" then "**liquidate all collateral posted against the Loan**", and Maple's own live API reports `collateralValue` **$1.921B** against `loansValue` **$1.301B**, a `collateralRatio` of **147.72%**. At the operational layer the *liquidation* is off-chain and relationship-mediated. See §5(b) — this is the sharpest refutation in this file.
- **Margin call and liquidation — [LEGAL] with no on-chain incentive.** "The Maple Operations team has a proprietary alert system in place with **three separate sources for price feeds**, and a 24/7/365 live monitoring process… the borrower is automatically notified and has **24 hours** to top up… If they fail to do so in time, **Maple liquidates their collateral as part of the legal agreement with the borrower**… Maple has partnered with a number of leading **OTC desks**… If needed, the team also has the ability to withdraw to CEXs or DEXs to liquidate instead." **There is no auction, no keeper, no liquidation bonus.**
- **Withdrawal — [ON-CHAIN] queue.** `WithdrawalManager` (queue variant) at `0x1bc47a0dd0fdab96e9ef982fdf1f34dc6207cfe3` for syrupUSDC; FIFO, processed by "the Maple Direct team", assets pushed to the lender's wallet without a second transaction; a pending request can be reduced or cancelled and the LP shares returned.
- **Fees — [ON-CHAIN].** Delegate management fee and protocol fee, split per pool. Measured: `delegateManagementFeeRate()` on the syrupUSDC PoolManager is **0**. On default, "All liquidated collateral and cover first goes towards recovering fees owed to the protocol, after which all funds are returned to the Pool. **The Pool Delegate does not recover any fees owed during a Loan default.**"
- **SYRUP staking — [ON-CHAIN] but functionally retired.** "**Staking rewards were sunset with MIP-019**"; "the staking interface only supports unstaking"; "stSYRUP can be unstaked at any time without any limits or penalties"; "25% of ongoing protocol revenue will be allocated to the Syrup Strategic Fund (SSF) for SYRUP buybacks". **stSYRUP is not slashable and never was.**
- **Lender emissions — [ON-CHAIN] and expired.** The Drips programme "**has been phased out** in favor of direct incentives for using syrupUSDC and syrupUSDT with ecosystem partners", distributed via Merkl; "Unclaimed Drips after 18th Feb 2026 cannot be claimed anymore." Verified on-chain: `syrupDrip` `0x509712F368255E92410893Ba2E488f40f7E986EA` has `asset()` = SYRUP (`0x643C4E15…52d66`), `maxId()` = 14,995 and **`deadline()` = 1771416000 = 2026-02-18T15:00:00Z — five and a half months in the past.** Current lender rewards are funded by third parties through Merkl, not by the protocol.
- **First-loss cover — [ON-CHAIN] but explicitly unused.** Verbatim from the docs' own heading: "**First-Loss Capital (Unused)** — *Note this aspect of the protocol isn't used as the Pool Delegate Cover is unused due to most pool's being managed by Maple Direct.*"
- **Control plane — [ON-CHAIN], with a defect worth naming.** Governor is the `GovernorTimelock` contract `0x2eFFf88747EB5a3FF00d4d8d0f0800E306C0426b` managed by a multisig, with "schedule → delay → execution window". Pool-delegate-level upgrades (`PoolManager.upgrade()`, `LoanManager.upgrade()`, `WithdrawalManager.upgrade()`) go through `MapleGlobals.scheduleCall()` with a two-week wait. A `Security Admin` can pause every function. **But:** "**Timelocks parameters are set by the `Governor` and can be changed at any time, having a retroactive effect**", and the docs give the worked example — a scheduled call becomes immediately executable if the Governor shortens the delay after it was scheduled.
- **Cross-chain — [ON-CHAIN], via Chainlink CCIP.** syrupUSDC/USDT are available on Solana, Arbitrum, Base, Plasma and others through a "CCIP-based crosschain receiver… developed in partnership with Chainlink", with documented failed-message retry and recovery. A `SyrupOftAdapter` also exists for the SYRUP token.

### 5. WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

**(a) Bilateral master loan agreement, governing law, lawyer-resolved default — CONFIRMED in substance, UNKNOWN in detail.** Confirmed: the enforcement is legal, not algorithmic — "Maple liquidates their collateral **as part of the legal agreement with the borrower**", and Maple positions itself as "a technology services provider… **Maple is not a lender**". **UNKNOWN — the governing law, and no agreement template is published.** I searched the docs tree and the public repos and found no loan agreement, term sheet, or choice-of-law clause. The corpus's "NY/English law" is **not evidenced here and should be marked UNKNOWN rather than asserted.** What is certain is the shape: `MapleLoan` "represents the agreement between a Lender and a Borrower" on-chain while the enforceable obligation lives in a document the protocol cannot read.

**(b) "Collateral is held in off-chain qualified custody (BitGo/Anchorage) under a tri-party control agreement, NOT in the lending contract" — REFUTED at the protocol layer, and this is the sharpest correction in this file.** The default procedure is unambiguous that the protocol holds and disposes of collateral: "If the Loan has collateral, **repossess it**… **liquidate all collateral posted against the Loan**. This will increase `totalAssets` by the total amount of cash that is recovered from the liquidation", and the two-step collateralised-default procedure exists precisely because collateral must be repossessed on-chain before liquidation. Maple's own API reports **$1.921B of collateral against $1.301B of loans**. **No primary source I fetched names BitGo, Anchorage, Copper, or any tri-party control agreement** — I searched the docs tree and found none. The accurate statement is a *split*: **custody and accounting of collateral are on-chain; price monitoring and the act of selling are off-chain and discretionary.** The corpus's claim that "`Ct` computes a threshold against collateral the protocol cannot itself seize" is therefore **wrong on seizure and right on sale** — and the residual finding is better and sharper than the original: *the protocol can seize but cannot sell.*

**(c) "The margin call is a phone call… there is no `Li`" — CONFIRMED.** No liquidator contract, no auction, no keeper reward exists in the repos or the docs. Liquidation is executed by Maple's operations team through OTC desks, with CEX/DEX as fallback. `Sv` covers the discretion; the **absence of an enforcement primitive** is the finding, and the vocabulary has no way to record a negative — there is no symbol meaning "solvency is tested on-chain and enforced off-chain".

**(d) Credit underwriting as a process — CONFIRMED unnameable; and `Uc` is doubly wrong for Maple.** "This interest is determined by the loan terms set by **Maple's credit underwriting and risk management**", and "the borrower's ability to meet margin calls quickly is **checked pre-issuance**… and monitored on an ongoing basis." Nothing on-chain represents diligence, financials, or a covenant package. And the collateralisation policy is now firmly over-collateralised — **147.72% measured live** — so `Uc` (undercollateralized credit) is the wrong symbol on the facts, while the *business* remains an underwriting business. **The vocabulary offers a symbol for the balance-sheet shape and none for the judgement, and here the balance sheet says "secured lending" while the risk is entirely in the judgement.**

**(e) Dynamic instant-liquidity buffer — REFINED, not simply confirmed.** The FIFO queue is real and on-chain, and the syrupUSDC FAQ states the policy target: "**normally have instant liquidity**, but in rare cases withdrawals can take around 24h with the maximum possible time being 30 days." Critically, Maple's documented *instant* path is not a protocol buffer at all — it is the secondary market: "As an alternative to withdrawals, users can **swap syrupUSDC via Uniswap or Balancer**… This enables instant liquidity for users that do not want to wait." **The instrument's liquidity is partly outsourced to AMM pools the protocol does not control**, which the vocabulary cannot express: `Wq` names the queue, and nothing names "the redemption right is slow and the real exit is a third-party market".

**(f) Concentration limits per borrower — UNKNOWN, leaning policy-only.** `liquidityCap()` (2,500,000,000 on syrupUSDC) caps the *pool*, not exposure to any one borrower. I found no per-borrower concentration parameter in `PoolManager`, `Globals`, or the docs. **UNKNOWN — whether any concentration limit is enforced on-chain.** The docs discuss concentration only as risk-management practice.

**(g) "The KYC-gated institutional pools and the permissionless syrup wrapper are the SAME loan book" — REFUTED by Maple's own FAQ.** "Maple established **new unique pools** for syrupUSDC, syrupUSDT, and syrupUSDG **to ensure that proceeds and risks are segmented from existing pools** accessible to Maple customers." They are separate pools with separate `PoolManager`s and separate loan books, underwritten by the same team under the same policy. And syrupUSDC is **not permissionless** — `permissionLevels` returns `FUNCTION_LEVEL`, not `PUBLIC`. So the corpus's framing ("a wrapper that launders a permissioned claim into a permissionless one") **does not describe Maple.** It *does* describe Centrifuge's deRWA tokens exactly (see that section), so the residue is real for the category — just attached to the wrong protocol.

**Additional unnameables.**
1. **A retroactively shortenable timelock.** "Timelocks parameters are set by the Governor and **can be changed at any time, having a retroactive effect**", with a worked example in which a pending scheduled call becomes immediately executable. `Tg` asserts delayed execution; it cannot express *a delay the delayed party controls*. This is arguably a defect the vocabulary should be able to name, because the protection is nominal.
2. **A manager-declared unrealised loss that reprices the exit.** `impairLoan` writes `unrealizedLosses`, which "is factored into the Pool's value for withdrawals, **incentivizing current Liquidity Providers to remain in the Pool**". A discretionary mark that changes what an exiting holder receives, is reversible, and is explicitly designed to alter exit incentives. Neither `Sv` (discretion) nor `Sl` (loss allocation) names *a reversible provisional loss used as an exit deterrent*.
3. **Asymmetric override between two discretionary authorities.** Governor impairment cannot be reversed by the Delegate; Delegate impairment can be reversed by the Governor. The vocabulary treats authority as a set, not a lattice.
4. **A default waterfall that pays protocol fees first.** "All liquidated collateral and cover **first goes towards recovering fees owed to the protocol**, after which all funds are returned to the Pool." This is a genuine priority ordering over recovery proceeds — `Tr`-shaped but at the fee layer, over a pool with no tranches.
5. **A permission gate that fails open.** "the `hasPermission` function will always return true if the bitmaps are not set. **This is by design.**" `Aw` has no notion of a default, let alone a permissive one.
6. **A one-way ratchet to permissionless.** "Once a lending pool is set to public, **it cannot be changed back**." An irreversible transition in the access regime — a property of the gate over time, which `Aw` cannot carry.
7. **An unused first-loss module that still exists in the code.** The vocabulary cannot distinguish "the protocol has a backstop" from "the protocol has a backstop contract that is deliberately empty".

### 6. DELTA

Corpus record: `Aw, Bs, Ct, Em, Fd, Ft, Gp, Pl, Sh, Sv, Tg, Up, Wq`, with `Ct` derived and `satisfiesRequirementsAndWarrants: false`.

- **`Ct` — REAL and on-chain, so its "derived" status is wrong.** Collateral is posted to and repossessed by the loan contracts and the ratio is published by the protocol's own API at **147.72%**. The threshold *test* that triggers a margin call is off-chain (three price feeds, an ops team), but the collateral accounting the test refers to is on-chain. Promote it, or split it: on-chain collateral custody, off-chain threshold evaluation.
- **`Bs` — REFUTED twice over.** (i) Pool Delegate Cover, the actual first-loss module, is documented as **"First-Loss Capital (Unused)"**. (ii) stSYRUP is not slashable and its rewards were **sunset with MIP-019**; it now supports only unstaking, "at any time without any limits or penalties". Maple currently has **no slashable first-loss capital of any kind**. Drop `Bs`.
- **`Sl` — MISSING and clearly present.** "Remaining losses are represented to LPs as **a decrease in `totalAssets`**" — loss assigned to a claim class, pro rata, which is exactly what `Sl` names. Its absence is the most consequential omission in this decomposition, because with `Bs` removed `Sl` becomes the *only* loss-absorption mechanism Maple has, and law L1d (`Pl` → one of `Li|Ad|Sl|Bs`) is satisfied by `Sl` alone. This very plausibly explains the recorded `satisfiesRequirementsAndWarrants: false`.
- **`Ep` — arguably MISSING, but the answer is "no" for syrupUSDC.** Maple's older cyclical withdrawal manager was window-based; syrupUSDC uses the **queue** variant (`WithdrawalManager` queue, FIFO, processed as liquidity appears). `Wq` is the right symbol and `Ep` should **not** be added for the current syrup pools. Flagging because the corpus's silence is correct here for a reason worth recording.
- **`Li` — genuinely ABSENT. Confirmed.** No liquidator, no auction, no keeper incentive anywhere in the repos or docs.
- **`Tr` — genuinely ABSENT between lenders. Confirmed.** All LPs in a pool are pari passu; there is no senior/junior share class. The only priority ordering is protocol fees ahead of the pool on recovery proceeds.
- **`Em` — REFUTED as of 2026-08-04.** Both protocol-funded emission channels are closed. Staking rewards were sunset by MIP-019; the Drips lender programme "has been phased out", its final claim window shut on 2026-02-18, and the on-chain `syrupDrip` contract's `deadline()` is **1771416000 = 2026-02-18T15:00:00Z**, five and a half months past. Remaining lender incentives are "distributed via Merkl" by **ecosystem partners** — third-party funded, which is precisely what `Em` (protocol-funded emissions) is not. What survives is `Fd`: 25% of revenue to the Syrup Strategic Fund for buybacks. Drop `Em`; keep `Fd`.
- **`Fd` — CONFIRMED**, but note `delegateManagementFeeRate()` is **0** on syrupUSDC, so the delegate/protocol split the corpus cites as "a real on-chain flow" is currently zero on the flagship pool.
- **`Tg` — present but materially weaker than the symbol implies.** See §5 note 1: the delay is retroactively mutable by the Governor.
- **`Aw` — present but structurally richer**: four levels, 256-bit criteria bitmaps, transfer-time enforcement, a permissive default, and a one-way ratchet to `PUBLIC`.
- **`Xf`/`Xm` — MISSING.** syrupUSDC/USDT move across Solana, Arbitrum, Base and Plasma over **Chainlink CCIP** with a documented receiver and a failed-message recovery path. That is cross-domain asset transfer under verified messaging, and the corpus records neither.
- **`Ft` — CONFIRMED and the corpus's marker is right**, but sharpen it: Maple runs **both** fixed-term and open-term loan managers, so the maturity mismatch the marker describes is a *choice* per strategy, not a fixed property.
- **Rank basis — verified at a primary source and materially higher than recorded.** The corpus cites DefiLlama $2.263B **[AGGREGATOR]**. Maple's own API on 2026-08-04 gives `loansValue` **$1.3005B**, `collateralValue` **$1.9211B**, and the docs' own AUM formula applied to the latest point (2026-08-03) yields **$3.525B**. These measure different things and the paper must say which. The corpus's characterisation of Maple as "the largest on-chain private-credit venue by a wide margin" is not contradicted.

## Centrifuge (Centrifuge Protocol V3)

### 1. WHAT IT DOES

An investor subscribes an ERC-20 asset (typically USDC) into a **vault** attached to a **share class** of a **pool**, and receives a **share token**: an ERC-20 that "represent[s] a claim on the pool's assets or yield" and is "**price-accruing**, meaning their value increases over time rather than their balance increasing". The flagship live example is **JTRSY** — `Janus Henderson Treasury Fund`, `0x8c213ee79581ff4984583c6a801e5263418c4b86`, 6 decimals, **792,751,306.27 shares at a price of 1.111749542989796511**, i.e. **$881.3M**, read from Centrifuge's own API. **Who owes the money is an off-chain fund, not the protocol:** the issuer is **Anemoy Capital SPC Limited**, a British Virgin Islands **segregated portfolio company** regulated by the BVI Financial Services Commission, with Anemoy Asset Management as investment manager and Janus Henderson as sub-investment manager; Centrifuge is infrastructure and owes nothing. NAV is not computed by the protocol: "A pool's NAV is **typically computed offchain**, for example from a portfolio held with a bank or custodian. The issuer publishes the resulting share price to the Hub" by calling `hub.updateSharePrice(poolId, scId, sharePrice, uint64(block.timestamp))`, and then pushes it to each spoke chain. Subscription and redemption are **asynchronous (ERC-7540)** and pass through a five-stage lifecycle — pending → approved → issued/revoked → fulfilled → claimed — in which the issuer, not the investor, controls approval and the execution price for each batch. Sync-deposit vaults (ERC-4626) skip the request phase for deposits only. On default of an underlying asset there is **no on-chain mechanism**: the loss appears only when the manager marks the share price down.

### 2. DESIGN

- **Hub and spoke — [ON-CHAIN].** "Each pool selects a single **hub** chain as its source of truth, then issues tokens and vaults across any number of **spoke** chains." Hub holds pool config, double-entry accounting, holdings, share-class management and cross-chain coordination; each spoke holds a local registry of token instances, vaults, escrows and balance sheets. Live hub-side contracts on mainnet: `HubRegistry` `0x19f46D81…aDE93`, `Accounting` `0x050206c3…a7396`, `Holdings` `0x3f0c8D8d…C918d`, `ShareClassManager` `0xaFFC269c…d69BEf`, `HubHandler` `0x0dEFb429…c3392C`; spoke-side `Spoke` `0xEC3582fc…525aB`, `BalanceSheet` `0x12a110cE…0f43e`, `VaultRegistry` `0xd9531AC4…f2CA7B`. **Core contracts are deployed at identical addresses across all chains.**
- **Share token and transfer hooks — [ON-CHAIN], and this is the compliance engine.** "Centrifuge share tokens are ERC-20 tokens extended with **ERC-1404** (Simple Restricted Token) and a modular **transfer hook** system." Four shipped hooks plus none: **FullRestrictions** (`0x8E680873…24a4`), **FreelyTransferable** (`0x2a9B9C14…1A61`), **RedemptionRestrictions** (`0xE5423eD8…17f06`), **FreezeOnly**, and no hook. Membership is **expiry-dated**: `BaseTransferHook` "encodes member validity and freeze status in the `hookData` structure (bytes16)… the first 8 bytes (uint64) representing the memberlist **valid-until timestamp** and the least significant bit indicating **freeze status**", packed into the same 256-bit slot as the balance. The hook is **action-aware** — it distinguishes deposit requests, deposit claims, redemption requests, redemption claims, cross-chain transfers and peer-to-peer transfers, "enabling different rules for different operation types" — and it is replaceable per share class via `updateShareHook` without redeploying the token.
- **Freeze and forced transfer — [ON-CHAIN], verified in source at tag v3.2.0.** "Freeze / unfreeze an address — Per-address freeze flag in token storage"; "Recover tokens from a lost wallet — **Forced transfer via balance sheet `transferSharesFrom()`**". In `src/core/spoke/BalanceSheet.sol` the function is guarded `isManager(poolId)`, refuses to pull from an endorsed contract, and calls `token.authTransferFrom(...)` on `ShareToken` (which is itself `auth`-gated). Issue and revoke are likewise `isManager(poolId)`.
- **NAV publication — [ON-CHAIN] channel for a single-party off-chain assertion.** `Hub.updateSharePrice(PoolId, ShareClassId, D18 pricePoolPerShare, uint64 computedAt)` is guarded by `_isManager(poolId)` and nothing else. **Two properties are worth stating plainly:** the `computedAt` timestamp is **supplied by the caller**, not taken from the block, so freshness is self-attested; and there is **no dispute window, challenge bond, or deviation bound** anywhere in the call path. Propagation is a separate explicit act: `hub.notifySharePrice{value: gas}(poolId, scId, centrifugeId, msg.sender)` per destination network. Third-party oracles (Chronicle is the named example) are **optional and additive** — "use a small wrapper that reads the oracle and calls `hub.updateSharePrice()`" — i.e. even an independent feed is laundered through the same issuer-controlled setter. A spoke-side manager can additionally call `BalanceSheet.overridePricePoolPerShare(...)`.
- **Async vaults — [ON-CHAIN].** ERC-7540 request/fulfil, with a `BatchRequestManager` (`0xc52Bd1Bd…3dFe77`) that sets "the execution price for individual deposit and redemption batches… separately" from the published reference price. Sync-deposit vaults (ERC-4626 + ERC-7540) and pooled ERC-7575 vaults also exist.
- **Cross-chain messaging — [ON-CHAIN], multi-adapter with a per-pool quorum.** Adapters deployed on mainnet: **Chainlink** `0x39CF679E…7D955`, **Axelar** `0x34e90423…a6484`, **Wormhole** `0x4BE43040…706A7` (and a second Wormhole adapter `0x6b98679e…D39Ec`), aggregated behind `MultiAdapter` `0x35C837F0…073BE` and `Gateway` `0x19a524D0…D3172`. Each pool calls `setAdapters` to choose its providers, "**a confirmation threshold**, setting the minimum number of adapters that must deliver a message before it is processed", and recovery adapters. "A pool handling high-value transactions can require three out of four adapters to confirm, while a pool with lower risk tolerance might operate with a single adapter."
- **Governance and emergency control — [ON-CHAIN].** "The protocol is controlled by the `Root` contract, an **immutable** smart contract that holds admin (ward) access on all other contracts" — mainnet `0xdc9456e7e20f15029C8231Ec433a20F404b7235E`. "**All privilege escalation through `Root` goes through a 48-hour timelock.**" Two guardians with separate Safes and identical addresses on every network: **ProtocolGuardian** `0xCEb7eD5d5B3bAD3088f6A1697738B60d829635c6` (pause, upgrades, cross-chain; "Every transaction is verified by third-party signers from Cantina") and **OpsGuardian** `0x055589229506Ee89645EF08ebE9B9a863486d0dE` (wiring and pool creation; **no pause authority**). Pause semantics are narrow and worth quoting: "the global pause **halts all cross-chain messaging**, both inbound and outbound. **Local vault operations (deposit, redeem, withdraw) are not affected.**" Access control is the `Auth` ward pattern throughout.
- **Pool-level roles — [ON-CHAIN].** Hub manager (top-level, and "**any hub manager can grant or revoke the hub manager role for other addresses**"); balance-sheet manager (per chain, asset movement and share issuance only); gateway manager (can block outgoing messages for one pool on one destination — a pool-scoped circuit breaker); `OnOfframpManager` (permissionless onramp, relayer-gated offramp to pre-approved destinations); and **`MerkleProofManager`**, which assigns each strategist a **merkle policy root** encoding the exact set of allowed calls, verified per call, restricting "by target contract, function selector, and parameter values".
- **Off-chain wrapper — [LEGAL].** For JTRSY: Anemoy Capital SPC Limited (BVI SPC), described on the fund's own site as "Registered and regulated fund" approved by the "**BVI Financial Services Commission**", with a "**Bankruptcy remote SPV**" structure, working "with an independent prime broker, custodian, tokenization and issuance and fund admin service providers". **UNKNOWN — the names of the administrator, custodian, prime broker and auditor; the Anemoy page discloses roles without entities.**
- **deRWA tokens — [ON-CHAIN] wrapper over a [LEGAL]ly restricted claim.** deJTRSY `0xa6233014b9b7aaa74f38fa1977ffc7a89642dc72` ("JTRSY deRWA", 18 dp, 5,005,320.37 supply, price 1.032850, own hook `0xd51d8450…a2a0`), plus deJAAA, deCRDX, deSPXA. "With deRWA, issuers can wrap existing tokenized securities into **freely transferable tokens**"; "**mint/redeem functions stay at KYC endpoints**." A permissioned share sits in the wrapper; a permissionless token trades on top.
- **Tranches — gone.** V2's DROP/TIN two-tranche structure is legacy. V3 pools have **multiple share classes**, described as capable of expressing "junior/senior tranches", but this is a naming and permissioning convention, not a mechanism (see §5(e)).

### 5. WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

**(a) Off-chain SPV, security trustee, bankruptcy remoteness — SPV and remoteness CONFIRMED at the issuer's own site; security trustee UNKNOWN.** Anemoy's JTRSY page states the fund is regulated by the BVI FSC and has a "**Bankruptcy remote SPV**" structure. **UNKNOWN — no security trustee is named anywhere I fetched**, and no offering document is public. The corpus's "security trustee holding the assets for tokenholders' benefit" is **not evidenced** and should be marked UNKNOWN rather than asserted. What *is* now nameable-in-fact but not nameable-in-vocabulary is the SPC form itself: a **segregated portfolio company**, where each portfolio's assets are statutorily ring-fenced from the others' liabilities. That is a legal firewall between claim classes that looks superficially like `Im` (isolated market) but is created by BVI statute, not by contract deployment, and survives insolvency — which `Im` does not claim to do.

**(b) NAV pushed by a NAV Manager as an unchallenged single-party assertion — CONFIRMED, and the source is worse than the corpus claimed.** `Hub.updateSharePrice(PoolId, ShareClassId, D18 pricePoolPerShare, uint64 computedAt)` at `src/core/hub/Hub.sol:314`, guarded by `_isManager(poolId)` alone. There is no dispute path, no bond, no deviation bound, no staleness rejection — **and the `computedAt` timestamp is a caller-supplied argument**, so even the freshness signal is self-attested by the party whose price it is. `hub.notifySharePrice` then pushes it to each spoke. Third-party oracles do not fix this: the documented integration pattern reads Chronicle and *calls the same setter*. `Oa` does not apply (no assertion/dispute game); `At` names a periodic report, not a continuous price; `Ex` presumes an adversarial or aggregated feed. **NAV manipulation is the primary failure mode and has no representation.** CONFIRMED, and strengthened.

**(c) Asset-level servicing — CONFIRMED unnameable, and the on-chain state is emptier than expected.** There is no delinquency state, no roll rate, no write-off flag, no impairment call anywhere in the V3 protocol. The `Holdings` and `Accounting` contracts carry valuations and journal entries; `src/valuations/` contains exactly `IdentityValuation.sol` and `OracleValuation.sol`. **A defaulted underlying asset has no on-chain representation at all — it appears only as a lower number in the next `updateSharePrice` call.** Compare Maple, which at least has `impairLoan` and `unrealizedLosses`. Centrifuge V3 has strictly less servicing state than the corpus's "`Sv` gestures at discretion but names no state" implies: there is no state to gesture at.

**(d) Origination — CONFIRMED unnameable.** Nothing on-chain records who selected an asset or on what criteria. The `MerkleProofManager` constrains *what calls a strategist may make*, which is an execution-scope constraint, not a credit criterion. The vocabulary has no way to distinguish a pool whose assets were chosen by a rated manager from one whose assets were chosen arbitrarily.

**(e) `Tr` — REFUTED for V3, and this needs saying loudly.** A code search over `repo:centrifuge/protocol` returns **zero** hits for `waterfall`, `senior`, `junior` and `subordinat`. `tranche` returns six, every one of them in a 2023 Code4rena audit document, a test-properties file, or a legacy `env/spell/*.sol` migration script — **none in `src/`**. The docs' "junior/senior tranches" phrasing describes a *configuration idiom*: an issuer may create two share classes, whitelist them differently, and price them differently. There is **no loss-allocation logic, no seniority ordering, no overcollateralisation test, no interest-coverage test, and no trigger that redirects anything.** Subordination, if it exists in a Centrifuge pool, exists entirely in the fund's offering documents and is expressed on-chain only as the manager choosing to mark one share class down before another. **`Tr` should be removed from Centrifuge's decomposition.** The corpus's residue note ("`Tr` gives DROP/TIN seniority but not the trigger events") is describing V2/Tinlake, not V3.

**(f) Hub/spoke NAV propagation and stale spoke prices — CONFIRMED.** The price is authoritative on the hub and a message-derived copy on each spoke, propagated only when someone pays gas for `notifySharePrice` per destination. There is no automatic propagation and no freshness check I could find at the consuming end; a spoke can therefore carry a stale price indefinitely if nobody pushes. Worse for the vocabulary, `BalanceSheet.overridePricePoolPerShare(...)` lets a per-chain manager set a *different* price locally. **`Xm` names verified messaging; it cannot say "the canonical accounting lives on chain A and chain B trades a copy that may be stale or locally overridden."** CONFIRMED unnameable.

**(g) The `Ex` marker — CONFIRMED.** The NAV feed is issuer-published and undisputable, structurally unlike a medianizer. See (b).

**Additional unnameables.**
1. **The deRWA wrapper is the category's cleanest instance of "laundering a permissioned claim into a permissionless one".** A restricted share (FullRestrictions hook, memberlist, freeze) is wrapped into a token with its own hook and "freely transferable" semantics, while "**mint/redeem functions stay at KYC endpoints**". The economic exposure becomes bearer; the redemption right does not. Two tokens, one claim, two access regimes, and the second has no path to the obligor. `Aw` cannot express this, and note the interaction with prohibition **X19** ("restricted claim bridged via `Xf` into a representation with no destination-side `Aw`") — deRWA is not a bridge, so X19 does not literally fire, but it is the same hazard through a different door. **The vocabulary's prohibition is domain-specific where the hazard is not.** (The corpus attached this residue to Maple, where §5(g) of that section shows it does not apply; it applies here.)
2. **A per-pool, configurable message-verification quorum.** `setAdapters` with a confirmation threshold and recovery adapters means *each pool chooses its own cross-chain security budget*. `Xm` is a boolean; here it is a parameter chosen by the party whose assets are at risk, and two pools on the same protocol can have materially different security.
3. **Merkle-rooted execution policy.** `MerkleProofManager` restricts a strategist "by target contract, function selector, and **parameter values**", updated by replacing a single root. This is `Au` (delegated execution scope) in a form far more expressive than the symbol suggests — and the corpus does not record `Au` for Centrifuge at all.
4. **Compliance state with a time dimension.** Membership is a **valid-until timestamp**, so eligibility expires with no actor doing anything. `Aw` is a bit; this is a bit with a clock.
5. **A pause that is deliberately partial.** "the global pause halts all cross-chain messaging… **Local vault operations are not affected.**" `Gp` implies stopping the protocol; here the guardian can stop the nervous system while the limbs keep moving — which is a *choice*, and arguably the right one, but the vocabulary cannot record the scope of a pause.
6. **`Root` is immutable but ward-omnipotent.** The vocabulary would render this as `Up` plus `Tg`, losing the actual design: the *authority* contract cannot be upgraded, but it can rewrite permissions on everything else after 48 hours.
7. **REGISTER OF RECORD — answered, and differently from BUIDL and USYC.** Anemoy states: "[the token] **represents an ownership share in the Fund. It is prima facie evidence of ownership under BVI law in the same way as a share certificate would be.**" *Prima facie evidence* is precisely not the register — a share certificate is rebuttable evidence of an entry in a book kept elsewhere. So all three fund-wrapped instruments in this category place the chain **below** an off-chain register, but they do so through three different legal devices: BUIDL through a US transfer agent's books and records, USYC through a fund administrator's share registry and an explicit "digital twin" metaphor, and JTRSY through the BVI law of share certificates. **The vocabulary has one word (none) for three distinct legal relationships, and the differences determine what happens when the chain and the book disagree.**

### 6. DELTA

Corpus record: `At, Aw, Ep, Ex, Gp, Sh, Sv, Tg, Tr, Up, Wq, Xf, Xm`.

- **`Tr` — REFUTED. Remove it.** Zero occurrences of `waterfall`/`senior`/`junior`/`subordinat` in the repository; the only `tranche` hits are in a 2023 audit file, a test, and legacy migration spells. V3 has no seniority mechanism. See §5(e).
- **`Fz` — MISSING, and it is a clear omission.** V3 ships a `FreezeOnly` hook (deployed), a per-address freeze bit packed into the token's own storage slot, and `BalanceSheet.transferSharesFrom` → `ShareToken.authTransferFrom` for forced recovery, plus a deployed `TokenRecoverer` (`0x1E70530e…B4037`). Centrifuge has **more** explicit freeze-and-forced-transfer machinery than USYC, which the corpus does credit with `Fz`. Add it.
- **`Rd` — MISSING.** Redemption is a first-class protocol operation (async ERC-7540 redeem request → approval → revoke → fulfil → claim, with `RedemptionRestrictions` as a dedicated hook). It is conditional on issuer approval, which is exactly the qualification `Rd` needs everywhere in this category — but the right response is to add `Rd` with a conditionality note, not to omit it.
- **`Au` — MISSING.** `MerkleProofManager` per-strategist policy roots restricting target, selector and parameter values is delegated execution scope in the strongest sense the vocabulary contemplates.
- **`Ep` vs `Wq` — `Ep` is right and `Wq` is doubtful.** Centrifuge's exit is a **request/approve/fulfil batch cycle** through `BatchRequestManager`, in which the issuer approves a batch and sets its execution price. That is epoch-gated transition, not a FIFO queue with a liquidity trigger. The corpus records both; on the evidence `Ep` carries the mechanism and `Wq` is at best a loose description. Contrast Maple, where `Wq` is exactly right and `Ep` is wrong — the two protocols are mirror images on this axis, and a decomposition that gives both symbols to both loses that.
- **`Xm` — CONFIRMED and materially richer than the symbol.** Four adapters deployed (Chainlink, Axelar, two Wormhole), aggregated by `MultiAdapter`, with a **per-pool confirmation threshold**. Keep, but the marker should record that verification strength is a per-pool parameter.
- **`Xf` — CONFIRMED.** Share tokens move between spokes with the hook invoked on both legs ("Limit token bridging — Hook is invoked on outgoing and incoming cross-chain transfers"), which also means law L26 (destination-enforced eligibility) is satisfied by construction here.
- **`Tg` — CONFIRMED and unusually strong.** A 48-hour timelock on all privilege escalation through an **immutable** `Root`, with third-party signer verification on the Protocol Safe. This is the strongest control plane of the five and the decomposition should not treat it as equivalent to BUIDL's (an EOA) or Maple's (retroactively shortenable).
- **`Gp` — CONFIRMED but the scope needs a marker:** the pause halts messaging, not local vault operations.
- **`Sv` — CONFIRMED and load-bearing**, but the corpus's marker ("used for NAV Manager and asset-manager discretion") understates it: with `Tr` removed, `Sv` becomes the *only* symbol carrying loss allocation, because the sole way a loss reaches a holder is a manager-set price.
- **`At` — retained but equivocal**, exactly as with USYC: there is a continuous issuer-published price and no independent reserve attestation in the protocol.
- **Rank basis — verified at a primary source and it holds.** Centrifuge's own public GraphQL API on 2026-08-04 gives primary share tokens totalling **$1.613B** (JTRSY $881.34M + JAAA $684.79M + ACRDX $43.45M + SPXA $3.37M + HYB $0.10M + dust), against the corpus's DefiLlama figure of $1.629B **[AGGREGATOR]** — agreement within 1%. The corpus's characterisation ("largest tokenization protocol not operated by a single asset manager") is supported by the composition: five issuers, four of them third-party asset managers (Janus Henderson ×3, Anemoy/Apollo, NYLIM).
