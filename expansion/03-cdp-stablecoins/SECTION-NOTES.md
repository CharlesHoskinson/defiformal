## Lane narrative

**What the section introduction should say.** Every member issues a unit meant to hold a peg against backing that is volatile, held by someone else, and can become worth less than the unit. The corpus records a *single* residue for the category — no price-of-credit instrument — and that turns out to be wrong in shape rather than wrong in fact.

**The finding: the rate gap is four gaps.** There are four distinct rate instruments among the five protocols, and the corpus files one under the wrong heading:

1. a **deployed bounded-delegate rate setter** — an agent sets the borrow rate, the savings rate and the savings-reserve rate within min, max and step bounds, with no executive vote;
2. an administered rate keyed to **external benchmarks**, including a central-bank policy rate;
3. a **closed-loop controller on peg error**;
4. **borrower-chosen** rates, priced per position by the borrower — which is not an instrument the protocol holds at all.

**Why (1) matters beyond this category.** It is the yield lane's delegated allocation mandate in different dress — named agent, bounded quantity, cap, step limit, revocation — bounding a *rate* rather than an allocation. Two lanes, different categories, different evidence, same five-part schema. That is the run's first cross-lane convergence and the strongest argument for a level above the element.

**Also.** The control-plane register is a bigger gap here than the rate one. One protocol's core is BUSL-1.1 until 2027-09-01. Two recorded residues for one protocol are refuted outright — its liquidation is standard, not gas-only. One protocol has no public repository for its CDP core at all, so its entire control plane is UNKNOWN.
