# Stage 1 research — Perpetuals / derivatives (05-perpetuals)

Lane: Hyperliquid · ApeX Protocol (ApeX Omni) · Aster · Lighter · edgeX. All access dates **2026-08-04** unless stated.

**Note on source quality for this category.** This is the worst-sourced category in the atlas and the reason is structural, not accidental: four of the five venues run a matching engine that nobody outside the operator can read. Only **Lighter** publishes the artefact that decides its trades — its Plonky2 circuits are open and the deployed verifier can be regenerated from source. **Hyperliquid** ships a signed *binary* node under an Apache-2.0 repo that contains no node source; **Aster** states as policy that core chain contracts and RPC are closed "to protect proprietary matching engine algorithms"; **ApeX Omni** and **edgeX** publish SDKs and settlement addresses only. Consequently the docs-site tier (GitBook, which for all five exposes machine-readable `llms.txt` / `llms-full.txt` corpora, used heavily here) carries more weight than it should, and I have leaned on two independent adversarial sources to check it: **L2BEAT** project pages (for DA mode, exit windows, upgrade authority and contract addresses) and **zkSecurity**'s public report on Lighter's circuits. Where a docs claim had no second source I have marked it UNKNOWN rather than repeat it. Two of the five records in the corpus are materially stale on their single most important architectural fact (Aster and edgeX both changed trust model since the corpus text was written), so the DELTA sections here are load-bearing, not cosmetic.

## Hyperliquid

### WHAT IT DOES

A trader deposits USDC — bridged from Arbitrum, or held natively — and it is credited first to a single **cross-margin** balance that backs every perp and spot market at once; the trader may instead ring-fence a position in **isolated** margin. Positions are opened by sending limit or market orders to a central limit order book with tick and lot quantisation, matched in price–time priority, with a margin check run both when the order is placed and again for the resting side at each match. Initial margin is `position_size * mark_price / leverage`, and moving margin out requires `max(initial_margin_required, 0.1 * total_position_value)` to remain. Positions are marked against a mark price that is the median of three independent constructions and refreshed roughly every 3 seconds when validators publish oracle prices; unrealised PnL, margining, liquidation and TP/SL triggers all key off it. Funding is charged hourly as one-eighth of an 8-hour rate, `F = P + clamp(interest_rate − P, −0.0005, +0.0005)`, capped at 4%/hour, and — a detail that matters for reconstruction — the payment notional is computed at the **spot oracle price, not the mark price**. A margin breach is a two-stage object: when account equity falls below maintenance margin (half the initial margin at max leverage, so 1.25%–16.7% by tier) the engine sends market orders to close enough of the position to restore the requirement, and any surplus collateral stays with the trader; if equity instead falls below **two-thirds** of maintenance margin without the book absorbing it, a *backstop* liquidation transfers the trader's entire cross position set and cross margin to the liquidator vault. If account value goes negative anyway, auto-deleveraging ranks traders on the opposite side by `(mark_price / entry_price) * (notional_position / account_value)` and closes them against the distressed account at the previous mark price. A position therefore ends in one of four ways: a closing trade, a book liquidation, a backstop transfer, or ADL — plus market delisting.

### DESIGN

**Where the book lives, and who runs it — in-consensus.** HyperCore *is* the state machine: order books, margin, matching and liquidations are protocol logic, and the docs say so adversarially — "HyperCore does not rely on the crutch of off-chain order books." Consensus is **HyperBFT**, described as a HotStuff variant, over a delegated proof-of-stake validator set weighted by delegated HYPE. Two facts distinguish this from "a book in a smart contract": (a) the mempool and consensus logic are *order-book aware* — within a block, actions are sorted into three classes, first actions containing no GTC or IOC order, then cancels, then actions carrying at least one GTC or IOC, preserving proposed order within each class, which is a fairness rule enforced at the ordering layer rather than the execution layer; (b) throughput is quoted at ~200k orders/sec with median 0.2 s and p99 0.9 s response for nearby clients, with execution, not consensus, named as the binding constraint.

**Margin engine.** A perps clearinghouse holds margin state per address; deposits land in cross by default. Maintenance margin is tiered by notional: `maintenance_margin = notional_position_value * maintenance_margin_rate − maintenance_deduction`, with the deduction making the requirement continuous across tier boundaries. Mainnet tiers as of access: BTC 40× to $150M then 20×; ETH 25× to $100M then 15×; SOL 20× to $70M then 10×; a large mid-cap band at 10× to $20M then 5×; a smaller band at 10× to $3M then 5×. Liquidation price is published as `liq_price = price − side * margin_available / position_size / (1 − l * side)` with `l = 1/MAINTENANCE_LEVERAGE`.

**Mark and index — an endogenous oracle.** Validators publish spot oracle prices every 3 seconds, each computing a weighted median of Binance, OKX, Bybit, Kraken, KuCoin, Gate.io, MEXC and Hyperliquid spot mids with weights 3,2,2,1,1,1,1,1; assets whose primary liquidity is on Hyperliquid (HYPE) exclude external sources, assets whose primary liquidity is elsewhere (BTC) exclude Hyperliquid. The clearinghouse then takes the **stake-weighted median of the validators' submissions**. Mark price is the median of: (1) oracle price plus a 150-second EMA of (Hyperliquid mid − oracle), (2) the median of Hyperliquid best bid, best ask and last trade, (3) a weighted median of Binance/OKX/Bybit/Gate.io/MEXC *perp* mids at weights 3,2,2,1,1; when only two of the three exist, a 30-second EMA of (2) is added. The price is thus produced by the chain's own validator set, not fetched from an external publisher.

**Funding.** `premium = impact_price_difference / oracle_price`; for HIP-3 perps `premium = (0.5 * (impact_bid_px + impact_ask_px) / oracle_px) − 1`. Hourly settlement of 1/8 of the 8-hour rate; ±0.05% clamp on the interest-rate term; 4%/hour cap.

**Liquidation, ADL and the backstop.** Book liquidation first; backstop liquidation second, into the **liquidator vault, which is a component strategy of HLP** — and "the pnl stream from liquidations go entirely to the community through HLP." **HLP** (Hyperliquidity Provider) is a protocol vault that simultaneously (i) makes markets through multiple strategies, (ii) performs liquidations, (iii) **supplies USDC in Earn**, and (iv) accrues a share of trading fees; it is "fully community-owned," has a 4-day post-deposit lock-up, and unlike user vaults charges no fee or profit share. It is *not* a bond and is *not* slashable. ADL is the terminal backstop and carries an explicit invariant: "a user who has no open positions will not socialize any losses of the platform."

**Bridge.** USDC on Arbitrum. Deposits are credited when >2/3 of staking power has signed. Withdrawals debit the L1 balance immediately, then require 2/3 of staking power to sign an EVM transaction to the bridge; a dispute period follows during which the bridge can be **locked** if the withdrawal does not match L1 state, and cold-wallet signatures from 2/3 of the stake-weighted set are required to unlock. The validator set and its stake are mirrored on the bridge contract. Withdrawals cost 1 USDC on Hyperliquid rather than requiring Arbitrum ETH. Audited by Zellic.

**Control plane.** Delegated PoS: validator set rotates in epochs of 100k rounds (~90 minutes); 10k HYPE self-delegation required, locked one year, and dropping below it puts a validator into undelegate-only mode; commission may not be raised except to ≤1%; delegations have a 1-day lockup; staking→spot has a **7-day unstaking queue** with at most 5 pending withdrawals; jailing is by peer vote for poor performance and is self-cured; slashing is "reserved for provably malicious behavior such as double-signing blocks at the same round," and **there is currently no automatic slashing mechanism**. Multi-sig accounts are supported.

**Permissionless listing.** HIP-1 sells the right to deploy a spot ticker through a **31-hour Dutch auction** whose gas decays linearly from `initial_price` to 500 HYPE, resetting to 500 on a failed auction and otherwise starting at twice the last clearing price. HIP-3 sells the right to deploy a *perp DEX*: 500k HYPE staked on mainnet, maintained ≥183 days after deployment; the first three assets per DEX skip the auction, further assets go through a Dutch auction with HIP-1 hyperparameters **shared across all perp DEXs**; the deployer sets oracle prices, leverage limits and settlement, and configures a fee share of 0–300% (0–100% in growth mode); validators slash by stake-weighted vote — up to 100% for invalid state transitions or prolonged downtime, up to 50% for brief downtime, up to 20% for degradation. Permissionless spot **quote** assets require 200k HYPE staked, committed 3 years and slashable by validator vote; "aligned quote assets" (AQAv1/AQAv2) escalate this to 1M HYPE total and share ~90% of cost-adjusted reserve yield with the protocol. HIP-4 outcome markets exist. **Builder codes** let a third-party front-end attach a fee to order flow it originates: ≤0.1% on perps, ≤1% on spot, charged on-chain as part of fee logic, requiring an `ApproveBuilderFee` action from the user's main wallet, ≤10 active approvals, and ≥100 USDC in the builder's perps account.

**Vaults.** User vaults: leader takes a 10% profit share, must hold ≥5% of the vault at all times and cannot withdraw below it, pays a 10k USDC creation fee, 1-day depositor lockup, minimum 100 USDC seed. Vaults may trade validator-operated perps only — not spot, not HIP-3 perps. HLP: 4-day lockup, no fee.

**Risk controls not in the margin engine.** Open-interest caps scaled to liquidity that block new positions when hit, and a rule that orders must be placed within **1% of the oracle price**, with HLP exempt so it can keep quoting.

### WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

**Corpus flag 1 — where the book lives and who runs it: CONFIRMED, and it is the sharpest case in the lane.** `Ob` is true of Hyperliquid and of a book deployed as a smart contract on someone else's chain, and the difference is the entire protocol. It is worse than the corpus states, because Hyperliquid's ordering *rule* — cancels sorted ahead of aggressive orders within a block — is a consensus-layer property with no execution-layer analogue, so even a symbol for "in-consensus book" would not capture it.

**Corpus flag 2 — the perp tether: REFUTED for Hyperliquid.** `Pf` fits exactly: a hourly long/short transfer tethering perp to index. The flag is real for the category but not here.

Others found:
- **Endogenous oracle.** The chain's own validator set *is* the price publisher, by stake-weighted median of stake-weighted medians. `Ex` names an external data oracle and cannot distinguish "the chain reports the price" from "Chainlink reports the price," which have opposite trust arguments. (This matches the corpus and I confirm it.)
- **A single capital pool occupying four roles at once.** HLP is maker, liquidator of last resort, USDC lender in Earn, and fee claimant. `Sh` names only the pro-rata claim; `Bs` names first-loss capital but demands slashability. Nothing composes "the loss absorber is also the price maker."
- **Slashable bonds that secure *listing*, not solvency.** HIP-3 (500k HYPE), quote assets (200k), aligned quote assets (1M) are genuinely slashable stakes — but they underwrite oracle honesty, uptime and asset quality, not trader losses. `Bs` is defined against a loss waterfall and cannot sit here.
- **Permissionless market creation priced by descending auction**, with auction hyperparameters *shared across all deployed DEXs* — a coupling between independent venues with no symbol.
- **Builder codes** — a third party attaching a fee to order flow it originated, enforced in the fee logic of the matching engine.
- **Leader/follower vaults with a mandatory skin-in-the-game floor** (≥5%, non-withdrawable) — a solvency-style constraint applied to a *manager*, not a borrower.
- **Order-placement bands and OI caps** — risk controls that act on the order, not on the account. `Ct` is an account test.
- **Jailing** — a liveness penalty that is not slashing and not exclusion.
- **Revenue-share alignment of a quote asset issuer with the venue** (AQAv2's ~90% reserve-yield share) — an economic relation between a stablecoin and an exchange, unnamed.
- **Margin scope** (cross / isolated / sub-account / vault) as an attribute of the collateral-threshold test.

### DELTA

The corpus record for Hyperliquid is the most accurate of the five. Discrepancies:

1. **HLP is broader than the record says.** The corpus residue lists HLP as "(a) the primary maker, (b) the liquidator of last resort, and (c) a pro-rata LP claim." It is also (d) a USDC lender into Earn and (e) a fee accrual claim — "provides liquidity … through multiple market making strategies, performs liquidations, supplies USDC in Earn, and accrues a portion of trading fees" (https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/protocol-vaults.md, 2026-08-04). A residue about role-multiplicity should count five roles, not three.
2. **The `Bs` marker's "the slashing half is absent" is right about HLP and wrong about the protocol.** Hyperliquid *does* have slashable bonded capital — HIP-3 deployers (500k HYPE, slashable up to 100% by stake-weighted validator vote) and quote-asset deployers (200k / 1M HYPE, "subject to slashing based on validator vote") — it just does not sit in front of the loss waterfall (https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals.md and .../llms-full.txt, 2026-08-04). The residue should be "slashable capital exists but secures listing quality, not solvency," which is a stronger finding.
3. **The `Vl` marker overstates slashing.** It cites "HYPE staking, delegation, jailing and validator rotation." Correct — but the docs state "There is currently no automatic slashing implemented" for consensus faults (https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/staking.md, 2026-08-04). Validator misbehaviour at the consensus layer is socially, not mechanically, punished; HIP-3 misbehaviour *is* mechanically punished. The symbol elides two different regimes.
4. **HIP-4 outcome markets** exist and are absent from the record (https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips.md, 2026-08-04). Whether they belong in this category or in prediction markets is a scoping question, but the record shows no awareness of them.
5. **Two risk controls are missing from the residue**: OI caps and the 1%-of-oracle order band with an HLP exemption (https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/risks.md, 2026-08-04). The corpus lists OI caps as a category-level gap but not against Hyperliquid specifically, where they are documented.

## ApeX Protocol (ApeX Omni)

### WHAT IT DOES

A trader deposits into an Omni **funding** account on any supported chain and transfers into a **Perps** or **Spot** account. Collateral is USDT plus eight cross-collateral tokens — USDC, WBTC, WETH, ETH, cmETH, mETH, cbBTC, USDe — each valued as `Margin Balance = Amount × Index Price × Collateral Ratio` with tiered haircuts and per-asset supply caps (USDC: 99% on the first 5,000,000, 97.5% thereafter, 20,000,000 cap; USDT 100% and uncapped). Margin is **cross only**: "All positions share a single unified margin pool backed by your total account equity in USDT," and there is no isolated mode. Positions are opened against a price–time-priority CLOB (maker 2 bps / taker 5 bps at tier 1, falling to 0 bps maker / 2.5 bps taker at the top tier), with up to 100× on majors. `Initial Margin = Position Size × Entry Price × IMR`; `Maintenance Margin = Position Size × Mark Price × MMR`. Mark price is `Median(Price1, Price2, Last Price)` where `Price1 = Index × [1 + Last Funding Rate × (Time to next funding / 8)]` and `Price2 = Index + 5-minute MA of ((Bid1+Ask1)/2 − Index)`, and mark — not last — drives liquidations, unrealised PnL, bankruptcy price and margin requirements. Funding settles **every hour on the hour UTC**: `F = P + clamp(I − P, +0.05%, −0.05%)`, `Funding Fee = Position Size × Index Price × F`, longs pay shorts when positive, with the hourly rate capped at ±0.05% and the excess absorbed by the platform rather than passed on. A margin breach is a single trigger: when **Margin Ratio (total maintenance margin ÷ total equity) reaches or exceeds 100%**, the liquidation engine closes positions in order of risk severity at available market prices. If a liquidated position cannot be closed at or above its bankruptcy price, **ADL** closes profitable opposite-side positions at the bankruptcy price, ranked by leveraged return (`PnL% × account margin rate` for winners, `PnL ÷ margin rate` for losers), surfaced to users as a ranking they can manage. Positions end by close, liquidation, ADL, or delisting.

### DESIGN

**Where the book lives, and who runs it — UNKNOWN in primary sources, and that is itself the finding.** ApeX's documentation never states where the matching engine runs. What *is* documented: ApeX Omni is "a decentralized exchange (DEX) built on Layer 2 (L2) using ZK-rollup technology provided by zkLink.X"; the architecture is "modular, intent-centric" across an Access Layer, Application Layer and Blockchain Layer, with the execution layer "leverag[ing] the zkLink engine" and a settlement layer that "provides proof verification and is where the transactions of ApeX applications are finalized and recorded." The API is a CEX-shaped REST/WebSocket surface with HMAC-SHA256 API-key authentication plus a second **`ZkKeys` signature — a "ZkLink L2 signature of order/transfer parameters (using zk seeds)" required on orders, transfers and withdrawals**. That combination (operator-hosted API, user-signed L2 orders, ZK-proven settlement of balances) is the signature of an operator-run engine with proven settlement, but I could not find ApeX asserting it, so I record the inference and mark the primary claim UNKNOWN.

**Rollup stack.** zkLink X, described by its own docs as "aggregated rollup infrastructure" with a "trading-specific-zkVM," two settlement schemes (**Nexus** and **Origin** — Nexus synchronising multi-chain state via zk-proof and Ethereum, Origin via zk-proof and a Light Oracle Network), and a *menu* of DA layers: Ethereum, Celestia, EigenDA, Polygon Avail, or a zkLink-organised DAC. **Which of these ApeX Omni selects is UNKNOWN** — neither ApeX's nor zkLink's docs state ApeX's configuration. Sequencing on zkLink X is described as delegable to third parties (Espresso, Astria, Fairblock); whether ApeX uses any of them is UNKNOWN. ApeX's own blog states proofs are "first committed on the host chain, which sends sync hashes through secure messaging channels," with batching at app-rollup and L2 levels before Ethereum settlement.

**Chains.** Deposits from Ethereum, BNB Chain, Arbitrum, Base, Mantle and Solana are cited; the force-withdrawal page enumerates ApeX's zkLink contract per chain: BNB `0xb8d9f005654b7b127b34dae8f973ba729ca3a2d9`, Ethereum `0x35D173cdfE4d484BC5985fDa55FABad5892c7B82`, Arbitrum `0x3169844a120c0f517b4eb4a750c08d8518c8466a`, Base `0xee7981c4642de8d19aed11da3bac59277dfd59d7`, Mantle `0x3c7c0ebfcd5786ef48df5ed127cddeb806db976c`.

**Escape hatch.** Two steps: submit a **forced exit** on zkLink L2 per zkLink's documented procedure, wait for on-chain confirmation, then claim on L1 by interacting directly with the ApeX zkLink contract on the chosen chain. **No deadline or exit-window duration is documented** — UNKNOWN, and materially so, since the deadline is the whole censorship-resistance argument.

**Backstop — and a correction to the standard story.** The string "insurance fund" does not occur anywhere in ApeX's documentation corpus (`llms-full.txt`, 383 KB, searched). What exists instead is a **Protocol Vault branded the "Insurance Vault"**: users deposit USDT, receive shares at NAV, and earn **100% of daily liquidation fees from all Omni Perp trades**, credited at 08:05 UTC against the previous day's 08:00 cutoff, with no lock-up and redemption at any time. The docs describe it as a product with a "stable, positive, and protocol-guaranteed" return profile *and* say "The Vault acts as a backstop for the protocol." So the first-loss layer here is **user-supplied capital rented with liquidation-fee income**, not protocol-owned reserve — the inverse of what the corpus assumes. ADL is the documented mechanism for shortfalls after liquidation, and the ADL page makes no reference to any fund absorbing losses first.

**Other vaults.** "Omni Vaults (Community Vaults)": traders create and manage vaults, attract external capital, and earn a performance fee (e.g. 10% of profits).

**Adjacent venues inside one account.** TradFi perpetuals (with a separately scoped API key), tokenized US stocks and Pre-IPO spot, and a **Prediction Market that integrates Polymarket order books directly into the ApeX interface, funded from the user's Omni account and settled in Polymarket USD (PUSD)** — i.e. ApeX surfaces a third party's book as a first-class venue inside its own margin system.

**Control plane.** APEX / esAPEX staking with revenue-sharing incentives, and a VIP program in which fee tier advances on the *stronger* of 14-day volume or staked amount. **Upgrade authority, timelocks, guardians and pause powers are UNKNOWN** — ApeX's docs do not describe them, and the contracts in question are zkLink's, not ApeX's. The FAQ answer on safety says only that "ApeX Omni is built on zkLink X — a fully audited infrastructure" and points elsewhere; **no auditor is named for ApeX Omni itself.**

**Legacy.** ApeX Pro, the StarkEx-based predecessor, is **discontinued**; its documentation is retained in a section explicitly labelled "(Discontinued)," and L2BEAT carries it as an **archived** project (~$1.42M value secured). The `/scaling/projects/apex` page 404s on direct fetch, consistent with archival.

## Aster

### WHAT IT DOES

A trader bridges collateral in from BNB Chain, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Solana or SUI through Aster's deposit-bridge contracts; the deposit is credited once **3 of 4 validator nodes** verify it, at zero deposit fee, in 1–5 minutes. Collateral is USDT plus, distinctively, **yield-bearing instruments** — asBNB (a BNB liquid-staking receipt), USDF / asUSDF (a yield-bearing stablecoin), asBTC, asCAKE — so the margin itself is a position in another protocol. Deposits land in the **cross-margin** balance by default; isolated margin is available per position, and multi-asset mode lets several tokens back one book. Positions are opened either on a price–time-priority **CLOB** (Perpetuals / Spot), or on one of two simplified oracle-priced venues — **Shield Mode** (AMM-style, privacy-first, high leverage) and **1001x** (one-click, MEV-resistant, up to 1001×) — both of which sit against the ALP pool rather than against another trader. `Initial margin = (Position size × Mark price) / Leverage`; maintenance margin is tiered by position size. Mark price is the median of `Price index × (1 + Funding rate × (hours to next funding / 8))`, `Price index + 5-minute MA of ((Bid1+Ask1)/2 − Price index)`, and the contract price, with guards: exchanges deviating >5% from the median are down-weighted, feeds staler than 3 seconds are dropped, and a contract price >5% from mark is replaced if not refreshed within 5 seconds. Funding is `F = [P + clamp(interest − P, 0.05%, −0.05%)] / (8/N)` with a default 8-hour interval, interest 0.01% (0% for BNBUSDT), premium index `[max(0, impact bid − index) − max(0, index − impact ask)] / index` recomputed every 5 seconds, and Aster reserves the right to change floor, cap and interval under stress. A margin breach fires when `Initial Collateral + Realized PnL + Unrealized PnL < Maintenance margin`, evaluated against the **stake-weighted median oracle price**; an IOC attempt runs first, a **liquidation fee ("Insurance Clearance Fee")** is taken to the insurance fund, and any residue is closed at the **bankruptcy price and handed to the insurance fund**, which covers the shortfall where it can; if it cannot, **ADL** reduces profitable opposite positions ranked by `PnL% × effective leverage` (or `PnL% ÷ effective leverage` when losing), with a four-bar `LeveragePnLQuantile` indicator exposing the queue position. A position ends by close, liquidation, insurance-fund takeover at bankruptcy price, ADL, or delisting.

### DESIGN

**Where the book lives, and who runs it — in-consensus, on a permissioned, closed-source L1.** Aster Chain is Aster's own Layer 1, launched **16 March 2026**, purpose-built for derivatives: "All order matching and trading logic are ultimately processed and stored **onchain**." Consensus is **PoSA (Proof-of-Staked Authority)** — PoS economics over PoA validator reputation — targeting 50 ms blocks, up to 100,000 TPS and zero gas. The **Clearinghouse** is "the core execution component of Aster Chain," managing per-trader margin balances and open positions, and **each perpetual market operates with an independent order book**. Two facts make this a *fourth* trust model, distinct from Hyperliquid's: (a) "During Phase 1, external validator participation is not supported" — the validator set is closed, with observer nodes and staking validators promised later; (b) "Core chain contracts and RPC infrastructure will not be open-sourced at the moment," explicitly in order to "protect proprietary matching engine algorithms." So: an in-consensus order book, executed by a permissioned validator set, running closed-source software. Data storage is hybrid — "Key trading and account events are recorded onchain, while certain operations remain offchain for privacy protection and system efficiency."

**Oracle — endogenous, like Hyperliquid's, but over a wider panel.** "Each validator calculates a weighted median spot price using data from 14 major exchanges": Binance, HTX, HitBTC, Gate.io, Poloniex, OKX, KuCoin, AscendEX, MEXC, Bitfinex, Coinbase, Bitstamp, Kraken, Bybit. These feed funding, mark price and TP/SL triggers, and **"For liquidation, the system uses the median oracle price weighted by validator stake."** The separate Price Index used in mark-price construction is described as a volume-weighted average over major spot exchanges (Binance, HTX, Kraken and others). The **1001x** venue uses a different pipeline: Pyth as the anchor, cross-verified against Chainlink by the smart contract at trade time, Binance Oracle as fallback where Pyth lacks a pair, with a **circuit breaker on >1% divergence**.

**Privacy — the differentiator, and it is now cryptographic.** Account Privacy, when enabled, hides balances and open positions behind two mechanisms: **ZK-Verifiable Encrypted Orders** ("Every order placed under Account Privacy is encrypted and verifiable using Zero-Knowledge (ZK) cryptography… The underlying order data is never exposed onchain in plaintext"), and a **Stealth Address Mechanism** generating a fresh one-time address per trade so that observers cannot correlate activity. Verification is by **viewer pass** — selective disclosure of one's own history, or of another trader's if the pass is supplied. Crucially: **"Hidden orders always require ZK verification regardless of whether Account Privacy is on or off,"** and internal transfers to other users are blocked while Account Privacy is active, for stated regulatory reasons. On the perp CLOB, a **Hidden Order** is a limit order that reveals neither size nor presence to the public book yet "shar[es] liquidity with other orders"; positions opened from hidden orders become **Hidden Positions**, excluded from all public data and portfolio analytics. The perp-product page for hidden orders describes no cryptography, so there is a documented seam between the chain-level ZK story and the product-level order-privacy story; I record both.

**Counterparty on the pool venues.** "ALP holders act as the market-maker counterparty for perp trades on the platform." ALP accrues trading PnL on Simple Mode flow, protocol trading fees, funding fees, liquidation profits and platform fees directly into NAV rather than distributing them; NAV "moves with the pool's overall profit and loss," i.e. ALP is structurally short aggregate trader profit. ALP is mintable on BNB Chain and Arbitrum. In **Shield Mode** funding is still a long/short transfer — "Aster will collect a Funding fee rate to balance the Long and Short ratios," computed from the long/short open-interest imbalance, clamped between a floor and cap, generated hourly. Shield-mode liquidation triggers on **last price** against a liquidation price derived from margin, leverage, entry, funding and a **liquidation loss rate that varies by pair and leverage band** (BTC/ETH: 75% at 1–200×, 70% at 201–500×, 65% at 501–1001×; EURUSDT 60% at 20–200×). At 1001× there is **no opening fee, no margin top-up, a dynamic PnL-based closing fee, and net ROI capped at roughly 300%**.

**Bridge.** Validator-signed and on-chain-verifiable; deposits on 3-of-4, withdrawals batched and confirmed on 2-of-3, ~30 seconds typical, gas charged to cover validator operating costs, minimum fee as low as 1 USDT. Deposit-bridge contracts: BNB `0x128463A60784c4D3f46c23Af3f65Ed859Ba87974`, Ethereum `0x604DD02d620633Ae427888d41bfd15e38483736E`, Solana `EhUtRgu9iEbZXXRpEvDj6n1wnQRjMi2SERDo3c6bmN2c`, Arbitrum `0x9E36CB86a159d479cEd94Fa05036f235Ac40E1d5`, SUI `0x16c5df59874dd4f12d8ea22859e8430064edd1b9ff330050a5ecdb5222003c3e`.

**Control plane.** $ASTER is staked to validators with a lock period; rewards split into a Base APY driven by the validator's processed-transaction share (`Validator Reward = Base APY Pool × Validator Share`) and Loyalty Rewards weighted by lock length via **veASTER**; Account Privacy must be **off** to stake. Listing is governed by **Listing Vote**: any active validator holding ≥20M staked $ASTER may propose, and passage requires a **double majority** — >50% of stake *and* >50% of validators. The Aster Open Standards formalise this: **AOS-1 (spot listing) live 25 June 2026**, AOS-2 (perpetual listing) announced. No timelock, guardian, pause authority or upgrade path for the chain is documented — **UNKNOWN**. An early-exit mechanism exists for staking. A testnet runs at `asterdex-testnet.com` on BNB Chain Testnet with faucet tokens and no user-initiated deposits or withdrawals.

**Legacy.** Aster is the March-2025 rebrand of **Astherus + APX**; an "AstherusEX Orderbook Perp Guide" survives in the docs, and APX→ASTER token exchange machinery is documented. Aster Chain Beta went live for selected traders in June 2025; mainnet 16 March 2026.

## Lighter

### WHAT IT DOES

A trader deposits USDC into the Lighter rollup contract on Ethereum (or USDG on the parallel "Lighter on Robinhood" deployment) and receives a **Main Account**, from which **Sub-Accounts** and **Public Pools** derive. Beyond the base asset, ETH — and, on the Robinhood deployment, SPY and USO — may be posted as collateral under a three-parameter haircut: **LTV** (50%) sets margin contribution, **LT** (75%) the liquidation threshold, **LF** (95%) the minimum value received per unit at liquidation; base collateral is always valued at 100% with no liquidation fee and no supply cap. Total account value is `TAV(USDC) = PortfolioBalance(USDC) + Σ_j LTV_j × MarginBalance_j × IndexPrice_j`, and `Total Account Value = Collateral + Unrealized PnL` with `Unrealized PnL := Σ (markPrice_i − avgEntryPrice_i) × position_i`; funding payments are applied immediately to realised PnL. Orders are submitted through API servers, ordered by a sequencer, and matched under **price–time priority that is proven in-circuit**; standard accounts pay **zero maker and taker fees** (at 300 ms taker / 200 ms maker latency), while Premium accounts buy lower latency at 0.4–2.8 bps scaling down with staked LIT and Plus accounts pay a flat 0.5 bps. Mark price is the median of an **impact price** (the average execution price of a $500 market buy and a $500 market sell, averaged), an **index-plus-liquidity-premium** smoothed by an 8-minute EMA and capped at ±0.5% of index, and the median mark across major CEXs; the index price comes from a combination of **Chainlink, Stork and Pyth**. Funding settles hourly: a per-minute premium `premium_t = [max(0, ImpactBid_t − index_t) − max(0, index_t − ImpactAsk_t)] / index_t` is sampled 60 times and time-weighted, then `smallClampedPremium = premium + clamp(InterestRate − premium, ±SmallClamp)` and `fundingRate = clamp(smallClampedPremium, ±BigClamp) / 8` with SmallClamp 0.05%, BigClamp 4%, InterestRate 0.01%; the account payment is `funding = (−1) × position × index × fundingRate`. A margin breach walks a **three-rung ladder** with `C_i < M_i < I_i` (close-out < maintenance < initial): below initial is *pre-liquidation*, below maintenance is *partial liquidation* against the book (with up to a 1% liquidation fee routed to the LLP when the fill beats the zero price), and below close-out is *full liquidation*; when the LLP cannot absorb a position without falling under its own initial-margin requirement, that position is **auto-deleveraged instead of assumed**, and ADL matches bankrupt positions against opposite-side accounts whose zero prices align, ranked by leverage and unrealised PnL. Exit is by close, liquidation, ADL, an ordinary withdrawal, a **forced priority transaction posted on Ethereum**, or — if the sequencer misses the deadline — **desert mode**, in which the state freezes and every user withdraws directly against the last settled root, all open positions settled at the latest mark price.

### DESIGN

**Where the book lives, and who runs it — a *proven* sequencer, which is a genuinely different object.** The sequencer "consumes user transactions from the mempool and executes them sequentially, structuring them into blocks," but its only real power is ordering: "Since transaction execution and order matching are verified by the Prover, all valid state transitions can be deterministically inferred from the transaction order and oracle data." The whitepaper is explicit about what this buys, and names the alternative it rejects: in "existing zk-rollup architectures for order book systems, where matching occurs directly at the Sequencer layer and proofs merely confirm that two orders intersect… the Sequencer could arbitrarily reorder or favor trades, compromising fairness and market neutrality." That is precisely the design ApeX and edgeX run.

**How price–time priority is proven.** The **Order Book Tree** encodes priority into the leaf *index*: an ask with price `p` and nonce `o` sits at `I = p × 2^O + o`; a bid at `I = p × 2^O + (2^O − 1 − o)` (the nonce inverted). Therefore `index_i < index_j` **iff** `price_i < price_j` or (`price_i = price_j` and `nonce_i < nonce_j`) for asks, with the dual for bids — priority is a *coordinate*, not a rule applied to a coordinate. Bids occupy the left of the tree with the best bid rightmost in that group; asks the right with the best ask leftmost. Internal nodes carry `AskSizeSum`, `BidSizeSum`, `AskQuoteSum`, `BidQuoteSum` as sums over their subtrees, composed by simple addition from children, and the node hash is `Hash(left, right, InternalData)` rather than `Hash(left, right)` — merkleised with **Poseidon2**. Order insertion proves *non-crossing* by walking root-to-leaf and summing right-subtree bid sizes wherever the path descends left: `crossingSize = Σ (BidSizeSum_{L_i} − BidSizeSum_{L_{i−1}}) × (1 − I_{i−1})`, and requires `crossingSize = 0`. Trade execution proves the maker leaf supplied is the **highest-priority** crossing order by recomputing `crossingSize` on that leaf's path and requiring it to be zero. All operations — existence, insertion, removal, quote retrieval — are Θ(log₂N), against Θ(N) for a linked list and O(N log₂N) for a Merkle-embedded linked list. This is the mechanism the corpus says the vocabulary cannot name, stated exactly.

**Batch structure and circuits.** Transaction → **Block** (a *pre-execution* phase plus consecutive transactions) → **Segment** (parallelisation unit) → **Batch**. Pre-execution applies funding (accumulating funding-rate × mark-price into a **Funding Prefix Sum**), updates index and mark prices, and records the premium. Multi-cycle operations (a taker order producing several trades, cancel-all, grouped orders, tied SL/TP triggers, reduce-only cancellation on position-sign change) are driven by an **Instruction Stack**; the next user-signed transaction executes only when the stack empties. Block proofs aggregate linearly into segment proofs, segments into a batch proof.

**Proof stack.** Plonky2 — "a circuit arithmetization based on TurboPlonk over Goldilocks field" with FRI in place of KZG — producing a STARK that is wrapped into a **gnark PLONK proof over BN254** for on-chain verification, using the **Aztec Ignition** (2019, 176 participants) trusted setup for the KZG commitments. Deployed verifiers: **ZkLighterVerifier `0xac3Ce44B6ff4E402858C99D5699ff63131572BaA`** (implementation `0xEd0a60EDaCd8c9B1221d2D9F753b6dF09Ea3baaa`), **DesertVerifier `0x866418061d4C1168e1c8E8f6facE79675395E008`**, plus a separate verifier for the Robinhood deployment. The main rollup contract is **ZKLighter `0x3B4D794a66304F130a4Db8F2551B0070dfCf5ca7`**, with logic split across two implementations for code-size reasons (`AdditionalZKLighter`), an **UpgradeGatekeeper `0x94da8A995D0D82Ef0fE7E509C6D76c22603B6f67`**, and **Governance `0xa464DA0B43f80EE3FfC4795cbbFC78472b5c81A1`**.

**Data availability — hybrid, sized to the escape hatch.** "Lighter executes tens of thousands of user operations per second… To overcome this scalability bottleneck, the Lighter Protocol employs a hybrid data availability model, posting only the minimal data required for a secure Escape Hatch while omitting redundant details." Concretely: an **Account Delta Tree** is initialised empty each batch, receives every update also applied to the **Public Account Tree**, then is serialised, compressed and posted to Ethereum in a **blob-carrying transaction**, together with market data (funding rates, mark prices). Reading every batch's blobs in order and applying them to an empty Public Account Tree reconstructs it exactly. Two circuits bind this: a **Blob Polynomial Equivalence Circuit** evaluating the claimed blob polynomial at the same random point Ethereum's point-evaluation precompile uses, and **Account Delta Traversal Circuits** evaluating the serialised execution public data at that point — random points derived by **Fiat–Shamir**. L2BEAT classifies Lighter as a **ZK Rollup** with Ethereum (blobs) as DA layer, badged "Application-specific chain," at **Stage 0**.

**Censorship resistance and the escape hatch.** Two transaction classes: **rollup transactions** (everything after deposit, queued via API servers, no fee) and **priority transactions** (queued directly through the Ethereum contracts). Forceable types per L2BEAT: deposits, withdrawals, **order creation, order cancellation**, and burning of pool shares — note that forcing an *order* is possible here, unlike a StarkEx forced trade which needs an out-of-band counterparty. If the sequencer misses the deadline (**14 days**), the system permissionlessly enters **desert mode**: further commitments and verifications are disabled, the state freezes, and users prove asset ownership, open-position PnL and public-pool share values against the frozen root using only blob data, and withdraw. "When the Escape Hatch is triggered, all open positions are assumed to be settled at the latest mark price."

**Accounts.** Four types: **Main Account** (one per L1 address), **Sub-Account** (isolated, same L1 address), **Public Pool** (a sub-account others buy into; can trade but cannot transfer or withdraw its holdings), and **Insurance Fund** — "a specialized type of Public Pool that both participates in market making and provides backstop liquidity when order book liquidity is insufficient to liquidate unhealthy accounts," run by an **Insurance Fund Operator**. Public pool operators are protocol-whitelisted, set an operator fee and a **Minimum Operator Share** (e.g. 10%, so 10,000 USDC of operator capital admits up to 90,000 of depositors), and there are **no lockups**. The user-facing insurance fund is the **LLP**, access to which is gated by staked LIT at a **10 USDC of deposit per 1 LIT staked** ratio.

**Token and control plane.** LIT: fee discounts by staking tier, LLP access, a fixed 6% staking APR, a 3-day unstaking lockup, and daily 24-hour-TWAP buybacks funded from trading-fee revenue. Governance per L2BEAT: "Regular upgrades are initiated by the 'network governor' and executed with a **21d delay**. The 'security council' is allowed to reduce the upgrade delay to zero in case of an emergency. The security council does not currently satisfy the Stage 1 requirements. The network governor also retains the ability to add or remove validators." Multisigs: `0x92b12c9d85BF7bd2EF5d2F53F4cd4Ce0BE432045` and `0x97A90Ec950B6BCd9B190b566525B2Bb92A2C03a2`.

**The weak seam.** L2BEAT: "Lighter uses a combination of oracles to determine index prices, with Stork as the primary source. **External signatures are currently not verified and the sequencer must be trusted to truthfully report data.**" So the matching *rule* is proven while the price *input* is trusted — a precise split that the protocol's own marketing does not foreground.

**Product surface.** Perpetuals plus RWAs (equity indices, pre-IPO markets, a futures-contract price-rolling mechanism), prelaunch markets, and **perpRFQ** — a size-seeking RFQ signalled through the order book, with market makers responding within 10 seconds and a 2-minute execution window. A second sovereign deployment, **"Lighter on Robinhood,"** runs the same stack with USDG as base collateral and SPY/USO as margin assets.

## edgeX

### WHAT IT DOES

A trader deposits USDT — from Ethereum mainnet (~15 confirmations, ~5 minutes) or from a non-Ethereum chain, in which case "edgeX transfers an equivalent amount of assets to the user's L2 account through the corresponding asset pool on the respective chain," with rebalancing latency of up to 2 hours for single deposits over $300K — or onboards by email through an MPC wallet, in which case a fresh address is created and funded over **Arbitrum**. Collateral is USDT; margin and PnL are denominated in USDT. The account is **cross-margined**, "allowing collateral to be shared among all positions within a single trading account," with isolation obtained by using separate **sub-accounts**; sub-account volume aggregates into the main account's fee tier. Positions are opened on a central limit order book. Risk parameters are per-contract **risk tiers** exposed through the metadata API: for BTCUSDT, tier 1 covers notional up to $50,000 at 100× with a maintenance-margin rate of **0.5%**, rising through 22 tiers to a top tier at 6× and **10.5%**. Two prices do different jobs: the **Index Price** — "the weighted average of prices collected from multiple major crypto exchanges, including Binance, OKX, Bybit, and Coinbase," with weights readjusted every four hours — drives funding; the **Oracle Price**, "sourced from the independent provider Stork," drives **margin requirements and liquidation prices**. Funding is exchanged hourly between longs and shorts: `F = P + clamp(I − P, 0.05%, −0.05%)` with `P = [max(0, ImpactBid − Index) − max(0, Index − ImpactAsk)] / Index`; positive rate means longs pay shorts. A margin breach, in cross mode, occurs "when the available balance reaches zero and the position margin drops to the maintenance margin level," at which point liquidation follows. **What happens after that is undocumented** — see DESIGN. Positions end by close, TP/SL, or liquidation.

### DESIGN

**Where the book lives, and who runs it — an operator-run off-chain matching engine, settling to a rollup whose stack changed underneath the product.** This is the important fact about edgeX and it is not in the corpus.

- **V1 (legacy).** "Powered by the StarkEx Layer 2 engine." Trades execute on edgeX, are batched by the StarkEx service, sent to the **SHARP** shared prover for STARK proofs, verified on Ethereum, and the state root is submitted to the StarkEx contract, which accepts it only against a validated proof. Throughput "when scale up to 10 shards" is quoted at 20,000 tps. L2BEAT confirms the configuration and grades it: badges **CairoVM**, **Data Availability Committee**, **StarkEx**, **Uses SHARP**; DA mode is **validium** — "Proof construction relies fully on data that is NOT published onchain.
