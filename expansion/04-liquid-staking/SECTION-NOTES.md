## Lane narrative

**What the section introduction should say.** The category turns a locked, slashable, operationally demanding consensus position into a transferable claim someone else operates. The corpus said one candidate symbol, `Vl`, stood for at least five separable mechanisms. Stage 3 found something stronger and worse.

**The finding: the symbol drops out of all five constructions.** Written as obligations, every obligation `Vl` would discharge is either residue or does not arise — so **no member of the category it supposedly defines carries it**. A candidate element that none of its own category carries is not a candidate; it is a category label mistaken for a mechanism.

| protocol | of the five sub-mechanisms |
|---|---|
| Lido | 5 of 5, **plus a sixth** the corpus never named |
| ether.fi | 5 of 5, in five separate source files |
| EigenLayer | 4 of 5; deposit front-running defence **closed by construction** |
| Babylon | 3 of 5; allocation and front-running closed by construction |
| WBETH | **0 of 5 — inapplicable, not approximate** |

**Three corpus claims are false rather than stale.** One protocol's unique-stake design *prevents* the same collateral backing mutually unaware slashing laws — the opposite of what the record implies. Another's tranche structure is in `src/archive/`. And the custodial receipt has a real on-chain permissionless mint and an on-chain withdrawal queue with a rate-limited oracle, not an admin key — established from verified bytecode alone, there being no repository or specification.

**Note for the paper.** This category is the second independent sighting of *discharge by construction*, after options.
