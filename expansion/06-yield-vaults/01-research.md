## Pendle

### WHAT IT DOES

A depositor brings a yield-bearing token — wstETH, sUSDe, aTokens, GLP, and so on — and wraps it in a **Standardized Yield (SY)** token, a uniform interface over an external protocol's yield source. SY can then be split, in *asset* units, into a **Principal Token (PT)** and a **Yield Token (YT)** for a fixed maturity date: `amountPY = SYUtils.syToAsset(indexCurrent, amountSy)`. PT is a zero-coupon claim redeemable 1:1 for the accounting asset at maturity and nothing before it; YT is the claim on all variable yield and all external reward tokens accruing to that SY between now and maturity, and its value trends to zero by construction as the remaining accrual window shrinks. The yield a depositor actually receives comes from one of four structurally different places: (1) buying PT below par and redeeming at par, which is a *fixed* rate locked at purchase; (2) holding YT, which is the *variable* SY interest plus SY reward tokens, both net of a 5% protocol fee; (3) providing liquidity to a PT/SY market and earning swap fees, of which LPs retain 20%; (4) PENDLE emissions routed to that market by the gauge. Nobody exercises discretion over where a depositor's capital goes — an SY wrapper is a passive adapter over exactly one named external protocol, and the depositor chooses the maturity and the market. What *is* discretionary is where **emissions** go, which is voted. Exit before maturity means selling PT or YT into the AMM or the limit-order book, or burning PT and YT together back to SY (`_redeemPY` requires both legs pre-expiry). At maturity PT redeems alone; YT's accrual freezes at a snapshot index, retains only already-accrued interest, and all subsequent incoming rewards on that expired YT are swept to the treasury.

### DESIGN

**Modules.** `IStandardizedYield` (SY), `PendlePrincipalToken`, `PendleYieldToken`, `InterestManagerYT`, `PendleYieldContractFactoryUpg`, `PendleMarketV7` + `PendleMarketFactoryV7Upg`, `MarketMathCore`, `OracleLib`, `PendleGauge`, `PendleGaugeControllerUpg`, `VotingEscrowPendleMainchain`, `PendleRouterV4` (a facet router, not an EIP-1967 proxy), `PendleLimitRouter`, `PendleGovernanceProxy`, `StakedPendle` (sPENDLE). Note the version numbering: the live market factory is **V7**, not the "V3" that older material names, and the deployments manifest key `marketFactoryV6` points at a proxy whose implementation has been upgraded in place to `PendleMarketFactoryV7Upg`.

**The AMM, in Pendle's own terms.** Pendle v2 prices PT against SY in *implied-rate space*, not price space. In `contracts/core/Market/MarketMathCore.sol`: the spot exchange rate is `exchangeRate = lnProportion.divDown(rateScalar) + rateAnchor` (line 343), where `proportion = (totalPt − netPtToAccount)/(totalPt + totalAsset)` (line 335) and `_logProportion = ln(p/(1−p))` (lines 348–354). It is a **logit curve in rate space** with a movable intercept. Two of its three parameters are functions of time to expiry:

- `rateScalar = scalarRoot × IMPLIED_RATE_TIME / timeToExpiry`, with `IMPLIED_RATE_TIME = 365 days` (`_getRateScalar`, lines 356–359, constant at line 44). This **grows without bound** as expiry approaches — the curve gets stiffer, not softer.
- `rateAnchor = e^(lnLastImpliedRate × yearsToExpiry) − lnProportion/rateScalar`, recomputed **before every single trade** so that the pre-trade implied rate equals the implied rate left by the previous trade (`_getRateAnchor`, lines 276–294; whitepaper eq. 22). Because `e^(r·t) → 1` as `t → 0`, the anchor decays toward 1.
- `lnImpliedRate = ln(exchangeRate) × IMPLIED_RATE_TIME / timeToExpiry` is persisted as `lastLnImpliedRate` after each trade (lines 298–312, 269–271).

`scalarRoot`, `initialAnchor`, `lnFeeRateRoot` and `expiry` are immutable per market; `timeToExpiry = market.expiry − blockTime` (lines 212, 264) is the only clock. Guards: `MAX_MARKET_PROPORTION = 96%` (line 46), `exchangeRate ≥ 1` enforced (line 345), `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY = 1000` permanently locked (lines 41, 125–126).

**Index accounting.** `_pyIndexCurrent() = PMath.max(IStandardizedYield(SY).exchangeRate(), _pyIndexStored)` (`PendleYieldToken.sol` line 406) — a **monotonic high-water mark**, which Pendle's docs call the "Watermark Rate": if the SY exchange rate falls, YT simply stops earning until it recovers, and the loss lands entirely on PT at redemption. Interest and rewards accrue on *separate* indexes: interest by a reciprocal-difference formula `principal × (cur − prev)/(prev × cur)` (`InterestManagerYT.sol` line 76), rewards by per-token `userReward[token][user].{index, accrued}` fed from `SY.rewardIndexesCurrent()` (`PendleYieldToken.sol` lines 386–390, 449–479).

**Fees, read live on-chain 2026-08-04.** On YieldContractFactoryV6 `0x3E6EBa46AbC5ab18ED95F6667d8B2fd4020E4637`: `interestFeeRate() = 5e16` and `rewardFeeRate() = 5e16` — **5% of all YT yield, including points** — each capped at `maxInterestFeeRate = maxRewardFeeRate = 2e17` (20%) in `PendleYieldContractFactoryUpg.sol` lines 73–74. There is **no global swap-fee rate**: `lnFeeRateRoot` is chosen per market by its creator (capped by `maxLnFeeRateRoot`), and the fee multiplier is `e^(lnFeeRateRoot × yearsToExpiry)` (`MarketMathCore.sol` line 223) — the fee is quoted as an *interest rate* and shrinks in notional terms as maturity nears. `reserveFeePercent() = 80` on MarketFactory `0x6d247b1c044fA1E22e6B04fA9F71Baf99EB29A9f`: **80% of every swap fee to the treasury, 20% retained by LPs** (`netAssetToReserve = fee × reserveFeePercent / 100`, line 246). Redemption of PT at maturity carries no fee. Treasury: `0x8270400d528c34e1596EF367eeDEc99080A1b592` (Safe, threshold 2).

**Governance token layer — mid-migration.** `VotingEscrowPendleMainchain` has `MAX_LOCK_TIME = 104 weeks`, `MIN_LOCK_TIME = 1 week`, expiries snapped to `time % WEEK == 0` (Thursday 00:00 UTC), and the gauge controller pays per-market `pendlePerSec`. But in the repo's own mainnet manifest `deployments/1-core.json`, the key `deprecated` contains exactly four entries — `vePendle: 0x4f30A9D41B80ecC5B94306AB4364951AE3170210`, `feeDistributor`, `feeDistributorV2`, `votingController: 0x44087E105137a5095c008AaB6a6530182821F2F0`. The replacement, `sPendle: 0x999999999991E178D52Cd95AFd4b00d066664144`, implemented in `contracts/LiquidityMining/sPendle/StakedPendle.sol`, is **not a vote-escrow at all**: it is a 1:1 stake with `cooldown(amount)` → `finalizeCooldown()` after `cooldownDuration`, or `instantUnstake(amount)` paying `instantUnstakeFeeRate` (the in-code comment reads `// base 1e18. 5e16 means instant unstake will have to pay 5% of the amount.`). There is no lock-time-weighted balance anywhere in the contract. `setCooldownDurationAndFee` is `onlyOwner` with the duration unbounded and the fee capped only at 100%.

**Dependency graph.** SY wrappers name their upstreams explicitly: Lido (wstETH), Rocket Pool (rETH), Puffer (pufETH), Aave (aTokens), Curve/Silo (scrvUSD), GMX (GLP), Renzo (ezETH), Bedrock (uniETH), plus Uniswap LP positions. Wrappers live in a separate public repo, `pendle-finance/Pendle-SY-Public`.

**Control plane.** Governance is a Gnosis Safe `0x8119EC16F0573B7dAc7C0CB94EB504FB32456ee1`, `getThreshold() = 3`, owning the yield-contract factory and ProxyAdmin `0xA28c08f165116587D4F3E708743B4dEe155c5E64`. `PendleGovernanceProxy` `0x2aD631F72fB16d91c4953A7f4260A97C2fE2f31e` (UUPS + AccessControl) owns the market factory and gauge controller and carries `bytes32 public constant GUARDIAN` with `function pause(address[] calldata addrs) external onlyGuardian` (lines 14, 37–40, 93). devMultisig `0xE6F0489ED91dc27f40f9dbe8f81fccbFC16b9cb1` (threshold 2). vePENDLE is **not** proxied — implementation slot is zero, so it is immutable. **Timelock: UNKNOWN** — `getMinDelay()`, `delay()` and `MINIMUM_DELAY()` all revert on governance, governanceProxy and proxyAdmin, and no timelock appears in the mainnet manifest; absence off-repo could not be proven. Market and yield-contract creation are **permissionless**: `createNewMarket` carries the comment *"Anyone is allowed to create a market on their own"* and has no owner modifier (`PendleMarketFactoryV7Upg.sol` line 70), and `createYieldContract` likewise (`PendleYieldContractFactoryUpg.sol` line 108), constrained only by `expiry % expiryDivisor == 0` with a live `expiryDivisor() = 86400`.

### WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

Pendle is the one member of this lane whose residue is **mechanism** residue rather than **level** residue, and that contrast is itself a finding: everything Pendle does that the vocabulary cannot name is an on-chain formula, whereas everything CIAN and Spark do that it cannot name is a human decision. Specifically:

1. **An invariant that is a function of time.** The corpus already flagged this and it survives contact with the code, in stronger form. The five pool-pricing symbols (`Cp`, `Wg`, `St`, `Cl`, `Pm`) are all *static* invariants: the same reserves imply the same price forever. `exchangeRate = ln(p/(1−p))/rateScalar + rateAnchor` with `rateScalar ∝ 1/timeToExpiry` means the same pool state prices differently on a different day, deterministically. There is no symbol for a curve parameterised by time-to-maturity. The corpus's rejection of the contested `Tw` (time-weighted AMM *execution*) is correct and should stand.
2. **A stateful re-parameterisation of the invariant on every trade.** `_getRateAnchor` resets the intercept before each swap so that the implied rate is continuous across trades. This is not slippage, not a fee, not an oracle — it is the invariant rewriting itself to preserve a derived quantity. No AMM symbol covers a curve whose parameters are functions of its own trade history.
3. **Quotation in rate space rather than price space.** `Py` names the *split* into PT and YT. It does not name the fact that the market that prices the split quotes an interest rate, and that the pool's state variable is `lastLnImpliedRate`.
4. **A monotonic high-water-mark index.** `Ix` (index-based accrual) permits an index to move in both directions. `PMath.max(SY.exchangeRate(), _pyIndexStored)` is a **ratchet**, and it is asymmetric across claim classes: YT stops accruing, PT eats the drawdown. This is a loss-allocation rule disguised as an accounting choice, and neither `Ix` nor `Sl` names it.
5. **A fee denominated in interest rate.** `fee = e^(lnFeeRateRoot × yearsToExpiry)` charges less notional as maturity nears, because the fee is a *rate* spread, not a *price* spread.
6. **Decaying notional.** A YT's claim shrinks to zero by construction. Every other claim symbol in the vocabulary (`Sh`, `Ix`, `Rb`) describes a claim whose notional is preserved or grows.
7. **Post-expiry escheatment.** Rewards arriving on an expired YT go to the treasury (`PendleYieldToken.sol` line 436, plus `postExpiry.totalSyInterestForTreasury`). Not a fee, not a redemption fee — an abandonment sweep, which has no symbol.
8. **A quantized term structure minted permissionlessly.** `expiry % 86400 == 0` on a permissionless factory means maturities exist on a fixed calendar grid that anyone may populate, and that the same PT can back unlimited parallel markets keyed `markets[PT][scalarRoot][initialAnchor][lnFeeRateRoot]`. `Ft` names one maturity-dated claim; it does not name a *term structure* as an object, nor the birth-and-death lifecycle of a maturity.
9. **Cooldown-or-pay-to-skip exit.** sPENDLE offers a queued exit (`cooldownDuration`) *or* immediate exit at a price (`instantUnstakeFeeRate`). `Wq` names the queue; it has no room for a priced option to jump it. This is a new pattern in the lane and it replaced a vote-escrow.
10. **Scoped-selector delegated governance.** `hasScopedAccess[caller][selector][target]` is finer-grained than `Au` (delegated execution scope) or `Aw`: authority is a per-(caller, function-selector, target) triple.
11. **Points.** YT accrues off-chain-attested, non-token reward streams, and Pendle taxes them at the same 5%. Nothing in the vocabulary names a claim on an issuer's discretionary future distribution.

**On the two claims this lane is asked to test:** Pendle does **not** exhibit the strategy level and does **not** exhibit a delegated allocation mandate. There is no allocator, no cap, no rebalancer, no performance fee on a discretionary manager, and no address with authority over another depositor's capital. This is a genuine null result and it is useful: it establishes that the level the corpus is reaching for is not a property of "yield protocols" generally, but specifically of protocols that take custody and then *choose*. Pendle takes custody and does not choose. The one place discretion enters — directing emissions — was implemented as vote-escrow and has now been **retired** in favour of a plain staked token, which removes even that.

## Spark Savings (sUSDS / Sky Savings Rate)

### WHAT IT DOES

A depositor brings **USDS** and receives **sUSDS**, an ERC-4626 share token: balances do not rebase, the redemption rate rises. The rate it earns is the **Sky Savings Rate (SSR)**, a per-second compounding rate stored as a RAY on the sUSDS contract itself. The yield does not come from a utilisation curve, an auction, or any market: `drip()` computes the interest owed and funds it with `vat.suck(address(vow), address(this), diff * RAY)` — it *mints* internal Dai against the Sky surplus/deficit account and pays savers unconditionally. Economically the payment is expected to be covered ex post by CDP stability fees, RWA and treasury revenue, but nothing in the payment path reads revenue, the surplus buffer, or any solvency condition; the SSR can therefore exceed Sky's income and the shortfall lands on the vow as `sin`. Nobody chooses where a *depositor's* USDS goes at the sUSDS level — sUSDS holds no assets and runs no strategy; it is a claim on Sky's balance sheet. The allocation decision happens one level up, in the **Spark Liquidity Layer**, where a relayer multisig deploys Sky's capital into Aave/SparkLend, Morpho, Curve, Uniswap V4, Ethena, EtherFi, Lido, Maple, Superstate USTB and OTC desks under on-chain rate limits. Exit is instant against idle buffers via the PSM at zero fee; large mainnet withdrawals are routed through a "Savings Liquidity Intents" request fulfilled off-chain within minutes. Live on 2026-08-04: `sUSDS.chi()` = 1.105245252046483725540827799, `sUSDS.totalAssets()` = **4,750,051,555.68 USDS**, SSR ≈ 3.52% APY, against `MCD_POT.dsr()` ≈ 1.25% — the two savings rates are demonstrably different numbers on different contracts.

### DESIGN

**Which contract is the claim.** `sUSDS` = `0xa3931d71877C0E7a3148CB7Eb4463524FEc27fbD` (chainlog key `SUSDS`), an `ERC1967Proxy`, solc 0.8.21, implementation `0x4e7991e5C547ce825BdEb665EE14a3274f9F61e0` — UUPS-upgradeable. Everything else is *around* it and must not be conflated with it: `sDAI` `0x83F20F44975D03b1b09e64809B757c47f942BEeA` over `MCD_POT` `0x197E90f9FAD81970bA7976f33CbD77088E5D7cf7` is the older DSR object with its own rate; **SparkLend** is an Aave-v3 fork (DefiLlama `forkedFromIds: ["1599"]`, category `Lending`, $3.63bn, 2 chains) and is *not* the savings product; the **Spark Liquidity Layer** is a separate DefiLlama protocol ($1.58bn, category **`Onchain Capital Allocator`**); "Spark Savings" is the front end over sUSDS plus newer V2 vaults (spUSDC / spUSDT / spETH / spPYUSD / spUSDG) whose yield is described as *"generated via the Spark Liquidity Layer"*, distinct from sUSDS which is *"Powered by rates set by Sky Governance"*.

**The accrual math** (`sky-ecosystem/sdai`, branch `susds`, `src/SUsds.sol`): state is `uint192 chi` (rate accumulator, RAY), `uint64 rho` (last drip), `uint256 ssr` (per-second rate, RAY). `drip()` is
`nChi = _rpow(ssr, block.timestamp - rho_) * chi_ / RAY; diff = totalSupply_*nChi/RAY - totalSupply_*chi_/RAY; vat.suck(address(vow), address(this), diff*RAY); usdsJoin.exit(address(this), diff); chi = nChi; rho = now;`.
`convertToAssets` extrapolates `chi` without writing, so views are exact without a recent drip. The setter is `function file(bytes32 what, uint256 data) external auth { if (what == "ssr") { require(data >= RAY, "SUsds/wrong-ssr-value"); ... } }` — **the only bound inside the token is non-negativity. There is no ceiling and no derivation.**

**Who moves the number — SP-BEAM.** `MCD_SPBEAM` = `0x36B072ed8AFE665E3Aa6DaBa79Decbec63752b22`, repo `sky-ecosystem/sp-beam`, "Stability Parameter Bounded External Access Module", with siblings `SPBEAM_MOM` and `EMSP_SPBEAM_HALT` in the chainlog. Independently verified by `eth_call`: **`sUSDS.wards(0x36B072ed…) = 1`** — SP-BEAM is authorised on the savings token on mainnet. ### WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

1. **A bounded discretionary parameter mandate.** This is the finding, and it is not the one the corpus expected to find here. SP-BEAM lets a *named non-governance agent* move a live economic parameter inside an on-chain envelope `{min, max, step, cooldown}`, revocable by `deny` and haltable by `bad`. `Tg` (delayed-governance execution) is wrong — the facilitator's change is immediate and skips the 48-hour GSM. `Au` (delegated execution scope) is wrong — law L17 requires `bounded scope + revocation + expiry + nonce/domain separation`, which describes a *session key over the principal's own assets*; a facilitator has no expiry, no nonce, and acts over the whole system's economics. `Gp` names the halt but not the envelope. **What has no symbol is the envelope itself: a numeric domain restriction on a delegated decision.** See the CIAN section — this is the same object as the curator mandate, applied to a rate instead of an allocation, and finding it in Sky's core is the strongest evidence in this lane that the corpus is right to reach for a level rather than an element.
2. **Deficit-funded interest issuance.** `vat.suck(vow, this, diff*RAY)` pays savers by minting protocol debt, unconditionally, with no read of revenue or reserves. Not `Em` (no separate token, no schedule), not `Sl` (there is no loss event; the deficit is created by the *payment*), not `As` (no peg rule drives it), not `Fd` (candidate `Fd` is *surplus* distribution, and this can run in deficit). The corpus's residue item "no element for a rate that can exceed protocol revenue" is confirmed, and the mechanism is now named precisely.
3. **A continuously regenerating capacity allowance (leaky bucket).** `_min(slope*(now - lastUpdated) + lastAmount, maxAmount)`. Not `Ep` (no discrete periods), not `Wq` (no ordering, no queue), not `Ct` (no solvency test). This is a *rate limit on an agent's throughput*, and it is the second half of the mandate envelope — the first half being a whitelist of venues.
4. **A rate-tuple mirror with a non-accruing remote claim.** `(ssr, chi, rho)` is bridged; the remote token contains no accrual code; value is recomputed locally and realised against local PSM inventory. The corpus said "no element for cross-chain sameness of a single savings claim… `Xf` only says value moved." That is right, but the corpus assumed the sameness was economic identity. It is not: the mainnet claim can `vat.suck`, the L2 claim cannot, and L2 redemption depends on PSM3 inventory. The unnamed object is **a replicated index with a locally-bounded settlement guarantee**.
5. **A split grant/revoke authority.** `AdministeredAgent`'s deliberate separation of `grantors` from `revokers` so revokers can act as watchdogs has no analogue in `Tg`/`Gp`/`Au`, all of which treat authority as a single scalar.

**On the two claims this lane is asked to test:** the strategy level is present at Spark, but *displaced*. sUSDS itself has no strategy — it is a pure claim on a balance sheet, and it decomposes cleanly. The strategy sits in the Liquidity Layer, which DefiLlama files as a **separate protocol in a separate category** ("Onchain Capital Allocator", $1.58bn). That separation is itself evidence for the level claim: the same organisation ships the claim and the mandate as different objects with different addresses, different roles and different repositories. A delegated allocation mandate is unambiguously present — a relayer multisig moving Sky's capital across a whitelist under regenerating caps, with a separate freezer multisig — and no symbol names it.

"Set by a governance vote" is superseded: as of the live chainlog, `sUSDS.wards(MCD_SPBEAM) = 1`, and the accurate statement is **governance writes the *bounds*; a whitelisted facilitator writes the *number* inside them, immediately, without a spell and without the 48-hour GSM delay, subject to a 16-hour cooldown and an emergency halt.** The corpus's residue item is not refuted — it is sharpened into something more valuable.
4. **"sUSDS pays from Sky's CDP interest and RWA/treasury revenue" — misleading as a mechanism claim.** The *payment path* is `vat.suck` against the vow with no revenue check. Economically the vow is funded by fees; mechanically the saver is paid first and the deficit is a residue. Restate as "funded ex post from the surplus buffer, not ex ante from realised revenue."
5. **"sUSDS on 8 chains is one economic claim" — materially wrong.** Only the mainnet contract holds `chi`/`ssr` and can mint against the vow. L2 sUSDS is a mint/burn ERC-20 with no accrual logic; its price is an oracle-forwarded extrapolation and its redeemability is local PSM3 inventory. 6. **`Ps` marker: the corpus's discomfort is correct and generalises.** The corpus flagged that the Sky PSM belongs to the system sUSDS sits on, not to Spark Savings, and that the vocabulary cannot mark an element as inherited. On L2 the situation is worse: the *only* thing standing behind the remote claim is an inherited PSM3 with its own inventory, so the inherited element is load-bearing for solvency while remaining invisible in the decomposition.

## Convex Finance

### WHAT IT DOES

Convex has three distinct deposit paths and they have different contracts. **(i) Curve LP tokens** go into `Booster`, which stakes them in the corresponding Curve gauge and credits a 1:1 receipt into a per-pool reward contract; the depositor earns CRV at Convex's pooled boost rate, plus CVX, plus whatever extra reward tokens the gauge emits, and can withdraw at any time with no deposit or withdrawal fee. **(ii) CRV** goes into `CrvDepositor`, which forwards it into the protocol's permanent veCRV lock and mints **cvxCRV**; this conversion is **one-way** — Convex's own documentation says *"cvxCRV conversion is 1-way"* and *"If a user deposits CRV into Convex, that CRV is locked forever on the platform as veCRV."* cvxCRV stakers receive Curve admin fees (now paid **as crvUSD**, not 3CRV), a share of Convex's CRV performance fee, and CVX, and may elect any mix between 100% crvUSD and 100% CRV/CVX. The only exit from cvxCRV is selling it on a secondary market. **(iii) CVX** is locked for 16 weeks into `CvxLockerV2` to become **vlCVX**, which earns a share of protocol fees and carries the governance weight that decides how Convex's veCRV votes. The yield in all three paths ultimately originates outside Convex — it is Curve's emissions and Curve's admin fees, amplified by a boost that Convex owns and rents. Who decides where capital goes: nobody, at the LP level (the depositor picks the pool); vlCVX voters, at the emissions level.

### DESIGN

**The lock.** `CurveVoterProxy` (deployed name; source file `VoterProxy.sol`) at `0x989AEb4d175e16225E39E87d0D97A3360524AD80` holds the veCRV. Live on 2026-08-04: `veCRV.balanceOf(proxy) = 420,633,657.30` against `veCRV.totalSupply() = 783,593,117.83` — **53.68% of all veCRV**; `locked(proxy).amount = 423,568,331.33 CRV`, `locked__end = 1911168000` (2030-07-25). `CrvDepositor` carries `uint256 private constant MAXTIME = 4 * 364 * 86400;` and `_lockCurve()` calls `increaseTime` whenever the week-rounded target exceeds the stored unlock, so the lock is a **perpetual max ratchet** sitting ~3.97 years ahead of the present at all times. Curve's `VotingEscrow.withdraw()` requires `block.timestamp >= _locked.end`, so the underlying CRV is unreachable by construction; `VoterProxy.release()` exists but is depositor-gated and only functions on an expired lock. Voting is exercised by `voteGaugeWeight(address _gauge, uint256 _weight)` and `vote(uint256, address, bool)`, both operator-gated, against hardcoded `gaugeController = 0x2F50D538606Fa9EDD2B11E2446BEb18C9D5846bB`.

**Why pooling is rational — the boost.** Curve's `LiquidityGaugeV3.vy::_update_liquidity_limit` sets `working_balance = min(l, 0.4·l + 0.6·L·V/V_total)` with `TOKENLESS_PRODUCTION = 40`, i.e. a maximum **2.5×** multiplier. Because `V/V_total` is a single shared term, one very large veCRV position saturates the boost for arbitrarily many small LP balances that could never individually reach 2.5×. That arbitrage is Convex's entire economic reason to exist.

**Fee structure — live, and different from the widely-quoted historical numbers.** All values `/FEE_DENOMINATOR = 10000` on `Booster`: `lockIncentive = 1000` (**10%** to cvxCRV stakers), `stakerIncentive = 450` (**4.5%** to CVX lockers, routed to `stakerRewards 0xCF50b810E57Ac33B91dCF525C6ddd9881B139332`), `earmarkIncentive = 50` (**0.5%** to whoever calls `earmarkRewards`), `platformFee = 200` (**2%** to treasury `0x1389388d01708118b497f59521f6943Be2541bb7`). **Total 17% of CRV revenue**, matching the docs verbatim (*"There is a 17% total fee on all CRV revenue"*), against an absolute code ceiling `MaxFees = 2000` (**20%**). `setFees()` additionally bounds each component: lock 1000–1500, staker 300–600, earmark 10–100, platform ≤ 200. The often-cited 16% / 10% / 5% / 1% split is **outdated**.

**CVX emissions — effectively exhausted.** `Cvx.sol` (`ConvexToken`): `maxSupply = 100_000_000e18`, `totalCliffs = 1000`, `reductionPerCliff = maxSupply/totalCliffs`, and `mint()` scales each issuance by `(totalCliffs - cliff)/totalCliffs`. Live `totalSupply() = 99,983,304.90` = **99.983% of cap → cliff 999 → multiplier 0.001**, and zero once cliff reaches 1000. CVX emissions are, for practical purposes, over.

**vlCVX and the vote.** `CvxLockerV2` `0x72a19342e8F1838460eBFCCEf09F6585e32db86E`: `lockDuration() = 9,676,800 s = 112 days = 16 weeks`, `rewardsDuration() = 604,800 s = 1 week`, `epochCount() = 232`, `lockedSupply() = 46,056,535.73 CVX`. Epochs start Thursday 00:00 UTC. Curve **gauge-weight** votes, however, are **bi-weekly** — *"starting Thursdays at 00:00 UTC and ending on Tuesdays 00:00 UTC"* — not weekly. Since 2026 the vote is executed by a purpose-built on-chain system: `convex-eth/voting`, created 2026-04-15, last commit 2026-07-29, registering `DaoVotePlatform` / `GaugeVotePlatform` / executors for **Curve, f(x), Frax and Resupply**, with a `SurrogateRegistry` and one-level `Delegation`. `GaugeVotePlatform.createProposal` anchors voting weights at `epoch = vlCVX.epochCount() - 2` via `vlCVX.balanceAtEpochOf`, and `CurveGaugeExecutor.executeGaugeVote(proposalId, gauges)` submits through `VoteDelegateExtension.GaugeVote(gauges, weights)`. It is live: `Booster.voteDelegate() = 0x5349fFbA494Ac3C888ffA16Fd438f44b8C67fb07`, exactly the `VoteDelegateExtension` in that repo's `deployment/mainnet.json`. Its README states the aim is *"shifting administrative control away from multisig signers toward transparent, token-weighted governance."*

**Bribes are not Convex.** No Convex contract implements a vote-incentive market. A code search over `convex-eth/platform` for "bribe" returns exactly two non-implementing hits — `contracts/contracts/interfaces/ICrvBribe.sol`, an interface with no bodies, and a test script `Script_ClaimBribes.js`; `convex-eth/voting` returns zero. **Votium** is a separate protocol, separate GitHub org, separate domain and separate addresses (Votium v2 `0x63942E31E98f1833A234077f47880A66136a2D1e`, `MultiMerkleStash 0x378Ba9B73309bE80BF4C2c027aAD799766a7ED5A`, delegation proxy `0xde1E6A7ED0ad3F61D531a8a78E83CcDdbd6E0c49`), sharing no address with Convex.

**Control plane.** `Booster.owner() = 0x3cE6408F923326f81A7D7929952947748180f1E6` (`BoosterOwner`); `feeManager() = 0xa3C5A1e09150B75ff251c1a7815A07182c3de2FB` (the multisig, a Gnosis Safe with live `getThreshold() = 3` and 5 owners); `poolManager() = 0x5f47010F230cE1568BeA53a06ebAf528d05c5c1B` (`PoolManagerProxy`); `staker()` = VoterProxy; `minter()` = CVX. **The core contracts are not upgradeable** — Blockscout reports `proxy = None` for every one of them; the only migration path is `shutdownSystem()` (owner-only, pulls all LP out of gauges and marks pools shut) followed by redeployment. ### WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

Convex is the clearest case in the lane of a protocol that is *almost entirely* residue, and the corpus is right about why. Strip the vote layer and what remains — `Sh` + `Ix` + `Em` — is indistinguishable from any staking-rewards contract, and `Em` is now numerically dead. Specifically unnamed:

1. **Vote-escrow itself.** A non-transferable voting weight that decays linearly with remaining lock time. `Ep` names a period boundary; `Tg` names a delay before execution; neither names a *weight that is a function of time remaining*. The corpus's contested-register use of `Ve` is the strongest in the lane and the alternative is to concede that a $455m protocol is 100% residue.
2. **Nonlinear boost.** `working_balance = min(l, 0.4·l + 0.6·L·V/V_total)` is a reward multiplier on one balance keyed to a *different token's* balance held by a *different party*. Neither `Sh` nor `Ix` can express a claim whose accrual rate depends on an external, transferable, pooled quantity. This is the mechanism that makes aggregation rational, and it has no name.
3. **Vote-directed emissions.** `Em` names issuance. Nothing names *voters choosing where issuance goes* — that emissions are an allocation decision rather than a schedule.
4. **A liquid wrapper of a permanently locked position.** cvxCRV is a transferable claim on a position with no redemption function; the peg is a secondary market. The vocabulary has `Rd` (direct redemption right) and the object here is its **structured absence** — a designed one-way valve. This pattern (liquid staking, liquid restaking, liquid lockers) is one of the most replicated in DeFi and there is no symbol for it.
5. **A perpetual max-lock ratchet.** `_lockCurve()` extends the lock whenever it would otherwise decay. The system's own commitment is renewed automatically, forever, by a permissionless call.
6. **Meta-governance capture across foreign protocols.** `convex-eth/voting` registers vote platforms and executors for Curve, f(x), Frax and Resupply. `Au` covers execution scope; it does not cover *acquiring and exercising governance rights in protocols you do not control*.
7. **An off-chain-sentiment-to-on-chain-execution surrogate.** vlCVX holders vote in a Convex-side proposal anchored to a historical epoch balance; a separate executor then casts the aggregated result into Curve's gauge controller. The two votes are different objects at different addresses with different electorates, joined by a surrogate registry.
8. **A permissionless harvest bounty.** `earmarkIncentive = 50` paid to whoever calls `earmarkRewards`. `Fd` names surplus distribution to claimants; this is a *subsidy for liveness*, paid to an anonymous caller for performing work the protocol cannot perform itself.
9. **Extra-reward stash pass-through.** `ExtraRewardStashV1/V2/V3`, `StashTokenWrapper` and per-pool virtual-balance children forward arbitrary third-party gauge tokens with balances that mirror the parent pool without holding them.

**On the two claims this lane is asked to test:** Convex has **no strategy level and no allocation mandate**, and the reason is instructive. Convex takes custody of enormous value and exercises enormous discretion — but the discretion is over *votes*, not over *allocation*. No Convex address decides where a depositor's capital is deployed; the depositor picks the pool and Convex only stakes it where the depositor said. So Convex is a second null result on the mandate question, and it isolates the variable: custody alone does not create a mandate, and discretion alone does not either. **A mandate requires custody *and* discretion over the deployment of the custodied assets.** Convex has the first and a different kind of second. That distinction is worth carrying into Stage 4, because it tells you the primitive is not "an agent with power" but specifically "an agent with power over where other people's principal sits."

## CIAN Yield Layer

### WHAT IT DOES

A depositor brings WETH — or ETH, stETH, wstETH, weETH through an `optionalDeposit` path — to a vault whose flagship instance is `0xB13aa2d0345b0439b064f26B82D8dCf3f508775d`, live `symbol() = "ylstETH"`, `name() = "CIAN yield layer stETH"`, `asset() = WETH`. The claim issued is an ERC-4626 share, which CIAN's documentation calls a "receipt token … certificate of deposit, reflecting a share of the stETH Yield Layer". The yield comes from a **levered liquid-staking loop**: the live strategy `StrategyAAVEV3LIDO` supplies wstETH into Aave v3's Lido market, sets `setUserEMode(1)`, borrows WETH against it, and re-stakes — so the depositor earns the LST staking rate multiplied by the leverage, minus the borrow rate, plus points and airdrops from the protocols touched. Who decides where the capital goes: **an off-chain agent holding a KMS-hosted key**. Nothing in the contracts chooses the strategy, the leverage, the venue or the timing. Exit is **not** ERC-4626: `withdraw()` and `redeem()` both `revert Errors.NotSupportedYet()`. A depositor calls `requestRedeem(uint256 _shares, address _token)` and waits for a `RedeemOperator` to batch-settle — documented at roughly **5 days** for the ETH vaults, ~7 for fBTC, and described by CIAN as *"batched execution with manual review."* Sister vaults follow the same shape for rsETH, ezETH, fBTC and BTCLST.

### DESIGN

**Vault.** `ERC4626Upgradeable` behind a `TransparentUpgradeableProxy`, implementation `VaultYieldETH`. Live `VaultParams`: `marketCapacity` 50,000 WETH, `managementFeeClaimPeriod` 7 days, `maxPriceUpdatePeriod` **3 days**, `admin 0x8FA9aa69a6e94c1cd49FbF214C833B2911D02553`, `rebalancer 0x6d425B3D302DD82cC611866eC8176d435307b616`, `feeReceiver 0xc554747f…493d`, `redeemOperator 0xdac6748cbb7cd9da1868eb7ad598273122f012db`. NAV is `totalAssets() = exchangePrice * totalSupply() / PRECISION`, where `exchangePrice` is **pushed by the operator**, not derived from an oracle.

**Strategy layer.** Separate per-strategy contracts behind their own proxies. The vault's only structural constraint on them is a size cap in `transferToStrategy`: `if ((nowAssets_ + _amount) > (totalAssets() * positionLimit_ / 1e4)) revert Errors.InvalidLimit();`.

**Rebalancing — the crux, in code.** `leverage()` and `deleverage()` are gated by
```solidity
modifier onlyRebalancer() { if (msg.sender != rebalancer) revert Errors.CallerNotRebalancer(); _; }
```
and take **caller-supplied** `_leverageAmount`, `_swapData`, `_swapGetMin` and `_flashloanSelector`. The only economic enforcement is a post-condition:
```solidity
function checkProtocolRatio() internal view {
    (, bool isOK_) = getCollateralRatio();
    if (!isOK_) revert Errors.RatioOutOfRange();
}
// isOK_ = safeProtocolRatio > collateralRatio_;
```
Live `safeProtocolRatio() = 0.923e18`, live `MAX_PROTOCOL_RATIO() = 0.93e18`. There is a `getLeverageAmount()` `public view` that computes exactly the amount needed to reach `safeProtocolRatio` — but it is a **helper, not a constraint**: nothing forces the rebalancer to call it, to use its output, to hit the target, or to act at all.

**Who the rebalancer is.** `vault.rebalancer` and `strategy.rebalancer()` both resolve to `0x6d425B3D302DD82cC611866eC8176d435307b616`, whose verified source is `contract Manager is Ownable` with the docstring *"This contract is used to manage KMS addresses for batch operations."* Its sole privileged entry point is
```solidity
function multiCall(address[] calldata _addresses, bytes[] calldata _callBytes) external onlyOperator
// modifier onlyOperator() { if (!operators[msg.sender]) revert Errors.CallerNotOperator(); _; }
```
The complete authority path is: **cloud-hosted signing key → `Manager.multiCall` → `Strategy.leverage/deleverage` and `Vault.updateExchangePrice / stakeTo / transferToStrategy`.** Every vault has its own `Manager`; all four share the `admin` address.

**Flash loans.** `StrategyAAVEV3LIDO` implements `IERC3156FlashBorrower` and calls `IFlashloanHelper(flashloanHelper).flashLoan(...)`. Live `flashloanHelper() = 0x49d9409111a6363d82c4371ffa43faea660c917b`, verified as `FlashloanHelper` (author "Cian") with `enum FLSource { AAVEV3, BALANCER, MAKER }` — **not Morpho**. The provider is chosen by the caller via `_flashloanSelector`; reentrancy is blocked by `if (executor != address(0)) revert Errors.FlashloanInProgress();`.

**Fees.** Documentation states a **8% performance fee** and a **0.02% exit fee**. On-chain, `revenueRate = 800` bps confirms the 8%, but `exitFeeRate = 0` and `managementFeeRate = 0` across all four vaults read. Hard-coded ceilings: `MAX_REVENUE_FEE_RATE = 1500` (15%), `MAX_EXIT_FEE_RATE = 120` (1.2%), `MAX_MANAGEMENT_FEE_RATE = 4`.

**Dependency graph.** Strategy contracts named in the docs for the stETH vault: `StrategyETHConverter`, `StrategyAAVEV3LIDO`, `StrategyMellowSteakhouse`, `StrategyCompoundRSETH`, `StrategyAaveV3LidoEzETH`, `StrategyAAVEV3RsETH`. rsETH vault: Compound and Aave v3. fBTC/BTCLST: Solv, Bedrock, PumpBTC. Docs additionally list Lido, EtherFi, Renzo, Kelp, Benqi, Avalon, Venus, Pendle, 1inch, Paraswap, Curve and Balancer V2. CIAN operates **no oracle of its own**: the strategy reads Aave's `IAaveOracle 0xE3C06198…0BE6`, and docs say Chainlink is used *"as a security check before accepting deposits."*

**Control plane — no timelock on the Yield Layer.** Vault `owner()` and `Manager.owner()` are both `0x261090afe1e7305474f4e3eefcaca9964ebffff5`, a Gnosis Safe with `getThreshold() = 3` over 4 owners. The vault's ERC-1967 admin slot points to ProxyAdmin `0xf24955e9d0a05222373d4555f8831f7a05e83f62`, whose `owner()` is **that same Safe** — so a 3-of-4 multisig can replace the vault implementation **instantly, with no delay**. A `Timelock 0xb39e6f93cff9Af7011810f41a4ed9b14582019b7` with `delay() = 86400` exists but is documented only for the 2022-era "flagship Vault" product, and its `admin()` is `0x8FA9aa69…`, which `eth_getCode` shows to be an **EOA, not a multisig**, despite the docs labelling it "MultiSig". Pause exists: `pause()` is callable by `owner()` **or** `rebalancer`, `unpause()` by owner only; live `paused() = false`.

**A contract-enforced liveness dependency.** `totalAssets()` reverts with `Errors.PriceNotUpdated()` if `block.timestamp - lastUpdatePriceTime > maxPriceUpdatePeriod` (3 days). Live `lastUpdatePriceTime = 1785722699` = 2026-08-03T02:04:59Z, `exchangePrice = 1.0735855763962243`. **If the off-chain agent stops for three days, NAV, deposits and redemptions all break** — before any Aave liquidation is reached.

### WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

This is where the lane's two central claims get decided, and both survive — one of them in a stronger and more precise form than the corpus states.

**Claim (a): a STRATEGY is a missing LEVEL, not a missing element. SUSTAINED, and the code shows why the distinction is real.** The corpus argues that a strategy is "a policy expressed over other protocols' mechanisms, with no on-chain mechanism of its own." CIAN's code makes that literal. Enumerate what is on-chain: the leverage machinery, the flash-loan plumbing over Aave/Balancer/Maker, a single ceiling `safeProtocolRatio = 0.923` under an immutable `MAX_PROTOCOL_RATIO = 0.93`, a per-strategy `positionLimit_`, a `marketCapacity`, `_swapGetMin` slippage floors, fee caps, a pause, and ERC-4626-shaped share accounting. Now enumerate what is **not**: nothing schedules a rebalance; nothing defines when the target is missed; nothing bounds the mandate to a particular strategy set; nothing compels the operator to use `getLeverageAmount()`'s output; and `checkProtocolRatio` is a **one-sided ceiling** — it can revert a transaction that ends *too* levered, but it cannot compel one, cannot reject an *under*-levered or simply bad allocation, and cannot fire at all if the operator stays silent.

The right way to say this is sharper than "the vocabulary has no symbol." **The vocabulary has no symbol because the object is not on the chain.** A missing *element* would be a mechanism the vocabulary failed to name. Here there is no mechanism to name: the contract encodes an **envelope**, and a human supplies the **point inside it**. That is a difference in kind, not in coverage, and it is exactly what justifies a third level rather than a 59th symbol. It also predicts, correctly, that no amount of reading CIAN's bytecode will ever reveal the strategy — which is why the absence of a source repository, though a real weakness of this target, does not damage this particular conclusion.

The same shape appears in an independent, fully open reference implementation. Morpho's `MetaMorpho` — the code behind the curated vaults that dominate the "Risk Curators" category — has `reallocate(MarketAllocation[] calldata allocations) external onlyAllocatorRole`, which accepts an **arbitrary target allocation vector from the allocator** and checks exactly three things: that each market is enabled with a non-zero cap (`UnauthorizedMarket`), that `supplyAssets + suppliedAssets <= supplyCap` (`SupplyCapExceeded`), and that `totalWithdrawn == totalSupplied` (`InconsistentReallocation`). There is no objective function, no risk model, no target, no trigger. Two protocols, different teams, different chains-of-thought, identical structure: **domain restriction on-chain, policy off-chain.**

- `Tg` (delayed-governance execution). CIAN's rebalancer acts immediately; Morpho's allocator acts immediately; Sky's facilitator acts immediately. The timelock, where it exists, gates *cap changes*, not *decisions inside caps*. The corpus already spotted this at Steakhouse ("delayed-agent execution, which is a materially different accountability story") and it holds here.
- `Sv` (servicing & determination discretion, candidate). This one *does* fit Huma's Evaluation Agent — determining outcomes on a credit claim — and the corpus is right to promote it there. It does not fit an allocator, who determines nothing about a claim; he chooses where principal sits.

The invariant shape has five slots, and every instance fills at least three: **(1) a named agent, (2) a domain whitelist, (3) a magnitude cap, (4) a rate-of-change limit or a delay, (5) a revocation or halt.** Two further slots are optional and appear where the mandate is compensated: **(6) a performance fee** (CIAN 8%, MetaMorpho `MAX_FEE`, Huma's EA fees) and **(7) skin in the game** (Huma alone requires the EA to hold junior-tranche capital; `PoolConfig._checkLiquidityRequirementForEA` enforces it). **The corpus's formulation — "curator with discretionary authority over other people's deposits, bounded by caps, a timelock and a performance fee" — is correct but a special case.** The general object is a *bounded discretionary mandate*, and the bounded quantity need not be an allocation: it can be a rate, a credit limit, or a leverage ratio. That generalisation is the highest-value thing this lane produces, and it comes from noticing that Sky's rate-setter and Morpho's curator are the same contract shape.

**External corroboration that the level is real.** DefiLlama, whose taxonomy the corpus otherwise uses only for ranking, has independently created **two** categories for exactly this object and nothing else: **"Risk Curators" ($8.813bn)** and **"Onchain Capital Allocator" ($7.737bn)**. Together they hold **$16.55bn**, against **$5.470bn** for "Yield" plus "Yield Aggregator" combined — the level the corpus says has no name is **3.0× larger than the category it is hiding inside**. CIAN itself is filed in both worlds: `CIAN Yield Layer` under `Yield Aggregator` and a separate `CIAN Curating` entry under `Risk Curators`.

**Secondary residue, specific to CIAN:**
1. **Keeper liveness as a contract-enforced dependency.** `totalAssets()` reverting after `maxPriceUpdatePeriod` is not merely "an off-chain agent should call rebalance" — it is the contract *declaring* that it cannot function without one. No symbol records that a mechanism requires an external caller to be alive.
2. **Operator-pushed NAV.** `exchangePrice` is written by the operator; Ackee's W4 is *"Protocol owner can set arbitrary exchange price to pools"*. This is neither `Ex` (no external data source), nor `At` (no attester, no assurance standard, no staleness recourse beyond the 3-day revert), nor `Ix` (an index that a party may set to any value is not an accrual). **An asserted NAV with no attester** has no symbol, and it is the single largest unmitigated risk in the protocol.
3. **A withdrawal queue settled by discretion.** `requestRedeem` plus a `RedeemOperator`, where per Ackee *"the withdrawn amount is also decided by the entity that confirms the withdrawal requests."* `Wq` names an ordering; it does not name a queue whose *settlement amount* is discretionary.
4. **Recursive leverage as an objective.** `Ct` names the constraint; nothing names the fact that the position is deliberately pressed against it.
5. **Composition depth.** A vault whose collateral is a vault whose collateral is an LST. `StrategyMellowSteakhouse` is a CIAN strategy that deposits into a Mellow vault curated by Steakhouse — three mandate layers deep, each with its own agent and its own fee, and the depositor sees one share price.
6. **Fee schedule diverging from documentation** is not a mechanism but is a modelling hazard: a construction built from the docs (8% + 0.02% exit) reproduces a protocol whose live exit fee is zero.

## Huma Finance V2

### WHAT IT DOES

Huma is a "PayFi" network financing real-world payment obligations — cross-border settlement, card settlement, payroll advance — and the name "Huma Finance V2" covers **two structurally different products that must not be conflated**, because the one the corpus decomposed is not the one carrying the TVL.

**Huma 2.0 (permissionless, Solana, launched April 2025)** is where all **$219.36m** of the DefiLlama TVL sits. A depositor brings USDC and receives **$PST** ("PayFi Strategy Token") in *Classic mode* — an administered **8% APY**, *"subject to monthly adjustments based on market conditions"*, plus a base allocation of "Huma Feathers" — or **$mPST** in *Maxi mode*, which pays **0% APY** and maximises Feathers instead. Switching modes is instant and costs only gas. Lockup is optional: none (1× Feathers, redeem anytime), 3 months (2×, promo 3×), or 6 months (3×, promo 5×); commitments can be lengthened but never shortened, and breaking one forfeits the bonus Feathers. Exit is **first-come, first-served against a global daily redemption cap that resets at 00:00 UTC**, with requests above the cap simply rejected; the documented service level is *"most redemptions are completed within 1 business day, though it may take up to 7 days."* Depositors can also bypass the queue by selling PST into the PST/USDC pool on Jupiter or Meteora. The yield originates in transaction fees paid by businesses using Huma's accelerated cross-border payment rails — in practice, short-duration advances originated by Arf.

**Huma Institutional (permissioned)** is the tranched architecture the corpus's element set describes. Lenders pass KYC/KYB and accreditation, are approved by Pool Operators, and deposit into a **senior** or **junior** `TrancheVault`. Senior is paid first on default; junior absorbs losses first and earns more when things go well. Borrowers draw against one of three credit products — a revolving credit line, a receivable-backed credit line (an advance rate below 100% applied to a presented receivable), or receivable factoring. An **Evaluation Agent** underwrites: it approves borrowers, sets the credit limit and the yield in basis points, and declares default. Redemption is epoch-batched, senior first, pro-rated when liquidity is short, with the unfilled portion rolled to the next epoch and the proceeds claimed by calling `disburse()`.

### WHAT LOOKS UNNAMEABLE

1. **The receivable itself — and the answer is sharper than "no symbol".** `ReceivableInfo` stores an amount, a creation date, a paid amount, a currency code, a maturity date, a creator and a state. It stores **no payer**. The obligation is linked to the world by `referenceIdHashToTokenId` — a hash of the originator's own internal reference — and a `uri` string, minted by whoever holds `MINTER_ROLE`. So the receivable *is* an on-chain object, but it is an **assertion, not evidence**: an ERC-721 whose entire credit content is the minter's word. `Uc` says credit is undercollateralised; `At` is a portfolio-level attestation with, per law L27, a named attester, independence, a stated assurance level, a staleness bound and recourse — none of which exists here. The unnamed object is **a per-claim self-assertion of an off-chain obligation**, and the corpus's instinct that `At` "clearly is not the right one" is confirmed by the struct definition.
2. **Off-chain legal recourse.** Nothing in the contracts references a jurisdiction, a governing agreement, a document hash or a servicer of record. The solvency of an Arf pool rests on contracts in a legal system that the code cannot name. The corpus's residue item stands, unqualified.
3. **A tranche thickness constraint.** `maxSeniorJuniorRatio = 4` is the parameter that makes the waterfall safe, and it is enforced twice — on deposit and on redemption ordering. `Tr` names the waterfall, not the leverage limit on it.
4. **Underwriting as an ongoing process.** `Aw` is a static gate. `approveBorrower` sets a limit, a rate, a period count and a commitment, and can be revisited by `updateLimitAndCommitment`, `updateYield`, `extendRemainingPeriod` and `waiveLateFee` — a continuing relationship, not an admission test.
5. **An administered retail rate with an optional zero-rate mode.** Huma 2.0 pays "8% APY, subject to monthly adjustments", and lets the depositor *elect* 0% APY in exchange for more points. This is the same administered-rate residue the corpus flagged for Spark, appearing independently at a second protocol — but with a twist no symbol covers: **a claim class whose holder chooses between a cash yield and a non-cash reward stream**, switchable instantly.
6. **A global daily redemption cap with an off-chain service-level agreement.** "First-come, first-served", rejected above the cap, "1 business day … up to 7 days". `Wq` names a queue; this is a *rationing rule plus a promise*, and the promise is not in any contract.
7. **Lockup as a reward multiplier rather than a claim modifier.** 3- and 6-month lockups do not change the yield; they multiply Feathers, and breaking one **retroactively reverts** the bonus — including on transfer. Nothing in the vocabulary names a commitment whose only consequence is a discretionary points multiplier.
8. **Points as the primary consideration.** In Maxi mode the depositor's entire return is Feathers. `Em` requires protocol-funded emissions of a *token*; Feathers are an off-chain ledger of a future claim.
9. **Automation cadence as protocol structure.** Five Autotasks every five minutes under a Sentinel wallet are what make the epoch close, the bills refresh and the payments pull. `Ep` names the epoch boundary; nothing names the fact that the boundary only exists if someone calls it.

**On the two claims this lane is asked to test:** Huma supplies the fifth and, in one respect, the strongest instance of the bounded discretionary mandate — and it is the one instance where the corpus's candidate symbol **works**. The Evaluation Agent is a single address that sets credit limits and interest rates and unilaterally declares default; the code bounds it with `maxSeniorJuniorRatio = 4`, a first-loss `minLiquidity` requirement, and — uniquely in this lane — **mandatory skin in the game**, since `PoolConfig` requires the EA to hold junior-tranche capital. That is `Sv` (servicing & determination discretion) in its intended sense, and the corpus is right that "if any candidate deserves promotion on this evidence, it is `Sv`." But note precisely what `Sv` covers and does not: it names the EA's authority to *determine an outcome on a claim*, and it does **not** name the EA's authority to *set the price* (`yieldInBps`) or *size the exposure* (`creditLimit`).
