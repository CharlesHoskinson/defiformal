## Lane narrative

**What the section introduction should say.** Given a yield-bearing position that already exists somewhere else, how do you repackage it into a claim someone wants to hold — and who decides where the capital goes? The corpus called the delegated allocation mandate the most-confirmed gap in the project. This lane sharpens it from a missing element into a primitive with a fixed shape.

**The finding: the mandate has five parts, and every one is residue.**

> named agent → domain whitelist → magnitude cap → rate-of-change limit or delay → revocation

It appears at four of the five targets, always bounding a *different* quantity — leverage, savings rate, venue, credit limit. The code proves the level claim: the reallocation and leverage entry points accept an arbitrary target from a human, and the contract checks only the envelope. Nothing computes the target, schedules a rebalance, defines a missed target, or compels the agent to act at all. **Above all the discretion itself is unnameable.**

**Corrections the lane made to the corpus, with evidence.** One protocol drops both its proxy and its timelock (neither exists) and keeps its emissions symbol only with a note that it pays a thousandth of the rate — which surfaced a new residue: *the vocabulary can name mutability but cannot assert immutability*. One protocol's element set decomposes the institutional product, not the product carrying the capital. One drops emissions entirely, having no token.

**Scale.** The largest object in the category by capital is the one the requirement tables reject, and its distinguishing feature has no symbol at all.
